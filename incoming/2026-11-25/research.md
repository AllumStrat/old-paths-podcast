## 1. Setting and background

### Daniel 10-12 (Old Testament, deepest)
- 10:1 "third year of Cyrus king of Persia" = 536/535 B.C., counting from Cyrus's capture of Babylon in 539. Cyrus's decree (Ezra 1:1) came in his first year; about 50,000 returned (Ezra 2:64-65). Altar built and temple foundation laid in the second year after the return (Ezra 3). Then "the people of the land discouraged the people of Judah... and bribed counselors against them... all the days of Cyrus" (Ezra 4:4-5). Calvin (10:2) says Daniel's grief was the halted work: "Cyrus was gone to a distance... His son Cambyses was corrupted by his couriers, and forbade the Jews to proceed with the rebuilding." Henry gives both options: the Jews who stayed in Babylon, or the obstruction of Ezra 4:4-5.
- Daniel was taken in 605 B.C. as a youth, so he is past eighty. He did not return.
- 10:4 "twenty-fourth day of the first month" (Nisan). His three weeks of mourning (10:2-3) ran from the 3rd/4th to the 24th, covering Passover (14th) and Unleavened Bread (15th-21st). (Hippolytus reads "fourth day", Jerome notes 21 days.)
- "the great river (that is, the Tigris)", Hebrew Hiddekel, one of Eden's rivers (Gen 2:14; Henry notes this).
- Structure: Henry: "This chapter and the two next (which conclude this book) make up one entire vision and prophecy... not by signs and figures, as before (ch. 7 and 8), but by express words." Chapter 10 prologue, 11:2-12:4 the revelation, 12:5-13 epilogue.
- Chapter 11 history (Henry, Calvin, Jerome): three Persian kings and a fourth richest (Xerxes, invaded Greece 480 B.C.); a mighty king (Alexander, d. 323 B.C.), kingdom divided to the four winds; king of the south = Ptolemies of Egypt, king of the north = Seleucids of Syria; 11:6 daughter of the king of the south = Berenice, daughter of Ptolemy II, married to Antiochus II, later murdered (Laodice); 11:10-19 Antiochus III the Great (Raphia 217 B.C.; Panium 200 B.C. gave him Judea, "the glorious land"; defeated by Rome at Magnesia, "a commander shall put an end to his insolence"); 11:20 Seleucus IV sends an exactor (Heliodorus); 11:21-35 Antiochus IV Epiphanes (175-164 B.C.): took the throne by flattery while his nephew Demetrius was a hostage in Rome; "Epiphanes", the illustrious / God manifest; Henry: "Hence he was called Epimanes—the madman"; 11:30 ships of Kittim = the Roman fleet that turned him back from Egypt in 168 B.C. (Popillius's line in the sand); 11:31 temple profaned, burnt offering stopped, "abomination that makes desolate" (altar/idol of Zeus Olympios, Kislev 167 B.C.; Jerome: "an image of Jupiter Olympius in the Temple"); 11:32-35 the Maccabees and the faithful (Henry: Eleazar and the mother with seven sons, 2 Maccabees 6-7; Hebrews 11:35); temple rededicated December 164 B.C. (Hanukkah).
- Porphyry (3rd century, Against the Christians book 12) argued Daniel was written in the time of Antiochus because chapter 11 is so precise. Jerome's preface answers him.
- The man in linen: linen (baddim) is priestly dress (Lev 16:4, Day of Atonement; Ezek 9:2). Uphaz gold (Jer 10:9).
- Hebrew notes: sar = prince/commander/captain (10:13, 20, 21; 12:1 "the great prince"). Michael = "Who is like God?". 12:1 "time of trouble". 12:2 "everlasting life" (chayyei olam) occurs only here in the OT (Cambridge Bible); dera'on "contempt/abhorrence" only here and Isaiah 66:24 (Ellicott; Barnes: "aversion, abhorrence"). "dusty ground" (admat-'aphar) only here (Cambridge); "dust" of the grave as in Job 20:11; 21:26. 12:3 maskilim "the wise" = "the wise among the people" in 11:33, 35; "those who turn many to righteousness" (matsdiqe harabbim) echoes Isaiah 53:11 "make many to be accounted righteous" (Cambridge, Keil). 12:7 time, times, half a time (cf. 7:25). 12:11-12 1,290 and 1,335 days.

### Job 22 (Psalms and Wisdom)
- Opens the third cycle of speeches (22-27): Eliphaz's third and last speech; Bildad short (25); Zophar never speaks (Henry: "Zophar drew back, and quitted the field").
- Structure (Henry): 2-4 God gains nothing from man; 5-11 charges of oppression; 12-20 charge that Job thinks God cannot see, and the "old way" of the wicked (flood and fire); 21-30 call to return with promises.
- 22:5-9 first specific charges: pledges for nothing, stripping the naked, no water/bread, widows sent away, orphans crushed. False: Job 29:12-17 and the oath of chapter 31 answer them; God's verdict in 1:8 and 42:7.
- 22:21 "Agree with God" (ESV) / "Acquaint now thyself with him" (KJV) / "Yield now" (NASB): Hebrew hasken (sakan), to be familiar with, accustomed to, of use to (same root as 22:2 "Can a man be profitable to God?"). Wordplay: can a man be "of use" to God? Then: get familiar with him.
- 22:24-25 "lay gold in the dust... then the Almighty will be your gold": Spurgeon's sermon 1885 on 22:26 "Delight in the Almighty".
- Satan's question 1:9 "Does Job fear God for no reason?" Eliphaz's offer (return and be rebuilt, gold, answered prayer) unknowingly argues the satan's theory: piety pays. Maclaren: "They mean little more than 'It pays to be religious.'"
- 22:30 "He delivers even the one who is not innocent, who will be delivered through the cleanness of your hands." Ellicott and JFB: unconsciously prophetic; fulfilled in 42:7-9 when Eliphaz himself is spared through Job's prayer.

### John 13:31-38 (Gospel)
- Night of the last supper, Thursday of Passion week, A.D. 30 or 33. Immediately after Judas goes out into the night (13:30). Upper Room discourse begins (13:31-17:26).
- "Now is the Son of Man glorified": glory/glorify five times in two verses (Keller sermon page). The cross as glorification (12:23, 28).
- 13:33 "Little children", teknia: tender diminutive, common in 1 John, here alone in the Gospel. "As I said to the Jews" (7:34; 8:21).
- New commandment: Lev 19:18 is old; "as I have loved you" is new. Latin mandatum novum gives "Maundy Thursday".
- Peter's vow and the prediction; fulfilled 18:15-27; restored 21:15-19 ("Follow me", 21:19; "you will follow afterward" 13:36 fulfilled in his death, 21:18-19).

### Jude 17-25 (New Testament)
- Conclusion of Jude (see ep. 36 intro: Jude brother of James, 60s A.D.). After the examples and the "these are" sections, Jude turns: "But you, beloved" (17, 20).
- 17-18: apostles' prediction; 2 Peter 3:3 nearly verbatim ("scoffers... following their own sinful desires").
- 19: "worldly people", psychikoi, natural/soulish (1 Cor 2:14), "devoid of the Spirit".
- 20-21: one imperative ("keep yourselves in the love of God") with three participles (building, praying, waiting) (Piper). "Kept" brackets the letter: verse 1 "kept for Jesus Christ", verse 21 "keep yourselves", verse 24 "able to keep you".
- 22-23: three groups (ESV text): doubters (mercy), those in the fire (snatch), others (mercy with fear). Zechariah 3:1-4: Joshua the high priest "a brand plucked from the fire", in filthy garments; 3:2 "The Lord rebuke you, O Satan", the very words Michael uses in Jude 9. Garment = chiton, undergarment next to the skin.
- 24-25: doxology. "keep you from stumbling" (aptaistous, without stumbling; Revised Version "guard you from stumbling"; Spurgeon No. 2296). "blameless" (amomous, sacrificial word, without blemish).

## 3. Interpretive questions

Daniel 10:13, 20: Who is "the prince of the kingdom of Persia"?
- Jerome: "In my opinion this was the angel to whose charge Persia was committed," citing Deut 32:8; resisted "enumerating the sins of the Jewish people as a ground for their justly being kept in captivity."
- Calvin: the human king Cambyses ("The prince, says he, of the kingdom of the Persians, meaning Cambyses"); the angel fought against him for Israel. Calvin open to Michael being Christ but leaves it in doubt.
- Henry: Cambyses opposed the Jews and the angel "counter-worked" him.
- Ligonier Tabletalk "Michael, the Chief Prince" (2010): "a demon with strong influence on the Persian empire"; caution about "binding" territorial spirits.
- Piper (Ligonier article 2007): "the clearest example in all the Bible of what is called by some people a 'territorial spirit'"; the 21 days match Daniel's 21 days of prayer; but Daniel's praying "was not about angels".
- Host: angelic opposition (the same word sar names Michael, a heavenly being, so the opposing prince is likely heavenly too), behind a human king; prayer matters; leave the angels to God.

Daniel 10:5-6: Who is the man in linen? Henry and Hippolytus: Christ (cf. Rev 1:13-15). Calvin: an angel ("We have already stated him not to be the Christ"), though he allows Michael may be Christ.

Daniel 11:36-45: Who is the king who exalts himself? Porphyry: Antiochus throughout. Jerome: Antichrist ("We too understand this to refer to the Antichrist"). Calvin: the Roman empire ("the angel did not prophesy of Antiochus, or any single monarch, but of a new empire, meaning, the Roman"). Henry: Antiochus, with expressions referring further to antichrist. Tabletalk (2013): "it appears that the prophet moves from Antiochus as a figure in history to Antiochus as a type of the final enemy to come." Host: Tabletalk/Henry.

Daniel 12:2: Bodily resurrection or national revival? Porphyry (via Jerome) and Henry's first two senses: metaphor of revival; Calvin, Henry (third and main sense), Jerome: final bodily resurrection of righteous and wicked. "Many" = all (Calvin).

Job 22:5-9: Where did Eliphaz get these charges? Henry: "laying to his charge that which he knew not"; inferred from the suffering. Spurgeon: "They inferred virtue from prosperity, and sin from adversity."

John 13:34: What is new about it? Augustine: love that renews, "as I have loved you". Chrysostom: new by the manner, Christ began first. Calvin: recommended "on the ground of novelty", as a law lately made. Sproul (via Tabletalk): set against Judas's treason. Henry: "an old book in a new edition". Spurgeon No. 2936: new in extent, new reason, new nature.

Jude 21 vs 24: Who keeps whom? Piper: "Keep yourselves in the love of God, for God is the one who keeps you in his love." Calvin: love as the guardian of life, with the last day in view.
