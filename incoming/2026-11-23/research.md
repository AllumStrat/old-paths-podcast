## 1. SETTING AND BACKGROUND

### Daniel 7-8 (deepest)
- Date: 7:1 "first year of Belshazzar" = c. 553/550 B.C., when Nabonidus left Babylon for Tayma and entrusted the kingship to his son (Nabonidus Chronicle, used ep 35). So ch 7 comes chronologically *before* ch 5 (Henry: "The date of this chapter places it before ch. 5, which was in the last year of Belshazzar"). Ch 8: third year of Belshazzar, c. 551/550 B.C.
- Structure: chapters 1-6 court stories, 7-12 visions (Henry: "The six former chapters of this book were historical; we now enter with fear and trembling upon the six latter, which are prophetical"). Language: Aramaic from 2:4b to end of ch 7; Hebrew returns at 8:1. The Aramaic section begins and ends with a four-kingdom vision (statue, ch 2; beasts, ch 7). Ch 7 thus belongs with both halves.
- Ch 7 parallels ch 2: four kingdoms then an everlasting kingdom from God. Ch 2 = how the kingdoms look to a king (precious metals); ch 7 = how they look from heaven (beasts).
- Identifications (traditional, Hippolytus, Jerome, Calvin, Henry): lion = Babylon; bear raised on one side = Medo-Persia (Persia dominant); leopard with four wings and four heads = Greece, Alexander, divided among four successors (Hippolytus names Seleucus, Demetrius, Ptolemy, Philip; Henry names Seleucus, Antigonus, Cassander, Ptolemy); fourth beast = Rome (Hippolytus, Jerome, Calvin; Henry gives Rome or Syria as options).
- Hippolytus on 7:4 (lion made to stand like a man): Nebuchadnezzar humbled and restored, ch 4.
- Ch 8 locations: Susa (Shushan), citadel in Elam, later a Persian royal capital (Esther, Nehemiah); Ulai canal. Barnes suggests Daniel may have had business in Susa; uncertain whether there in body or only in vision.
- Ram, two horns, the higher came up last = Media and Persia, Persia rising later under Cyrus (8:20). Goat from the west not touching the ground = Alexander's speed (334-323 B.C.); great horn broken at height = Alexander's death 323 B.C.; four horns = his four successor kingdoms (8:22).
- Little horn from one of the four (8:9, 23-25) = Antiochus IV Epiphanes (175-164 B.C.), Seleucid. JFB (8:9 page): "Antiochus, afterward called Epiphanes, or Illustrious, by his flatterers; but by Polybius termed more properly Epimanes, or the madman." Jerome: image of Jupiter in the temple; Calvin: "the image of Olympian Jove." Desecration Kislev 167 B.C.; cleansing by Judas Maccabaeus Kislev 164 B.C. (Jerome, Calvin: month Kislev).
- 2,300 evenings and mornings (8:14): Calvin: "Christ numbers 2300 days for the pollution of the sanctuary, and this period comprehends six years and about four months." Keil argues Hebrew readers could not take it as 1,150 days; others (Delitzsch, many) take it as 1,150 days counting the twice-daily sacrifices (biblehub 8-14 page). Jerome: about six years and three months from Antiochus's entry to the cleansing.
- Critical view: Porphyry (3rd c.) argued Daniel was written in the time of Antiochus Epiphanes (Jerome's prologue). Mention in one sentence.

### Job 21:1-21
- Job's answer to Zophar's second speech (ch 20; "the exulting of the wicked is short", 20:5). Second cycle of speeches ends here; Job directly contradicts the friends' thesis with observation.
- 21:17 echoes Bildad 18:5-6 (lamp of the wicked put out) to question it (Barnes, JFB). Keil: the antithesis of Zophar's thesis, the experience that nearly ruined Asaph (Psalm 73).
- Structure: 2-6 plea to listen, appalled; 7-13 wicked prosper (long life, children, safe houses, cattle, music, peaceful death); 14-15 their creed; 16 Job disowns their counsel; 17-18 how often?; 19-21 the reply "God stores up iniquity for their children" rejected: let the man himself feel it.

### John 13:12-17
- Upper room, the evening before the crucifixion; continues the foot washing (13:1-11, ep 35). 13:12 he puts on garments and reclines again; now the teaching. Ends the first unit of the Farewell section before Judas is exposed (13:18-30 next).
- Luke 22:24 the dispute about greatness the same night (used ep 35; don't repeat).

### Jude 1-7 (new book)
- Author: Jude (Judas), "brother of James" = James of Jerusalem, so a half-brother of Jesus (Mark 6:3 names James, Joses, Judas, Simon). Clement of Alexandria: Jude knew the near relationship but did not call himself the Lord's brother. Henry: James whom "the ancients style the first bishop of Jerusalem."
- Date: conservative c. A.D. 60s (close relationship with 2 Peter 2). Readers: probably Jewish Christians, familiar with Jewish tradition (Enoch, Moses' body).
- Structure: greeting 1-2; purpose 3-4; three examples 5-7; portraits of the intruders 8-16; apostolic warning 17-19; charge 20-23; doxology 24-25.
- "Kept" motif: v1 "kept for Jesus Christ"; v6 angels "did not stay/keep" their position and God "has kept" them in chains (JFB: same Greek, translate "He hath kept"); later v21 "keep yourselves", v24 "able to keep you".
- v5 textual: "Jesus" (ESV) vs "the Lord" (KJV). Ellicott: "a most remarkable reading, showing how, in Christian language, the Man Jesus had become identified with the Eternal Son." JFB: oldest manuscripts read "Jesus." Compare 1 Corinthians 10:4.
- v6 angels: either the original fall (Calvin, Gill, Poole) or the Genesis 6 "sons of God" tradition from 1 Enoch (Lange/Hofmann, Alford). JFB prefers Sethite reading of Gen 6. Not a main point today.

## 3. INTERPRETIVE QUESTIONS

Daniel
1. Who is the one like a son of man, and is 7:13 the second coming or the ascension? Jewish view: Israel (Ligonier devotional answers: singular, divine, rides the clouds). Calvin: Christ's ascension, "He came to the Ancient of days... ought to be explained of Christ's ascension." Henry/Hippolytus: last judgment. Jesus at his trial (Mark 14:62) cites it. Host: the direction is upward, to the throne; ascension and enthronement fulfil it; the visible return completes it.
2. Are the little horns of ch 7 and ch 8 the same person? Porphyry: both Antiochus, written after the fact. Jerome: ch 7 Antichrist, ch 8 Antiochus as type. Calvin: ch 7 the Caesars; ch 8 Antiochus. Hippolytus: ch 7 Antichrist. Host: ch 8's horn comes from the Greek goat and is named; ch 7's comes from the fourth beast, after Greece; different, but the same pattern.
3. 2,300 evenings and mornings: 2,300 days vs 1,150.

Job: Is Job denying God's justice? No: he denies the friends' timetable. Henry: God's patience, and the world of retribution is the next. Gregory: prosperity no witness to innocence.

John: Is foot washing an ordinance? Sproul: not a sacrament; the cleansing it pictures belongs to baptism. Calvin: not an annual ceremony but a lifelong readiness. Augustine: better also done with hands; the bent body awakens humility. Ligonier devotional: no formal institution like baptism and the Supper.

Jude: Why did Jude change his letter (v3)? What does "once for all delivered" mean? Who are the angels of v6?
