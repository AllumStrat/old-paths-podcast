## 1. SETTING AND BACKGROUND

### Daniel 3-4 (deepest)
- Where the story stands: 2:46-49, the king falls before Daniel, confesses "your God is God of gods," and promotes Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego over the province of Babylon; Daniel remains at court (which explains why Daniel is absent in ch. 3: no explanation given; commentators speculate).
- Date: the Hebrew/Aramaic gives none. The Greek versions (LXX and Theodotion per Pulpit on 3:1; Hippolytus quotes "In the eighteenth year, Nebuchadnezzar the king made a golden image") date it to Nebuchadnezzar's eighteenth year, i.e. 587/586 B.C., the year Jerusalem fell. Present as "an early Greek tradition."
- The image: sixty cubits by six (ESV note: cubit about 18 inches), about ninety feet tall and nine wide, probably gold-plated; image of the king or of a god uncertain, likely a god (Ligonier, "Courage Under Fire", https://learn.ligonier.org/devotionals/courage-under-fire). Barnes: Dura, an open plain, site now impossible to determine; Herodotus mentions a forty-foot gold image in the temple of Bel (Barnes/JFB on 3:1, https://biblehub.com/commentaries/daniel/3-1.htm). Sexagesimal 60 and 6 fits Babylonian counting.
- Answer to ch. 2: the dream statue had a head of gold; this one is gold from head to foot.
- Literary features: the long lists of officials (3:2, 3, 27) and of the six instruments (3:5, 7, 10, 15) repeated four times: the machinery of compulsion. The names Shadrach, Meshach, Abednego recur thirteen times (Spurgeon No. 2217 notes the repetition).
- Accusers: "certain Chaldeans" (3:8), possibly jealous of the foreigners promoted (Ligonier). Spurgeon notes these Chaldeans had just been saved from death by Daniel (2:12-18).
- 3:15 "who is the god who will deliver you out of my hands?" The question of the chapter.
- 3:17 ESV footnote: "Or If our God whom we serve is able to deliver us, he will deliver us..." The Aramaic hen ("if") is hard; the point remains: confidence in God's power, no presumption on his will.
- 3:21 garments: meaning uncertain (ESV note). 3:27 not even the smell of fire.
- 3:25 bar 'elahin: "a son of the gods"; Aramaic 'elahin is always a true plural in Daniel (Cambridge on 3:25, https://biblehub.com/commentaries/daniel/3-25.htm). LXX: "the likeness of an angel of God."
- Chapter 4 (Aramaic 3:31-4:34): written as the king's royal proclamation (4:1-3 and 4:37 frame it). Point of view: first person 4:4-18, Daniel speaks 19-27, third person 28-33 (while he is mad), first person resumes 34 at "my reason returned to me."
- The watcher, 'ir (Jerome: Theodotion keeps the Chaldee word; "it signifies the angels, because they ever keep watch"; Theodoret: bodiless, not subject to sleep), sermonindex ECF Daniel 4.
- "Seven periods of time" (4:16): seven years (Jerome) or three and a half (Theodoret, two seasons a year).
- 4:27 Aramaic peraq: "break off" (Calvin) or "redeem" (Vulgate tradition; Jerome "make up for"; Chrysostom and Augustine use the verse for almsgiving). Calvin: Daniel exhorts to repentance; righteousness and mercy are the outward duties of the second table; repentance begins in the heart.
- Twelve months of patience (4:29): Theodoret calls it "the span of divine longsuffering."
- 4:30 archaeology: "thousands of bricks on the plain, the inscriptions of which have been deciphered, each marked 'Nebuchadnezzar, the son of Nabopolassar'" (JFB on 4:30, https://biblehub.com/commentaries/daniel/4-30.htm). The East India House Inscription (British Museum) records his building works; I did not fetch a scholarly translation, so mention only generally that his own inscriptions boast of building Babylon. Herodotus does not mention Nebuchadnezzar (JFB).
- Pulpit on 4:30: in the Babylonian empire "Nebuchadnezzar was the empire, therefore the splendour of the city was a testimony to his glory."

### Job 19
- Job's reply to Bildad's second speech (ch. 18). Structure: 2-6 to the friends ("ten times," "my error remains with myself"); 7-12 God as besieger (walls, darkness, crown stripped, tree uprooted, siege ramp); 13-20 total abandonment (brothers, guests, servant, wife, children, intimates); 21-22 "Have mercy on me... why do you, like God, pursue me?" (eating flesh = slander, Keil); 23-24 inscription wish; 25-27 the Redeemer; 28-29 warning to the friends.
- Engraving: Benson: letters cut in rock and filled with lead (bh 19-23/24). Barnes: the wish was granted more permanently.
- go'el: nearest kinsman who redeems property, avenges blood, maintains the name (Ellicott, Cambridge, Poole). Ellicott traces the line: daysman 9:33, witness 16:19, surety 17:3, now Goel.
- 19:25 "at the last he will stand upon the earth," ESV note: Hebrew "dust." 19:26 ESV note: "Or without" my flesh.

### John 12:37-50
- End of Jesus' public ministry (Book of Signs). 12:36b "he departed and hid himself from them." 12:37-43 the evangelist's verdict; 12:44-50 Jesus "cried out", a summary of his public teaching.
- Isaiah 53:1 (LXX) and Isaiah 6:10 quoted; 12:41 Isaiah saw "his glory," the vision of Isaiah 6 (Ligonier "The Glory Isaiah Saw"; Sproul "One Sent Me").
- 12:42 rulers who believed but would not confess, fear of being put out of the synagogue (cf. 9:22). 12:43 doxa: glory/praise of men.

### 2 John
- The elder (John the apostle in tradition). The elect lady: a woman named Electa or Kyria, or a church (13, "your elect sister" = sister church). Clement of Alexandria (Adumbrations) says a Babylonian lady named Electa, and also that the name indicates the election of the church.
- Context: itinerant teachers lodged in Christian homes; deceivers "who do not confess the coming of Jesus Christ in the flesh" (docetism, Cerinthus). "Goes on ahead" (9) = progressives who outrun the teaching of Christ.
- 13 verses, would fit a single papyrus sheet; "paper and ink" (12).

## 3. INTERPRETIVE QUESTIONS
- Daniel 3: Who is the fourth? (angel: Calvin, Cambridge, Barnes, Keil, Geneva; Christ: Hippolytus, Irenaeus, "the majority" per Jerome, Gill, Poole, JFB "messenger of the covenant", Spurgeon No. 2217; Jerome: angel historically, type of Christ). Why does the king say "son of the gods"? Pagan theology (Keil, Ellicott). Was "If this be so" doubt? No.
- Daniel 4: Does 4:27 teach that alms atone (Jerome, Chrysostom, Augustine use it for almsgiving) or repentance shown in justice (Calvin)? Was Nebuchadnezzar converted? (Henry yes; Calvin reserved; Jerome: understood and humbled himself but spoke "like a worldling" in 4:35.)
- Job 19: Does 19:25-27 teach bodily resurrection and point to Christ? (Clement of Rome, Poole, Gill, Henry, Ligonier: yes; Cambridge: God as Goel, sight of God "without my flesh"; Ellicott in between.)
- John 12: How can "they could not believe" be just? (Augustine: could not because would not; Chrysostom: same; Sproul: judicial hardening, God hands sinners over.) Whom did Isaiah see? (the Son, Sproul, Ligonier.)
- 2 John: Who is the lady? What does "do not receive him" forbid? (Barnes: sponsorship as teacher, not common kindness; Ligonier: official sanction of false teachers, not contact with unbelievers; Gill: neither the house of God nor your own house.)
