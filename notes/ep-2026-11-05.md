## 1. SETTING AND BACKGROUND

### Ezekiel 22-23 (deepest)
- Date: undated. The section opened at 20:1 (seventh year, fifth month, tenth day: August 591 B.C.). The next date is 24:1 (ninth year, tenth month, tenth day: January 588 B.C., siege begins). So these oracles fall roughly 591-589 B.C., while Zedekiah still reigns in Jerusalem and courts Egypt (17:15), and Ezekiel is in Tel-abib.
- Place in the book: chapters 20-23 are a second cycle of indictment after the 592 temple vision. Keil: chapter 22 is "another description of the sins of Jerusalem and Israel, by which this judgment is occasioned" (i.e., the sword of chapter 21). Chapter 23 is co-ordinate with 21 and 22 but in allegory, closely resembling chapter 16, yet "the guilt and punishment of Israel stand in the foreground of the picture throughout, so that a parallel is drawn between Jerusalem and Samaria" (Keil, Ezekiel 23 intro). Chapter 16 depicted past, present and future; 23 is about the alliances. URL https://biblehub.com/commentaries/kad/ezekiel/23.htm
- "Bloody city" (22:2): Hebrew ir haddamim, "city of bloods" (Bible Hub interlinear, https://biblehub.com/interlinear/ezekiel/22-2.htm). Keil cross-references Nahum 3:1, where the same title is Nineveh's ("Woe to the bloody city", ESV). Also 24:6, 9.
- Keil: the refrain "to shed blood" (22:6, 9, 12) divides the list into three groups. Princes = kings like Jehoiakim (2 Kings 24:4) and Manasseh (2 Kings 21:16). 22:13 striking the hand = gesture of wrathful indignation (21:14, 17).
- Dross: sig / sigim (Bible Hub interlinear 22:18). Keil: the siege as "a melting process." The image: Isaiah 1:22, 25; Jeremiah 6:28-30; Malachi 3:3 (Pulpit, Ellicott). Pulpit: "there is hope, as well as terror, in the parable" but Henry, Keil: this furnace is for dross.
- Ellicott on refining: completion of refining determined by "the reflection of the image of the refiner from its molten surface." https://biblehub.com/commentaries/ellicott/ezekiel/22.htm
- 22:24 "not cleansed or rained upon in the day of indignation": drought (Jeremiah 14:4) or no washing rain (Henry gives options).
- 22:25-29: all four classes. Keil: prophets, priests, princes, people; Zephaniah 3:3-4 is very close (Ezekiel echoes Zephaniah). 22:28 repeats 13:10 (whitewash) almost verbatim.
- 22:30: gader (wall, fence) and perets (breach) (Bible Hub interlinear 22:30). Same image as 13:5 (false prophets did not go up into the breaches). Psalm 106:23 (Moses). Keil: Abraham for Sodom floating before Ezekiel's mind; closing words allude to Genesis 18:28. Jeremiah 5:1 (search for a man). Isaiah 59:16 ("He saw that there was no man, and wondered that there was no one to intercede; then his own arm brought him salvation").
- Ezekiel 23: Oholah = "her tent," Oholibah = "my tent is in her" (Keil, Pulpit, Poole, Gill, Ellicott all agree). Pulpit: proper names almost unique in biblical parables; only other instance Lazarus (Luke 16:20); cf. Oholibamah (Genesis 36:2). Oholah "the greater" (ten tribes), not "the elder" (Keil, Ellicott).
- History behind 23: Samaria's alliances with Assyria (2 Kings 15:19; 17:3-6), fell 722 B.C. Judah: Ahaz and Tiglath-pileser (2 Kings 16), Hezekiah showing Babylon's envoys his treasures (Isaiah 39; Henry), Manasseh (2 Kings 21; Ellicott), Zedekiah's embassy to Babylon (Jeremiah 29:3; Keil) then Egypt (17:15; 23:19-21).
- Archaeology: 23:14 men portrayed on the wall in vermilion (shashar, red ochre, Keil). Keil: "answers perfectly to the sculptures upon the inner walls of the Assyrian palaces in the monuments of Nimrud, Khorsabad, and Kouyunjik (see Layard...)". Lachish reliefs: Sennacherib's South-West Palace at Nineveh, carved 700-681 B.C., discovered by Layard 1845-47, now in the British Museum; they depict the 701 B.C. siege of Lachish in Judah (Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lachish_reliefs, background only). Ellicott: colours used on Assyrian and Babylonian portraiture.
- 23:25 nose and ears: Ellicott: mutilating prisoners; in Egypt adultery punished by cutting off nose and ears.
- 23:31-34 the cup: Jeremiah 25:15; Isaiah 51:17; Psalm 75:8; Pulpit cross-references Matthew 20:22; 26:39.
- 23:38-39: child sacrifice and temple attendance "on the same day."
- 23:40-44: Keil (after Kliefoth): the alliance-making with Chaldea, envoys received like paramours, God's incense and oil on the harlot's table.
- Glory arc: glory left at 11:23 (still absent). Muteness continues (3:26 to 33:22).

### Job 8
- Bildad the Shuhite: second of the friends (2:11). Henry reports Bishop Patrick's suggestion: descended from Shuah, son of Abraham by Keturah (Genesis 25:2). Pulpit: younger than Eliphaz, blunter; novelties are appeal to past ages (8-10) and new metaphors.
- Structure (Keil, Henry): 2-7 (God's justice, your children, if you seek God); 8-10 (ask the former generations); 11-19 (three pictures: papyrus and reed, spider's house, lush plant on stones); 20-22 (God will not reject the blameless; laughter).
- Words: gome' (papyrus), found elsewhere only with Egypt: Exodus 2:3 (Moses' basket), Isaiah 18:2, 35:7 (Keil, Pulpit). Spider's web: beth akkabish, "spider's house" (Keil). Job 8:4 literally "delivered them into the hand of their transgression" (Pulpit).
- Job 8:7 "though your beginning was small..." came true in 42:12 (Henry; Spurgeon No. 311).
- Gregory, Moralia Book 8: the rush that cuts the hand that plucks it.

### John 9:1-12
- Setting: follows 8:59 (Jesus hid himself and went out of the temple). Chrysostom: he went out of the temple and healed the blind man, "mitigating their rage by His absence." Still around the Feast of Booths period. 9:5 repeats 8:12 "I am the light of the world."
- Siloam: Hezekiah built the pool fed by the Siloam tunnel (2 Kings 20:20; Wikipedia Pool of Siloam, background). Second Temple pool found in 2004 during sewer work (Shukron and Reich). Isaiah 8:6 "waters of Shiloah that flow gently." Episode 13 (John 7:37) covered the water-drawing from Siloam at Booths.
- 9:2 assumption: Calvin mentions a belief in transmigration; Chrysostom says the disciples spoke in perplexity. Jesus' answer: not cause but purpose (Chrysostom discusses whether "that" is purpose or result).
- Clay: Chrysostom ties to Genesis 2:7 (dust); Augustine: Word made flesh; Ryle: Christ not tied to one means.
- Neighbors' debate 9:8-9; "I am the man" (Greek ego eimi on the man's lips, contrast 8:58; ESV "I am the man"). "Where is he?" "I do not know" sets up the interrogation in 9:13-25.

### 1 John 1:1-4 (book introduction)
- Author: letter anonymous, universally received as the apostle John's (Ellicott: Eusebius lists it among the homologoumena; 35 passages shared with the Fourth Gospel). Polycarp, John's disciple, echoes 1 John 4:2-3 in his Letter to the Philippians (Ellicott).
- Place and date: Ephesus, late first century. Irenaeus, Against Heresies 3.3.4: the church in Ephesus had "John remaining among them permanently until the times of Trajan" (Trajan reigned from 98).
- Opponents: Ellicott: "The particular heretics combated had a Docetic tendency, not yet fully developed." Cerinthus (Irenaeus 1.26.1): Christ descended on Jesus at baptism and departed before the suffering. Bath-house story (Irenaeus 3.3.4).
- Structure: one long sentence in Greek; main verb "we proclaim" (1:3); four "that which" clauses; purpose clauses: fellowship (koinonia) and joy (1:4). Echoes John 1:1-4 (beginning, Word, life, light).
- "Touched": Augustine: "Who is he that with hands does handle the Word..." Luke 24:39; John 20:27.
- Tests of life to come: 1:5-10 next (God is light, confession).

## 3. INTERPRETIVE QUESTIONS

Ezekiel 22:30: How can God find "none" when Jeremiah was in Jerusalem?
- Gill: good people like Jeremiah and Baruch existed, "but not among these" (the classes listed).
- Ellicott: not that there was not a single godly man, but none of commanding character whose intercession could avert the doom.
- JFB: Jeremiah had been forbidden to pray (Jeremiah 11:14), so none of the godly was willing to interpose; none among the guilty classes was morally able.
- Carson: Jeremiah and Ezekiel were to declare God's word, not to avert wrath; ultimately only one Mediator would suffice.
- Host: combine Jeremiah 15:1 (even Moses and Samuel) and Ezekiel 14:14 (even Noah, Daniel, Job). The office of intercessor for the nation was closed; the breach needed someone none of them could be. Isaiah 59:16.

Ezekiel 22:17-22: Is the furnace punitive or purifying?
- Pulpit: "there is hope, as well as terror." Henry: God leaves these in the furnace like dross; the refiner sits by his own. Gregory: God meant purification but they became baser metals. Keil: melting to separate silver ore from baser metals. Host: for the nation, judgment; for a remnant, refining (22:15 "consume your uncleanness out of you").

Ezekiel 23: Why tell the chapter 16 story again, and why so graphically?
- Keil: 23 centers on guilt and punishment and the parallel with Samaria (the alliances), where 16 covered past, present and future.
- Carson: meant to be horrible; metaphors do not walk on all fours; Ezekiel no misogynist; God as husband; apostasy as betrayal.
- Henry: sinful love turns to hatred (23:22, 28).

Job 8: Is Bildad right?
- Henry: right in general (God does not pervert justice), wrong in application; "an accuser of the brethren."
- Gregory: wondrous truth, wrong target.
- Spurgeon No. 311: friends not inspired in what they say; but 8:7 came true. Spurgeon No. 651: preach the text on hypocrisy.

John 9:2-3: Did Jesus deny that the man or his parents had sinned? Is the man used?
- Augustine and Chrysostom and Calvin: not sinless, but sin was not the cause of the blindness. Sproul: false dilemma; there might always be another reason. Chrysostom: "What wrong" did he suffer; he gained inner sight. Purpose vs result of "that".

1 John 1:1: Is "that which was from the beginning" the gospel message or the person?
- Augustine: the Word made flesh handled; "Word of life" is Christ. Neuter "that which" covers the whole revelation of the person. Host: the person in his manifestation.
