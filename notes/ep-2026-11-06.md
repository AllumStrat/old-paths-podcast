## 1. SETTING AND BACKGROUND

### Ezekiel 24-26 (deepest)
- Date of 24:1: ninth year, tenth month, tenth day of Jehoiachin's exile, which coincides with Zedekiah's reign; same date as Jeremiah 39:1; 52:4; 2 Kings 25:1; later observed by the Jews as a fast (Zechariah 8:19). "It was doubtless the day on which the investment of the city was completed." (Ellicott, https://biblehub.com/commentaries/ellicott/ezekiel/24.htm). January 588 B.C. (series dating; episode 21 thread).
- 24:2 "write down the name of this day": Ellicott: the prophet was informed the same day; supposition that he heard by ordinary means "is quite inconsistent with the whole verse". Henry: news "many days' journey" away; when proved true, "a confirmation of the prophet's mission" (https://www.biblestudytools.com/commentaries/matthew-henry-complete/ezekiel/24.html). Henry also: last intelligence may have been that Rabbath of Ammon was the target (cf. 21:20).
- Pot parable 24:3-14: Ellicott: "not a symbolical action, but was simply a parable spoken to the people." Henry: echoes Jeremiah 1:13 seething pot, and confronts the princes' saying in 11:3 "This city is the caldron and we are the flesh."
- "Scum/corrosion" (chel'ah): Ellicott 24:6: "This word, which occurs five times in these verses... is found nowhere else. Interpreters are agreed in the correctness of the old Greek version of it, rust." City itself corroded. "no lot is to fall upon it to make a discrimination, since nothing is to be spared. All previous judgments had been partial; this is to be complete."
- Blood on bare rock 24:7: Ellicott: law required blood poured on ground and covered (Leviticus 4:7; 16:15; Deuteronomy 12:16; cf. Leviticus 17:13); Jerusalem put it on the hard rock, "glorying in her shame". Henry: "they commit their murders in the face of the sun".
- 24:12 ESV footnote: meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain.
- 24:15-27 Keil (https://biblehub.com/commentaries/kad/ezekiel/24.htm, also at verse page https://biblehub.com/commentaries/ezekiel/24-16.htm): same day as the blockade; "by a stroke" = sudden death inflicted by God (Numbers 14:37; 17:13). Mourning customs: uncover head and strew ashes (Isaiah 61:3), barefoot (2 Samuel 15:30; Isaiah 20:2), cover the beard/lower face (Micah 3:7); "bread of men" = bread neighbours sent to the house of mourning (Deuteronomy 26:14; Hosea 9:4; Jeremiah 16:7). Temple called "pride of your strength" from Leviticus 26:19; people trusted it (Jeremiah 7:4). Keil on 24:23: not "stolid indifference" but "overwhelming grief, for which there were no tears, no lamentation, but only deep inward sighing on account of the sins which had occasioned so terrible a calamity"; "This grief... leads to a clear perception of the sin, and also to true repentance".
- Ellicott 24:17: priests allowed to mourn nearest relations (Leviticus 21:2-3), "and Ezekiel is therefore here made an exception."
- Carson (age): if thirty in fifth year (1:1-2), now no more than thirty-four or thirty-five (For the Love of God vol. 2, Sept 21).
- 24:27 Ellicott: "for almost two years (comp. Ezekiel 24:1 with Ezekiel 33:21), from the investment of the city until he heard of its fall, Ezekiel gave no prophecy to the Israelites... He has, however, recorded a considerable number of prophecies against foreign nations (Ezekiel 25-32)."
- Oracles against nations: Ellicott intro to 25 (https://biblehub.com/commentaries/ellicott/ezekiel/25.htm): seven nations (Ammon, Moab, Edom, Philistia, Tyre, Sidon, Egypt); all were in common hostility to Babylon; Jeremiah 27:1-3 league; "It was, therefore, necessary for Israel to know that there was no help to be found against Babylon in any earthly power". Carson Sept 22 (https://gospeltranslations.org/wiki/For_the_Love_of_God,_Volume_2/September_22): seven like Amos; Babylon not included, God's agent; majority on Tyre; placed between 24 and 33 to heighten dramatic tension.
- Ammon: Henry 25: Josephus, Antiquities 10.181, Nebuchadnezzar destroyed Ammon about five years after Jerusalem; Ammonites accessory to Gedaliah's murder (Jeremiah 40:14). Archaeology note (not to quote): seal impression from Tell el-Umeiri naming an Ammonite king Baalyasha, widely proposed as Baalis (BAS "Seal of Baalis Surfaces" listing, https://library.biblicalarchaeology.org/article/seal-of-baalis-surfaces/). Mention only cautiously or skip.
- Edom: Henry: Psalm 137:7 "Rase it", Obadiah; fulfilled by Judas Maccabeus (1 Maccabees 5:3) and Hyrcanus (Josephus Antiquities 13.257).
- Tyre: Ellicott intro 26 (https://biblehub.com/commentaries/ellicott/ezekiel/26.htm): Old Tyre on the mainland, New Tyre on a rocky island about half a mile offshore; resisted Shalmaneser five years; Nebuchadnezzar besieged it thirteen years; Jerome asserts he captured it; friend of David and Solomon; later sold Hebrew captives (Joel 3:4-8; Amos 1:9-10). 26:1 eleventh year, month not given. 26:2 exultation "purely selfish and commercial". 26:3 "God will bring nation after nation to the destruction of Tyre as the sea throws wave after wave against her rock." 26:8 channel about 1,200 yards; Alexander's mole 250 years later made the island a peninsula. 26:12 nominative changes to "they"; "the long succession of calamities". 26:16 "princes of the sea" = merchant princes. 26:18 colonies: Cyprus, Carthage, Cadiz, etc. 26:20 tsevi/glory used of the land of Israel; "A ray of Messianic promise".
- Josephus, Against Apion 1.21 (https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2849/pg2849.txt): Phoenician records: "Nabuchodonosor besieged Tyre for thirteen years in the days of Ithobal, their king"; after a series of judges, "they sent and fetched Merbalus from Babylon" (Tyre under Babylon's control).
- Alexander: Poole on 26:12 (https://biblehub.com/commentaries/ezekiel/26-12.htm): "this was fulfilled when Alexander, with the ruins of old Tyre, its stones, timber, and rubbish, and trees from Lebanon, made a causeway from the continent to the island; and by that means took it, after seven months' toil". (Alexander's siege: 332 B.C.; JFB misprints 322.)
- Gill on 26:14 (https://biblehub.com/commentaries/ezekiel/26-14.htm): "must be understood with some restriction"; Tyre rebuilt, in the New Testament (Acts 21:3), "and in Jerom's time it was a most noble and beautiful city, as he on this passage observes"; destroyed 1291; "now quite uninhabited, unless by fishermen". Poole on 26:14: "not built in greatness and glory... or not by the inhabitants of old Tyre". Ellicott on 26:20: "This is to be understood of the Tyre that then was, the proud mistress of the sea."

### Job 9:1-20
- Job's first reply to Bildad (8). Henry (https://www.biblestudytools.com/commentaries/matthew-henry-complete/job/9.html): no reflection on his friends; "keeps to the merits of the cause". Outline: doctrine 9:2; proof 9:3-13; application 9:14-21.

## 3. INTERPRETIVE QUESTIONS

Ezekiel
1. Why did God take Ezekiel's wife and forbid him mourning? Poole: "by death I take from thee, but it is I the Lord, and I take her to myself, though from thee." Henry: the sign "cost the prophet very dear"; green tree/dry; we may suppose he prayed for her and was not heard, so how should he be heard for this people. Ligonier devotional: "God never finds Himself in a desperate situation" but the people's refusal to believe made it desperate from a human view. Carson: "Ezekiel is not silent because of the depth of his loss but because of the command of God."
2. What does the people's tearless response mean (24:22-23)? Carson (a) callousness (rejects), (b) too deep for grief (possible), (c) commanded not to grieve since judgment deserved (14:22-23; 1 Samuel 16:1). Keil: overwhelming grief with no tears, sighing for sin, leading to repentance. Henry: stupefied, despair, "mourn, not to God in prayer... but one towards another, murmuring". Benson: public calamity so great private sorrows not noticed.
3. Never rebuilt (26:14)? Gill, Poole, Ellicott, JFB as above.
Job
1. Is 9:2 confession or complaint? Henry: confession; others: complaint. Gregory: "man put with God is not justified".
2. Does 9:20 confess sin (Spurgeon) or protest unfair power (Pulpit, Cambridge)?
John
1. Is the synagogue ban historical? Calvin. 2. Why "Give glory to God"? Calvin (oath, Joshua 7:19); Augustine (deny what you received). 3. "Whether he is a sinner I do not know" - doubt? Chrysostom: "Not doubting (God forbid!) but knowing that He was not a sinner" - he lets the fact testify.
1 John
1. How "just" to forgive? Calvin (fidelity to promise) vs Spurgeon No. 255 (justice satisfied by substitute). 2. Do Christians still sin? 1:8, 10; Westminster Confession 6.5; Augustine on light sins. 3. "Walk in the light" sinless? Calvin: "integrity of conscience is alone that which distinguishes light from darkness".
