## 1. SETTING AND BACKGROUND

### Ezekiel 16 (deepest)
- Date: undated oracle. Falls inside the block that opens with 8:1 (sixth year of Jehoiachin's exile, sixth month, fifth day, late summer 592 B.C.) and runs to the next date at 20:1 (seventh year, 591 B.C.). Zedekiah reigns in Jerusalem as Babylon's vassal and is drifting toward Egypt; Ezekiel is in Tel-abib by the Chebar; still mute except when God opens his mouth (3:26; 33:22).
- Place in the book: chapters 12-19 answer the false securities of Jerusalem (episode 17: our prophets, our righteous men, we are God's vine). 15 ended with the useless vine and "because they have acted faithlessly" (15:8). 16 expands "faithless" into a whole life story. 17 (two eagles and a vine) is the political version; 23 retells the marriage allegory with two sisters, Oholah and Oholibah.
- Earlier prophets with the marriage image: Hosea 1-3 (ESV cross-reference at 16:15 lists Hosea 1:2), Jeremiah 2:2 ("the devotion of your youth, your love as a bride") and 3:1-10 (Jeremiah 3:11, faithless Israel more righteous than treacherous Judah, is the seed of 16:51). Ezekiel takes it furthest.
- Length: 63 verses, the longest chapter in Ezekiel. Jerome (via the SermonIndex catena of his commentary) notes it runs to "nearly ... two hundred verses" in his division up to "when I am appeased". [paraphrase only]
- Reception: Mishnah Megillah 4:10 (William Davidson translation on Sefaria): Rabbi Eliezer says one may not conclude [the Torah reading with a prophetic portion from] "Make known to Jerusalem her abominations" (Ezekiel 16:2). https://www.sefaria.org/Mishnah_Megillah.4.10 [paraphrase only; modern translation]
- 16:3 "your father was an Amorite and your mother a Hittite": Jerusalem was a Jebusite city until David took it (2 Samuel 5:6-9); Amorites and Hittites stand for the Canaanite peoples (Genesis 15:16; Deuteronomy 7:1; Hittites in Hebron, Genesis 23). Keil rejects reading it only of the city; it is the nation's moral pedigree. Calvin: God speaks of what they were "in themselves", "as far as the people were concerned, that they are looked upon as profane Gentiles."
- 16:4 birth customs: Calvin and Gill describe cutting the cord, washing, rubbing with salt to firm the skin, swaddling. Ligonier devotional: exposure of infant girls was "all too common in the pagan culture of Babylon in Ezekiel's day" and Ezekiel uses "the hygienic practices of ancient Near Eastern culture (salt rubbing)".
- 16:6 Ligonier devotional: "(Ancient Near Eastern parents adopted an infant in emergency situations by placing their hands on a newborn covered in birth fluids and declaring, "Live!")" [unsourced claim; paraphrase cautiously or omit]
- 16:8 corner (kanaph) of the garment: Ruth 3:9 (ESV cross-reference). Calvin thinks the nuptial rite is "only a conjecture" and reads it as God covering their disgrace at the exodus; Trapp: "A marriage rite is imported by this expression." Oath and covenant = Sinai (Calvin, Henry, Keil).
- 16:20-21 child sacrifice: Keil: the actual burning of children to Moloch from the time of Ahaz (2 Kings 16:3; 21:6; 23:10, Topheth in the Valley of Hinnom). God calls them "my children".
- 16:26-29 lovers Egypt, Assyria, Chaldea: political alliances as adultery (Calvin's two kinds of fornication: superstition and "perverse and unlawful treaties"). Ligonier: Ahaz to Assyria (2 Kings 16), others to Egypt (Isaiah 31:1), Hezekiah with Babylon (Isaiah 39).
- 16:27 and 57: daughters of the Philistines and of Syria (Edom in some manuscripts) despising Judah: Ahaz's reign (2 Chronicles 28:18; Keil).
- 16:46 Samaria "elder" (larger) sister to the north, Sodom "younger" (smaller) to the south (left hand and right hand when facing east; Henry).
- 16:49-50: Sodom's guilt; the Dead Sea always "under their nose" (Henry). ESV cross-reference at 16:48 to Matthew 10:15; 11:24.
- Hebrew notes: zanah, to play the whore, dominates the chapter (ESV footnote: "Or were unfaithful"); etnan, payment/hire (v31, 34, 41); hephek, "the contrary" (v34; Calvin's footnote: "the verb hphk, hephek, means to overthrow; hence the noun signifies 'inversion'"); chayi, "Live!" (v6, twice); kipper, atone (v63; KJV "when I am pacified toward thee"). Spurgeon on the word: properly "to cover a thing with that which adheres and sticks", same word as the pitch of Noah's ark (Genesis 6:14).
- 16:63 and the muteness thread: "never open your mouth again because of your shame". Ezekiel himself is mute (3:26) until the fall of Jerusalem (33:22).

### Job 5
- Eliphaz's first speech, second half (4:1-5:27). Patriarchal setting; Eliphaz of Teman (Edom; Jeremiah 49:7).
- Structure: 5:1-7 no holy one will answer you; fools are cut off; trouble is man's birthright. 5:8-16 "As for me, I would seek God": a hymn to God who reverses fortunes (rain, lifting the lowly, catching the crafty). 5:17-27 blessed is the one God reproves; sixfold, sevenfold deliverance (graded numerical saying); covenant with stones and beasts; grave like a sheaf. 5:27 "this we have searched out; it is true."
- 5:7 Hebrew: bene reshef, "sons of reshef"; Keil: reshef "signifies also a flame or blaze (Sol 8:6). Children of the flame is an appropriate name for sparks"; older interpreters took it as birds of prey; Gregory's Latin (Vulgate) reads "Man is born to labour, and the bird to flying."
- 5:13 quoted in 1 Corinthians 3:19, the only explicit New Testament quotation of Job's friends (thread from episode 17).
- 5:17 echoes Proverbs 3:11-12 (quoted in Hebrews 12:5-6).
- 42:7: God says the friends have not spoken of him what is right.

### John 8:21-30
- Same occasion: after the Feast of Booths, teaching in the treasury in the Court of the Women (8:20), "his hour had not yet come".
- 8:21 echoes 7:33-36; there they guessed the Dispersion, here they guess suicide (Henry: "see how indulged malice grows more and more malicious" [paraphrase]).
- 8:21 singular "your sin", 8:24 plural "your sins" (Calvin, Henry).
- 8:24 and 28 ego eimi, ESV "I am he". Ligonier devotional: no "he" in the Greek; ego eimi is the phrase of the Greek Old Testament at Exodus 3:14 and in Isaiah 40-55 (e.g., Isaiah 43:10).
- 8:25 crux: ten archen. ESV "Just what I have been telling you from the beginning." Augustine (Latin principium) reads "The Beginning"; Calvin: "from the beginning", "I did not arise suddenly, but as I was formerly promised"; Chrysostom (per Calvin) reads it continuously.
- 8:28 the second of three "lifted up" sayings (3:14; 8:28; 12:32-33).
- 8:30 "many believed in him", tested at 8:31 (tomorrow).

### 2 Peter 2:17-22
- Conclusion of chapter 2's portrait of the false teachers (2:1-22), before chapter 3's scoffers and the delay of the day. Peter writing near the end of his life from Rome, mid-60s A.D. (1:14).
- 17: waterless springs and mists; gloom of utter darkness reserved (cf. 2:4, Tartarus). Jude 12-13 parallels (waterless clouds).
- 18: "loud boasts of folly" (huperonka mataiotetos; Calvin's editor: "overswellings of vanity"); deleazo again (entice with bait, from 2:14); targets "those who are barely escaping".
- 19: promise freedom, slaves of corruption; maxim "whatever overcomes a person, to that he is enslaved" (Calvin: from military law, the conquered become slaves). John 8:34 tomorrow says the same.
- 20-21: escaped the defilements through the knowledge (epignosis) of Christ, again entangled; last state worse (Matthew 12:45, Luke 11:26); better never to have known.
- 22: Proverbs 26:11 dog; the sow saying is a non-biblical proverb (Calvin: "two ancient proverbs, the first of which is found as the saying of Solomon").

## 3. INTERPRETIVE QUESTIONS

Ezekiel 16
1. Why such graphic language in Scripture? Spurgeon (1876): could scarcely read it in public; but the Spirit applies it privately. Rabbi Eliezer kept it out of the synagogue prophetic reading. Answer: God chooses the most humiliating picture so that sin appears exceedingly sinful (Spurgeon) and so the sinner knows her abominations (16:2; Calvin: "men are often so blinded by their vices that they do not perceive what is sufficiently evident to every one else").
2. Restoration of Sodom (16:53-55)? Calvin: irony, impossibility. Henry: most take as threat; some (Grotius, Jewish writers) Moab and Ammon; Henry notes promise reading too. Keil: real promise to the literal Sodom, realized "beyond the present order of things"; pardon of Sodom "carries with it the assurance that mercy will be extended to all heathen nations"; rejects Moab/Ammon and Jerome/Origen's typical heathenism. Calvin on 16:61 reads the sisters as "all nations" received as daughters through the gospel from Zion. Host: 16:61 makes the promise reading likelier; with Calvin's caution that the wicked mass is not saved.
3. "Not on account of the covenant with you" (16:61): Calvin: God's covenant, not theirs; Trapp: not Sinai but covenant of grace from Zion; Henry: not by the covenant of peculiarity.
4. Covenant continuity: Calvin "the new covenant so flowed from the old, that it was almost the same in substance, while distinguished in form" ; Westminster Confession 7.6.

Job 5
1. How can Paul quote Eliphaz (1 Cor 3:19) when God says he did not speak rightly (42:7)? Spurgeon No. 43: friends often uninspired and wrong, but this chapter "in the main" the word of God, because Paul quotes it. Henry: friends not always directed by the Spirit, but their general doctrines "expressed the pious sense of the patriarchal age", and Paul "quoted v. 13 for canonical scripture". Calvin on 1 Cor 3:19: "it is with good reason that Paul applies it". Gregory: true sentence wrongly aimed at Job ("Which same sentence would have been true, had it not been delivered against the patience of so great a man"). Ligonier: true statements wrongly applied. Host: inspiration guarantees an accurate record of all speech; truth of individual sentences judged by the rest of Scripture; 5:13 is true; the error is the target.
2. Is 5:17 true for Job? Yes as doctrine, no as diagnosis: Ligonier "no category for nonpunitive discipline"; Gregory: Eliphaz thought Job was visited "not with the trial of probation, but of purification".
3. Sparks or birds (5:7)? Keil sparks.

John 8:21-30
1. What does "I am he" mean? Calvin: office, not essence; Augustine, Henry, Gill, Ligonier: divine name (Exodus 3:14; Isaiah 43:10 LXX). Host: both; the absolute form in 8:58 settles the trajectory.
2. 8:25 "from the beginning"? Augustine vs Calvin/ESV.
3. How will lifting up make them know? Chrysostom: resurrection, miracles, destruction of the temple; Calvin: some know too late in judgment; Augustine: the Pentecost converts; Sproul: double sense, crucifixion and exaltation.

2 Peter 2:17-22
1. Does 2:20-21 teach that true believers can be lost? Calvin: the faithful sin but do not allow sin dominion; Gill: escape by outward reformation and notional knowledge, natures unchanged; Westminster Confession 17.1; Ligonier: "all true converts will never fall finally away" yet the warnings are means.
2. Who are those "barely escaping"? New converts (Ligonier, Calvin: those who had "really escaped from the society of those in error").
3. Why the two animals? Calvin: gospel as purge and as washing; the animal's nature unchanged (Gill).
