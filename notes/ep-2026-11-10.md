## 1. SETTING AND BACKGROUND

### Ezekiel 31-32
- Place in book: oracles against the nations, chs. 25-32, between the beginning of the siege (24) and the fugitive's news (33:21). Egypt receives seven dated words (Keil, used ep 24). Chapters 31-32 are the fifth, sixth, and seventh.
- 31:1 eleventh year, third month, first day: about June 587 B.C., two months after 30:20 (April 587). Jerusalem still under siege; Hophra's relief army had been turned back (Jer 37:5-7).
- 32:1 twelfth year, twelfth month, first day: about March 585 B.C. (Pulpit: "March, B.C. 584, nineteen months attar the destruction of Jerusalem"; Ellicott: "one year and between six and seven months after the destruction of Jerusalem" and "nearly two months (Ezekiel 33:21) since Ezekiel had heard of this calamity through a fugitive"). 32:17: month not stated; presumably the same month, a fortnight later (Cambridge, Ellicott); LXX reads first month.
- Barnes (32:1): in the meantime Gedaliah murdered and the flight to Egypt (Jer 41-43); Jeremiah 44 probably contemporaneous.
- Assyria's fall: Nineveh fell in 612 to Nabopolassar and Cyaxares (ABC 3, Fall of Nineveh Chronicle). Last king Ashur-uballit II at Harran, abandoned 610, failed to retake 609 with "a large army from Egypt". Egypt supported Assyria (Livius intro). 2 Kings 23:29 ESV: "Pharaoh Neco king of Egypt went up to the king of Assyria to the river Euphrates. King Josiah went to meet him, and Pharaoh Neco killed him at Megiddo." Carchemish 605 finished the Assyrian remnant and broke Egypt (Barnes on 31:3: "The Assyrian empire, after having been supreme in Asia for four centuries, had been overthrown by the united forces of the Babylonians and Medes, in the year of the battle of Carchemish (605 b.c.), which had broken the power of Egypt.").
- Cedar: Lebanon cedars (Ellicott); Isaiah 37:24 ESV Sennacherib boasts "to cut down its tallest cedars"; Isaiah 10:33-34 "Lebanon will fall by the Majestic One"; Daniel 4:10-12 Nebuchadnezzar's tree (OT parallel); Ezekiel 17 cedar imagery.
- Cambridge: "It is evident that the Assyrian has nothing to do here" (reads 'asshur' as a tree name, teasshur); Keil rejects: "the question, whom does he resemble? is asked again in Ezekiel 31:18" and the fall is described as past, so it "can only be Asshur, and not Egypt at all." Pulpit sides with the traditional Assyria reading; compares 31:18 to Nathan's "Thou art the man!"
- Uncircumcised: Pulpit (31:18): "As a matter of fact, the Egyptians practiced circumcision, and Ezekiel must be thought of as using the term as simply an epithet of scorn." Keil quotes Kliefoth: "in the sense in which Ezekiel understands circumcision, the Egyptians were uncircumcised, even if they were accustomed to circumcise their flesh."
- 32:27 swords under heads: Keil follows Vulgate and rabbis: Meshech-Tubal "would not receive the honour of resting in the nether world along with those fallen heroes whose weapons were buried with them in the grave, because they fell with honour." Ellicott prefers a question. Pulpit: LXX omits the negative. Poole: "a ceremony observed in pompous funerals of great captains".
- 32:7-8 Keil: "The thought on which the figure rests is that of the day of the Lord, the day of God's judgment, on which the lights of heaven lose their brightness" (paraphrase only).
- Hebrew: Sheol (31:15-17; 32:21, 27); bor, pit (31:14, 16; 32:18, 23-25, 29-30); erets tachtit, world below; nacham, comforted (31:16; 32:31; Keil: "to comfort oneself"). Compton (Ligonier): Hebrew uses qever or bor/shachath for grave; "Ezek. 32:23, 24".
- Fathers: Gregory the Great, Moralia 32 (on Job 40:19 Behemoth) reads Ezek 31:8-9 as the devil's pre-fall eminence among angels ("cedars, fir trees, and planes" = "bands of heavenly virtues"). No patristic commentary on Ezek 32 found (Jerome's Ezekiel commentary not in NPNF; searched).
- Spurgeon: searched spurgeon.org Ezekiel scripture index: no sermons on Ezekiel 30, 31, or 32 (https://www.spurgeon.org/resource-library/scripture-index/ezekiel). Morning and Evening/Faith's Checkbook index also skip them.
- Carson For the Love of God vol 2 checked: Sept 28 (Ezek 31) and Sept 29 (Ezek 32) meditate on Psalms 79-80, not Ezekiel.
- Desiring God: no Piper material found on Ezekiel 31-32. Ligonier: no Ezekiel 31-32 devotional; used Sproul's Tough Questions on OT afterlife and Compton's Sheol article.

### Job 11
- Zophar the Naamathite, third friend, first speech; the shortest and bluntest. Structure: 1-6 rebuke of Job's talk and claim; 7-12 the unsearchable God; 13-20 call to repent with promise.
- 11:6 Keil: kiflayim "denotes not only that which is twice as great, but generally that which far surpasses"; hashsheh "denotes here oblivioni dare" (to give to forgetfulness): God has let some of your iniquity be forgotten.
- 11:12 Ellicott: "Literally, it runs, And hollow man is instructed, and the wild ass's colt is born a man." "by the wild ass's colt Zophar means Job." Barnes: navuv from "to bore through, and then to be hollow".
- Job 42:7 God says the friends have not spoken of me what is right. 1 Corinthians 3:19 quotes Eliphaz (Job 5:13), so their words can be true in themselves.

### John 10:11-21
- Continuous with 9:40-41 (ep 24). Feast of Tabernacles to Dedication (10:22). Ezekiel 34 (tomorrow) is the backdrop: false shepherds, God himself shepherd, "my servant David." Sproul's sermon quotes Ezekiel 34:1-5.
- kalos, good, noble. "lays down his life" (tithemi ten psychen), Johannine (13:37; 15:13). Third division (schisma) over him: 7:43; 9:16; 10:19.
- Calvin footnote: Latin "grex" (flock) not "fold" in 10:16: one flock, one shepherd.

### 1 John 2:18-23

- Calvin: John refers to Cerinthus and Carpocrates "with the ancients".

## 3. INTERPRETIVE QUESTIONS

Ezekiel: (1) Is the cedar Assyria or Egypt? Keil/Pulpit/Ellicott: Assyria as mirror; Cambridge/Ewald: a kind of cedar (Egypt). Host with Keil. (2) Is 32:17-32 a map of the afterlife? Keil: Sheol "the gathering-place of the dead; not the place of punishment for the damned"; Gill: "all the comfort wicked men have in hell"; Pulpit: "the germs of the later belief of Judaism in Paradise and Gehenna"; Sproul: OT afterlife "vague and shadowy"; Compton: three views; WCF 32.1. Host: a dirge with real theology, not a floor plan. (3) "Comforted" — how? Keil: he sees he could expect no better lot; Pulpit: "fiend-like temper which exults in the miseries of others"; Gill: poor comfort.
Job: (1) How to use the friends' words (Spurgeon, Gregory, Calvin via Moore). (2) 11:6 is it true? Spurgeon: true for all of us, cruel to say to Job; Gregory: rightly said, wrongly addressed. (3) 11:12 meaning.
John: (1) Who is the hired hand (Augustine: the silent pastor; Calvin: ministers serving for gain, still to be heard; Sproul: every pastor is at best a good hireling). (2) Other sheep: Gentiles (Calvin, Sproul) and the fold = Israel (Calvin) or the disciples (Spurgeon 1713). (3) Who raised Jesus (Augustine: the Son also raised himself; Chrysostom: power not belonging to men).
1 John: (1) "Last hour" 2,000 years later (Augustine, Calvin). (2) Can true believers fall away (Calvin, WCF 17.1, Ligonier). (3) "You all have knowledge" (Calvin: confined to the subject).
