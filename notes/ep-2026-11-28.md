## 1. Setting and background

### Daniel 10-12 (deepest)
- Date: "the third year of Cyrus king of Persia" (10:1), counting from Cyrus's capture of Babylon in 539 B.C., so about 536 B.C. About two years after ch. 9 (first year of Darius the Mede). Cyrus's decree (Ezra 1, 538) has already let the first exiles return; Daniel, now in his mid-eighties (taken in 605), did not go. Ezra 4:4-5 tells of opposition that frustrated the rebuilding "all the days of Cyrus", which fits Daniel's mourning.
- 10:4 "the twenty-fourth day of the first month": Nisan; his three-week fast (from about the 3rd) ran across Passover (14th) and Unleavened Bread (15-21). Place: the bank of the Tigris (Hebrew Hiddekel).
- Structure: chs. 10-12 are one vision: prologue (ch. 10, the messenger and the heavenly conflict), the revelation (11:2-12:4), epilogue (12:5-13, the oath and "go your way"). Book: chs. 1-6 court narratives, 7-12 visions; Hebrew resumes at 8:1. This is the fourth and last vision, an expansion of chs. 8 and 9.
- Man in linen (10:5-6): compare Ezekiel 9 and 10 (scribe in linen), Ezekiel 1 (beryl, bronze, sound of many waters) and Revelation 1:13-15. Calvin takes him as an angel only; others as the pre-incarnate Son. Keep this to one sentence.
- 10:13, 20-21: "prince of the kingdom of Persia", "prince of Greece", "Michael, one of the chief princes", "Michael, your prince" (plural you, Israel). Michael appears again in 12:1 and in Jude 9 and Revelation 12:7.
- History behind 11:2-35 (Calvin, Jerome, Ligonier "Kings of North and South"):
  - 11:2 three more Persian kings after Cyrus, then a fourth "far richer" who stirs up all against Greece: Xerxes (invasion of 480 B.C.).
  - 11:3-4 Alexander the Great (336-323), kingdom split "toward the four winds", not to his posterity.
  - 11:5 king of the south Ptolemy I; "one of his princes" Seleucus I.
  - 11:6 the daughter of the south: Berenice, daughter of Ptolemy II, married to Antiochus II about 252; she and her son murdered after Laodice's return.
  - 11:7-9 her brother Ptolemy III's revenge and plunder of Seleucid gods.
  - 11:10-12 Antiochus III and the battle of Raphia (217 B.C.), Ptolemy IV wins.
  - 11:13-16 Antiochus III returns; Panium (200 B.C.), "stand in the glorious land", Judea passes to the Seleucids.
  - 11:17 "the daughter of women": Cleopatra I given to Ptolemy V (193); she sided with Egypt.
  - 11:18-19 coastlands; "a commander" Lucius Scipio defeats Antiochus III at Magnesia (190); he dies 187 robbing a temple in Elymais.
  - 11:20 Seleucus IV, "an exactor of tribute": Heliodorus and the temple treasury (2 Maccabees 3); Seleucus dies "neither in anger nor in battle" (175).
  - 11:21-35 Antiochus IV Epiphanes ("God manifest"; nicknamed Epimanes, "madman", Poole), "a contemptible person" who obtained the kingdom by flatteries. 11:22 "prince of the covenant" = the high priest Onias III (killed about 171). 11:25-28 first Egyptian campaign (170-169). 11:29-30 second campaign, "ships of Kittim" (Romans): the envoy Popilius Laenas drew a circle in the sand around Antiochus at Eleusis near Alexandria (168) (Jerome reports it). 11:31 temple profaned, daily offering stopped, "abomination that makes desolate" (1 Maccabees 1:54: 15 Chislev 167 B.C.; Zeus Olympios). 11:32 "the people who know their God shall stand firm and take action": Mattathias and Judas Maccabeus. 11:33-35 the wise (maskilim) teach many and fall by sword and flame (2 Maccabees 6-7, Eleazar, the seven brothers), "a little help" (Maccabean revolt), refined and made white. Temple rededicated 25 Chislev 164 (Hanukkah, cf. John 10:22).
  - Antiochus died in 164 B.C. in Persia (Jerome cites Polybius and Diodorus: after trying to plunder a temple at Elymais), not "between the sea and the glorious holy mountain" (11:45).
- 12:1 "time of trouble, such as never has been": echoed by Jesus, Matthew 24:21. "Everyone whose name shall be found written in the book": cf. Exodus 32:32, Psalm 69:28, Malachi 3:16.
- 12:2 Hebrew: "many of those who sleep in the dust of the earth (admat-afar) shall awake (yaqitsu)": compare Isaiah 26:19. "everlasting life" (hayye olam): Gill notes the phrase is never before in the Old Testament. "contempt" (dir'on). Reformation Study Bible: a clear prediction of bodily resurrection of godly and ungodly. Jesus echoes it in John 5:28-29.
- 12:3 maskilim (Strong's 7919, "those who are wise", Hiphil: those who make wise) and "those who turn many to righteousness" (umatsdiqe harabbim, 6663 + 7227). Isaiah 52:13 "my servant shall act wisely" (yaskil, 7919) and 53:11 "make many to be accounted righteous" (yatsdiq larabbim). Daniel's wise teachers are the Servant's people: they do on a small scale what the Servant does fully. Verified on Bible Hub interlinear: https://biblehub.com/interlinear/daniel/12-3.htm ; https://biblehub.com/interlinear/isaiah/53-11.htm ; https://biblehub.com/interlinear/isaiah/52-13.htm
- 12:4, 9 "shut up the words and seal the book": preserve and authenticate till the end, contrast 9:24 "seal both vision and prophet". "Many shall run to and fro": searching (Amos 8:12).
- 12:7 "time, times, and half a time" = 7:25. 12:11-12: 1,290 and 1,335 days. Jerome: Antichrist's three and a half years, and 45 more days of waiting; Porphyry: the Antiochus desecration (but that lasted three years). Hold loosely.
- 12:13 "you shall rest and shall stand in your allotted place (goral, lot) at the end of the days": death then resurrection (Calvin).

### Job 22
- Opens the third cycle of speeches (22-27); Eliphaz's third and last speech. Bildad's third speech is short (25), and Zophar has none: the friends run out.
- Structure: 22:2-4 God gains nothing from you; 22:5-11 specific charges (pledges taken for nothing, the naked stripped, water and bread withheld, widows sent away, orphans crushed) and therefore snares; 22:12-20 God is high, and you say "What does God know?" (twisting Job 21); 22:21-30 call to repent with promises.
- Charges are false: Job 29:12-17 and 31:16-22 (answered point by point). Eliphaz has no evidence; his theology requires the sins.
- 22:21 "Agree with God (haskhen-na, KJV "acquaint now thyself with him"), and be at peace". 22:24-25 "the Almighty will be your gold". 22:30 KJV "island of the innocent": Hebrew 'i as negative particle, "even the one who is not innocent" (Gill, Barnes). Irony: in 42:7-8 Eliphaz is the guilty one delivered through Job's prayer (Barnes notes this).

### John 13:31-38
- Upper room, Thursday night of Passion Week. Judas has just gone out "and it was night" (13:30, ep. 36). This begins the Farewell Discourse (13:31-17:26).
- "Now is the Son of Man glorified" (aorist edoxasthe): the hour of glory (12:23) is the cross. Five uses of glorify in two verses.
- "Little children" (teknia), only here in John's Gospel; frequent in 1 John. "Where I am going you cannot come" repeats 7:34; 8:21.
- New commandment (entole kaine): Leviticus 19:18 was old; new standard "just as I have loved you" (the foot washing, 13:1-15; the cross). Latin mandatum novum, source of "Maundy" Thursday. Peter's boast 13:37; prediction 13:38; fulfillment 18:15-27; restoration 21:15-19 ("follow me", 21:19, answers "you will follow afterward").

### Jude 17-25
- Jude, brother of James, probably 60s A.D. After the denunciation (5-16), "But you, beloved" (17, 20) turns to the faithful.
- 17-18: apostolic prediction of scoffers; nearly verbatim with 2 Peter 3:3. 19: "worldly people" (psychikoi, "soulish", natural) "devoid of the Spirit" (pneuma me echontes).
- 20-21: one command (keep yourselves in the love of God, teresate) with three participles: building, praying in the Holy Spirit, waiting for mercy.
- 22-23: textual variant: two or three groups; ESV three: doubters, those snatched from the fire (Amos 4:11; Zechariah 3:2-4, the brand plucked and the filthy garments), others shown mercy with fear, hating the garment stained (chiton, the undergarment) by the flesh.
- 24-25: doxology. "Keep" in v. 24 is phylaxai (guard), not tereo; "from stumbling" aptaistous; "blameless" amomous (sacrificial word, without blemish). Tereo runs through the letter: v. 1 kept for Jesus Christ, v. 6 angels kept in chains, v. 13 gloom reserved, v. 21 keep yourselves. "Only God, our Savior, through Jesus Christ our Lord".

## 3. Interpretive questions
### Daniel
1. Who is the prince of Persia (10:13)? Piper: a spiritual being assigned to the kingdom; the battle in heaven ran exactly as long as Daniel's prayer (D13). Calvin: the angel fought against the Persian king (Cambyses), restraining him (D9). Answer: the text calls Michael a "prince" too, so princes here are angelic; Calvin's practical point (prayer heard on day one, D8) stands either way.
2. How can chapter 11 be so exact? Porphyry (3rd c.) said it was written after the events, under Antiochus; modern critical scholarship agrees. Jerome: the precision proves the prophecy (D1); Duncan: unbelief here masks a God who cannot rule history; prophecy is God's interpretation (D16). Answer: confessional view, God who rules history can tell it.
3. Who is the king of 11:36-45? Critics: still Antiochus (but his death does not fit 11:45). Calvin: the Roman Empire (D6, D7). Jerome (D3, D4) and Poole (D20): Antichrist with Antiochus as type; Tabletalk (D22) tentatively the same. Paul's "man of lawlessness ... exalts himself against every so-called god" (2 Thessalonians 2:4) draws on 11:36. Answer: type and fulfillment; the final enemy, followed immediately by resurrection.
4. 12:2 "many": Calvin, "many" put for all (contrast with one) ; Gill: literal resurrection, against Porphyry's figurative reading.
### Job 22
1. Are Eliphaz's words true? Henry: very true in itself but unjustly applied (J3), and the last section good counsel (J1). Spurgeon uses 22:21 devotionally (J5). Answer: true words, wrong diagnosis; God's verdict in 42:7.
2. 22:30 "island" or "not innocent": Gill, Barnes (J7, J4).
### John 13:31-38
1. Why is the cross "glory"? Calvin (G7); Augustine "What says the day when the night had gone out?" (G5); Piper on "now" (G8).
2. What is new about the new commandment? Augustine: "as I have loved you", love that renews (G6). Sproul: no place for treason (G10). Piper: pattern and power (G9).
3. Peter's boast: Augustine (G3, G4), Chrysostom (G1): he learned he did not so love as he was loved; denial allowed for his humbling.
### Jude 17-25
1. "Keep yourselves" and "him who is able to keep you": Manton (N1, N2, N3), Calvin (N8).
2. How to treat the wavering: Calvin (N7), Clement (N9).
3. Stumbling: Spurgeon (N4-N6).
