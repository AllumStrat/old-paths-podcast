## 1. SETTING AND BACKGROUND

### Daniel 3-4 (deepest)
- Where the story stands: Daniel 2:46-49 ends with the king falling on his face before Daniel, confessing "your God is God of gods and Lord of kings," and promoting Daniel's three friends over the province of Babylon, while Daniel stays at the king's court (2:49). That last detail explains Daniel's absence in chapter 3. Last episode closed: "He heard that his kingdom would end, and he built one that would not" (ep. 33 transcript).
- Nebuchadnezzar II: enthroned September 605 B.C. (Babylonian Chronicle, ep. 33), died 562 (Calvin translation editor's chronology tables, calcom24 ~line 15505ff: "562. Death, after about forty-four years' reign").
- Date of chapter 3: "This is unknown ... Theodoret and Chrysostom fix upon the eighteenth year of the king's reign" (Calvin editor's dissertation "The Colossus at Dura", calcom24 line ~16449). The eighteenth year = 587/586 B.C., the year Jerusalem fell (Jeremiah 52:29 counts the deportation in his eighteenth year). The Septuagint opens chapter 3 with "In the eighteenth year." Use cautiously: "an old tradition."
- The image: sixty by six cubits. Ligonier "Courage Under Fire": "a gold (probably gold-plated) image measuring (in modern equivalents) ninety feet tall and nine feet wide." Ligonier "The Courage of Holy Conviction": "as tall as a nine-story building", built "to cement his political power, exalt his glory, and superstitiously counteract Daniel's inspired interpretation of his dream." Calvin's editor: "there is no proof that the statue had throughout a human form. Columns with a human head on the top were often erected"; also "allowing for a pedestal." Sixty and six reflect Babylonian base-60 reckoning (host's observation; keep brief or skip).
- Dura: Calvin's editor: a plain; one derivation from a word meaning "an enclosure."
- Chapter 3 repetition: the list of eight officials (3:2, 3:3) and six instruments (3:5, 7, 10, 15); the phrase "the image that Nebuchadnezzar had set up" repeated. Spurgeon: "Thirteen times their names recur in this chapter, like a refrain."
- Aramaic section continues (2:4b-7:28).
- Chapter 4: a royal proclamation in the king's own voice (4:1-3, 4-18, 34-37), third person in the madness section (4:28-33). Jerome: "The epistle of Nebuchadnezzar was inserted in the volume of the prophet ..." against Porphyry. Ellicott (via Bible Hub 4:34) notes the doxologies echo Psalm 145:13 and Isaiah 40:17 and conjectures Daniel composed the letter.
- Date of ch. 4: Calvin editor's tables: "569. Daniel 4:30. Driven out into the fields. 563. Restored after seven years. 562. Death"; Gill on 4:34 (Usher/Prideaux) "before Christ 563 ... after which he lived but one year."
- Archaeology: the East India House Inscription (British Museum), found in the ruins of Babylon before 1803, dated 604-562 B.C., records Nebuchadnezzar's building and religious works in Babylon (British Museum collection record, via search; do not quote its wording). Fits 4:30 "which I have built."
- "Seven periods of time" ('iddanin): most read seven years (Jerome, Gill, Calvin).
- Watcher ('ir): "a watcher, a holy one" (4:13, 17, 23), an angel of the heavenly council; only in Daniel 4.
- 4:35 "none can stay his hand": Keil: "to strike on the hand, to hinder, derived from the custom of striking children on the hand in chastising them" (Bible Hub 4:34 page).

### Job 19
- Job's reply to Bildad's second speech (ch. 18). Promised twice on air (17:15 "where then is my hope?"; 18:21 "him who knows not God").
- Structure: 2-6 complaint against the friends ("These ten times," i.e., over and over); 7-12 God as a besieging army ("siege ramp," 12); 13-20 abandonment by brothers, guests, servants, wife, children, intimate friends; 20 "skin of my teeth"; 21-22 "Have mercy on me, have mercy on me, O you my friends"; 23-24 wish for words written, inscribed, engraved with iron pen and lead in rock; 25-27 Redeemer; 28-29 warning.
- go'el: Barnes: blood-avenger, "vindicator of violated rights"; Lev 25:25-26 (land redemption), Num 35 (avenger of blood), Ruth 3-4 (kinsman). Spurgeon No. 504 (1863): goel = kinsman; duties to buy back the heritage, redeem by power, avenge blood.
- Earlier steps: 9:33 arbiter, 16:19 witness in heaven, 17:3 surety (Pulpit: "thirdsman ... can scarcely be other than a Divine Personage").

### John 12:37-50
- John's summary at the end of the public ministry (end of the "book of signs"); 12:36 "he departed and hid himself from them." Chapter 13 begins the upper room.
- Two Isaiah texts: 53:1 and 6:10. Then 12:41. Then the secret believers. Then 12:44-50 Jesus "cried out", a summary of his public teaching.

### 2 John
- "The elder." Traditional authorship: John the apostle (Ligonier "Truth and Love": "John, the son of Zebedee, is the author of all three letters"). Late first century, Ephesus.
- Ligonier "Truth and Love": itinerant teachers were "welcomed into the homes of believers"; "this system was open to abuses."

## 3. INTERPRETIVE QUESTIONS

Daniel 3:
- Q1 Who is the fourth man (3:25)? Hippolytus: the Word, recognized by Nebuchadnezzar; no name yet, not yet born of the Virgin. Spurgeon No. 2217: the Son of God left heaven to stand by them. Henry Concise: "supported and encouraged by the presence of the Son of God." Gill (per Barnes) and Tertullian, Augustine, Hilary (per Barnes) say Christ. Jerome: Septuagint says angel; "as the majority think, the Lord our Savior"; he doubts a godless king merited the vision and follows Symmachus; typologically it foreshadows Christ. Calvin: "One of his angels"; the king "could not recognize the only-begotten Son of God"; called "son of God" by the usual custom. Barnes: an angel (3:28 the king himself says "sent his angel"). Keil: we may hold either. Host: hold it lightly; Nebuchadnezzar's words cannot carry the doctrine, but whoever it was, he was sent from God to be with them in the fire, and Isaiah 43:2.
- Q2 "But if not" (3:18): doubt? Jerome: not "if He is not able" but "if He does not will." Ellicott: "There is not the slightest ground for supposing that they expected a miraculous deliverance." Ligonier: "their refusal was not based on their possessing sure knowledge." Calvin: double reason: God is able; and life is not worth denying God.
Daniel 4:
- Q3 4:27, can alms atone? Jerome: deeds of mercy delayed sentence twelve months; pride lost it. Calvin: "break off" (peraq); repentance shown in second-table duties; Papists abuse it for satisfactions. Host sides with Calvin.
- Q4 Was Nebuchadnezzar converted? Henry Concise: "There can be no reasonable doubt that Nebuchadnezzar was a true penitent, and an accepted believer." Henry 4:1-18 "lead us to hope." Calvin: "this shews how long it takes to cure pride." Jerome: 4:35 spoken "like a worldling" (impugning God's justice) - a dissent. Host: hopeful, not certain.
Job 19:
- Q1 Who is the go'el? Cambridge: God; "he dies but his redeemer lives". Gill: the Son of God. Spurgeon: Christ, kinsman. Gregory: "he who does not say, Creator, but Redeemer". Host: Job means God, the very God he says is against him; a God who is also next of kin.
- Q2 19:26 in or without my flesh? Cambridge: "without my flesh". Barnes/Poole/JFB/Pulpit: from my flesh, bodily vision (19:27 "mine eyes"). Clement of Rome read it as resurrection of this flesh. Host: flesh, because of 19:27.
John 12:37-50:
- Q1 "Could not believe" (12:39). Augustine Tr. 53: because they would not; God hardens by withdrawing aid, by hidden but just judgment. Chrysostom Hom. 68: "could not" put for "would not"; the prophecy foretold, did not cause. Sproul: judicial hardening; God "turns the sinner over to his sin."
- Q2 Whose glory did Isaiah see (12:41)? Chrysostom: the Father's, the dignity one; Augustine: Christ, seen symbolically; Sproul: the pre-incarnate Logos.
- Q3 Were the secret believers saved? Calvin: weak faith, "lukewarm, or rather cold"; Augustine: if they advanced, they would overcome the love of human glory. Host: John 19:38-39 Joseph and Nicodemus (perhaps skip).
2 John:
- Q1 Who is the elect lady? Clement of Alexandria: "a Babylonian lady, by name Electa, and indicates the election of the holy Church." Ligonier: an individual and her family. TGC (search snippet only, not fetched): a church. Host: a church, v. 13.
- Q2 Does v. 10 forbid all hospitality to unbelievers? Pulpit: "a rule for a special case, not laying down a general principle." Ligonier: "not forbidding contact with non-believers in general"; forbids "official sanction." Gill: neither the house of God nor your house. Irenaeus 1.16.3 applies it to the Gnostics. Host: no platform, no partnership.
