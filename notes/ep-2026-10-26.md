## 1. SETTING AND BACKGROUND

### Lamentations 3
- Date and situation: written in the aftermath of the fall of Jerusalem to Nebuchadnezzar's army in the summer of 586 B.C. (fifth month, Av; 2 Kings 25:8-9; Jeremiah 52:12-13). Temple burned; Zedekiah blinded and taken to Babylon; Gedaliah's governorship (Jeremiah 40-41). Traditional authorship: Jeremiah (LXX preface; 2 Chronicles 35:25), held by Henry, Gill, Calvin.
- Structure of the book: five poems; 1, 2, 4 single acrostics of 22 stanzas; chapter 3 a triple acrostic of 66 lines (three lines per letter, each starting with the same letter); chapter 5 has 22 lines but no acrostic. Chapter 3 is the center of the book. Henry: "that was in single alphabets, this is in a treble one." Calvin: "each letter is thrice repeated." (calcom21, 3:2)
- Speaker shift: chapters 1-2 Zion as a woman; chapter 3 "the man" (geber, a strong man, a man in his vigor; also in 3:27, 35, 39). Calvin: "he spoke before of the people under the person of a woman... but now the Prophet himself comes before us." Calvin thinks the poem sets forth "the common calamity of the whole people," not Jeremiah's prison experience. Henry: "it seems rather to be spoken in the person of the church now in captivity."
- Pe before ayin again in 3 (stanza 46-48 pe, mouths of enemies; 49-51 ayin, eyes flowing).
- Ligonier, "Hope Amid the Ruins": 3:22-33 is "the very center of the book."
- Divine name: in 3:1-17 God is only "he"; Calvin on 3:1: "He does not mention the name of God, but Jeremiah speaks of him as of one well known, using only a pronoun." The name Yahweh first appears at 3:18 (K&D translation "my hope from Jahveh").
- Textual: 3:22 ESV footnote: "Syriac, Targum; Hebrew Because of the steadfast love of the Lord, we are not cut off." Gill: "Jarchi observes, that 'tamnu' is as 'tammu'"; Targum, Septuagint, Syriac read the mercies not ceasing. Calvin prefers "the mercies of God were not consumed."
- Jeremiah 38:6 cistern "no water, but only mud" vs 3:54 "water closed over my head": Calvin says the pit image is "a metaphorical expression or a similitude" (3:53).

### Song of Songs 8:1-7
- The Song's last chapter; 8:5-7 is widely treated as the climax. Third adjuration refrain (2:7, 3:5, 8:4). "Who is that coming up from the wilderness" echoes 3:6 (Solomon's procession) and 6:10.
- Seal (chotam): a personal stamp or cylinder seal worn on a cord around the neck (over the heart) or as a ring on the hand; Genesis 38:18 Judah's seal and cord; Jeremiah 22:24 signet ring (Ellicott cites Jeremiah 22:24: "A symbol of something especially dear and precious.").
- Ellicott: jealousy "Strong passion, from a word meaning to be red with flame; not in a bad sense, as the parallelism shows"; "A most vehement flame.-Literally, a flame of Jah, the only place where a sacred name occurs in the book". Flashes (reshef) compared with Deuteronomy 32:24 "burning heat".
- Henry: reads it as Christ and the church; "Some make these to be the words of Christ to his spouse."

### John 7:14-24
- Feast of Booths (Tabernacles), autumn, roughly six months after the Passover of chapter 6; about the middle of the eight-day feast Jesus begins teaching in the temple courts after going up privately (7:10).
- "How is it that this man has learning" - literally "knows letters" (ESV footnote), meaning trained in the Scriptures and rabbinic learning. Ellicott: "He who has never been technically trained as a Rabbi is acquainted with the literature of the schools." Compare Acts 4:13 of Peter and John, "uneducated, common men."
- "One work" = healing of the man ill 38 years at Bethesda on a Sabbath (John 5:1-18), which provoked the plot to kill him (5:18).
- Circumcision on the eighth day (Leviticus 12:3; from the fathers, Genesis 17:10) overrides the Sabbath. Ellicott: "according to Rabbinic precept, 'circumcision vacated the Sabbath.'" Gill cites Jewish writings: circumcision "drives away the sabbath."
- Isaiah 11:3 (cross reference for 7:24): the Branch "shall not judge by what his eyes see."

### 1 Peter 5:1-7
- Peter writing probably from Rome (5:13 "Babylon") about A.D. 62-64 to churches of Asia Minor under slander and coming fiery trial (4:12). 5:1-5 elders and younger; 5:6-7 humility and anxiety; 5:8-11 the devil (tomorrow).
- "Fellow elder" (sympresbyteros). "Witness of the sufferings": Ellicott says the word calls attention to "bearing testimony to the sufferings." Peter was present at the arrest and in the high priest's courtyard.
- "Shepherd" (poimanate): Ellicott: "It is the verb used in John 21:16". "Exercising oversight" (episkopountes); Calvin: bishop and presbyter "are synonymous."
- "Chief Shepherd" (archipoimen), unfading crown (amarantinon; compare 1:4 inheritance "unfading").
- 5:5 "Clothe yourselves" - Ellicott: a cape named from this verb "was worn by slaves, and by no others"; "St. Peter bids them all gird themselves for one another in a slave's 'tie-up' of humility." Quotes Proverbs 3:34.
- 5:7 Ellicott: "An adaptation of Psalm 55:22, according to the LXX."
- Polycarp (c. 110) echoes 1 Peter 5:5 in his letter to the Philippians.

## 3. INTERPRETIVE QUESTIONS

Lamentations 3
- Who is "the man"? Jeremiah (Gill; Jarchi via Gill: his affliction greater than other prophets who did not see it) vs. the nation/church personified (Calvin; Henry). Carson (Aug 26): "IT IS DIFFICULT TO DECIDE whether the first part of Lamentations 3 describes the experience of an individual (perhaps Jeremiah), or if the individual is a figure representing the entire nation"; 3:14 favors individual; the "we" of the second half favors the nation; "more important than deciding this issue is the striking way in which hope or confidence twice break out." Host: a real man who stands for the people.
- 3:33 vs 3:37-38: how can God not afflict "from his heart" and yet good and bad come from his mouth? Calvin's judge; Ligonier's necessary wrath vs free love; Henry: "He comes out of his place to punish, for his place is the mercy-seat."
- 3:22 textual question (above).
- Imprecation 3:64-66: Carson: "must not be reduced to bitter vengeance. If God is just, then in the same way that he has chastened his own covenant people, he must mete out justice to those who have cruelly attacked others" (Isaiah 10:5).
Song 8:1-7
- Why wish he were a brother? Public affection between siblings acceptable (Ellicott: "none would despise me"; Henry: a more open profession).
- What is love's "jealousy"? Not suspicion but exclusive ardor (Ellicott); Henry applies it to holy jealousy.
- Flame of Yah: divine name or superlative? Ellicott: adverbial superlative, but the only sacred name in the book.
John 7:14-24
- Does 7:17 make knowledge depend on moral will? Augustine: believe in order to understand; Calvin: "sound judgment flows from fear and reverence for God."
- How can "my teaching" be "not mine"? Augustine: "My doctrine is not mine, meaning the same thing as if He said, I am not from myself."
- Sproul: did the human Jesus learn? Yes, in his human nature (Luke 2:52).
1 Peter 5:1-7
- Who are "elders"? Office (5:1) vs age (5:5); Calvin: shift from office to age in 5:5.
- Primacy? Calvin: Peter claims no primacy, only "fellow elder."
- Humility and anxiety: why does 5:7 follow 5:6? Calvin: fear that humility will get us trampled is answered by providence; Spurgeon: humbling cuts off the cares we should not have.
