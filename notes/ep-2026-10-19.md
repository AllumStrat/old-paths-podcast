## 1. SETTING AND BACKGROUND

### Jeremiah 35-38 (deepest)
- Where the story stands: Jeremiah 34 ends (34:21-22) with Zedekiah and his officials handed over to "the army of the king of Babylon which has withdrawn from you," and the Lord promising to bring that army back to burn the city. That withdrawal is the same lull described in 37:5 and 37:11 (Pharaoh's army approaching).
- Order of the book: chapters 35 and 36 jump back from Zedekiah to Jehoiakim. Calvin (Lecture 139, on 35:1-7) says the prophecies were collected "without regard to the order of time," and that Jeremiah's discourses were summarized and later formed into a volume. Placement creates a pair: chapter 35, a family that obeys the word of a dead ancestor; chapter 36, a king who burns the word of the living God. Then 37-38 return to Zedekiah under siege. The two kings bracket the same refusal: neither will listen.
- Key verb across all four chapters: shama (Strong's 8085), "hear, listen, obey" (NASB glosses include heard, listen, obey). 35:8, 10, 14, 16, 18 (obeyed / not listened); 36:25 (he would not listen); 37:2 (neither he nor his servants listened); 37:14 (Irijah would not listen); 38:15 (you will not listen to me); 38:20 (Obey now the voice of the Lord).
- Jeremiah 35 date: "in the days of Jehoiakim" (35:1), after Nebuchadnezzar came up against the land with Chaldeans and Syrians (35:11), most naturally the raiding bands of 2 Kings 24:2 near the end of Jehoiakim's reign (about 599-598 B.C.). Henry also places it late in Jehoiakim's reign. Jonadab son of Rechab rode with Jehu in 2 Kings 10:15-16 (Henry; Calvin). Rechabites were Kenites (1 Chronicles 2:55; Henry; Calvin derives them from Jethro's line).
- Hebrew "rising early" in 35:14-15: hashkem, from shakam (Strong's 7925), "rise early," a denominative from shekem, "shoulder" (to load the shoulder for the day's work). Interlinear for 35:14 shows "hashkem ... rising early."
- 35:17: Calvin notes two prepositions, God speaking "to" them (el) and "against" them (al): when men refuse the word of teaching they meet the word of judgment.
- Jeremiah 36 date: "fourth year of Jehoiakim" = 605 B.C., the year of Carchemish (Jeremiah 46:2) and the first year of Nebuchadnezzar (Jeremiah 25:1). Ligonier Tabletalk devotional dates it 605-604 and Jeremiah's call to 627 B.C. The scroll covers "from the days of Josiah until today" (36:2), so about twenty-two years of preaching.
- The public reading: "fifth year ... ninth month" (36:9), Kislev, roughly December 604. Background: the Babylonian Chronicle records Nebuchadnezzar's capture of Ashkelon; BAS endnote cites Stager, "Ashkelon and the Archaeology of Destruction: Kislev 604 B.C.E.," and the 1992 confirmation by curator I. Finkel that "the city referred to is indeed Ashkelon." The fast in Jerusalem fell in the same month that a neighboring coastal city was destroyed. (Linking the fast to Ashkelon is an inference; present it as timing, not as a documented cause.)
- Gemariah son of Shaphan (36:10-12, 25). COJS (citing Tsvi Schneider, BAR 1991): a bulla reading "Belonging to Gemaryahu / son of Shaphan," excavated by Yigal Shiloh with 50 others in the City of David, from a stratum shortly before 587/586; identified with the Gemariah of Jeremiah 36:10. Shaphan was Josiah's secretary who read the found law book to Josiah (2 Kings 22:8-11), and Josiah tore his clothes. Henry makes the Josiah contrast explicit.
- The winter house (36:22): a brazier, not a chimney hearth (Calvin's editor, note 106: "There were no chimneys, and therefore no 'hearths' in the East"). The knife: ta'ar hassopher, "the knife of the scribe" (interlinear 36:23), the tool for trimming pens and cutting scroll sheets.
- Wordplay in 36:23-24 (Calvin, Lecture 143, on 36:24): qara with aleph, "to read" (Strong's 7121), and qara with ayin, "to tear/cut" (Strong's 7167). Interlinear: 36:23 yiqra'eha, "he cut it" (7167); 36:24 lo qare'u, "did not tear" their garments (7167). Jehudi read; the king tore; nobody tore his robe.
- 36:26 "but the Lord hid them." Calvin: our life is in God's hand; "he hides and conceals us."
- 36:30 difficulty: "He shall have none to sit on the throne of David," yet Jehoiachin (Coniah) reigned three months (2 Kings 24:8). Gill and Poole answer. Body cast out: compare Jeremiah 22:18-19; 2 Kings 24:6 records only that he "slept with his fathers"; Poole: "We no where read of the time or manner of this king's death."
- Jeremiah 37-38 date: Zedekiah, installed by Nebuchadnezzar in 597 (37:1). Siege begun in his ninth year, tenth month (Jeremiah 39:1; 52:4), early 588. Egyptian relief army (37:5; Pharaoh Hophra named in 44:30) causes a temporary lift. Begg dates chapters 36-38 "probably ... right around 588 BC, perhaps into the early months of 587 BC" (his dating applies to 37-38; 36 is earlier). City falls 586 (Jeremiah 39).
- 37:12: Jeremiah heads to Benjamin "to receive his portion there," most naturally the family land of chapter 32 (the field at Anathoth). Arrested at the Benjamin Gate by Irijah.
- 37:15-16: house of Jonathan the secretary made a prison; "dungeon cells" (vaulted cells). 37:21: daily loaf "from the bakers' street until all the bread of the city was gone."
- 38:1 officials. BAS (Bible History Daily, "Jeremiah, Prophet of the Bible, Brought Back to Life"): Eilat Mazar's City of David excavations produced two bullae: "Yehuchal [or Jehucal] ben Shelemyahu [Shelemiah]" from what may be David's palace, and "Gedalyahu [Gedaliah] ben Pashur," found a few yards away under the tower identified as Nehemiah's. Both men named in Jeremiah 38:1 (Jehucal also 37:3).
- 38:4 "weakening the hands." Tabletalk (Joshua Van Ee, Westminster Seminary California): Lachish letters found 1935 and 1938 in the destruction layer at Lachish (588-586 B.C.); in Letter 6 someone's words "are not good, to slacken hands [i.e., to discourage]," "the same wording" as the charge against Jeremiah in 38:4. Letter 4: "we cannot see Azekah" (compare Jeremiah 34:7).
- 38:6 cistern: bor (Strong's 953), "pit, cistern"; "no water ... only mud (tit), and Jeremiah sank (taba)." Calvin: "He lay fixed in the mud." Henry cites Josephus: sunk up to the neck. Lamentations 3:55: "I called on your name, O Lord, from the depths of the pit" (Henry links it).
- Literary echo: 38:22, the women's taunt to Zedekiah, "now that your feet are sunk (hatbe'u, taba, Strong's 2883) in the mud (botz)." Same verb as 38:6. The prophet sank in the king's cistern; the king's feet will sink in the mud of his own choices. Calvin reads 38:22 as metaphor for Zedekiah's torpor.
- Ebed-melech: "the Ethiopian," Hebrew Cushite (Nubia/upper Nile); "eunuch" (saris), which can mean court official (Calvin, Begg). Name means "servant of the king" (Begg). Begg: cisterns about three feet wide at the mouth, bulbous below.
- 38:7 "the king was sitting in the Benjamin Gate," the place of judgment (Calvin, Begg).
- Ebed-melech's reward follows in 39:15-18 (tomorrow's reading).

### Ecclesiastes 11
- Traditional view: the Preacher (Qoheleth) is Solomon, son of David, king in Jerusalem (1:1, 12), writing late in life. Chapter 11 opens the book's final movement: 11:1-6 on acting under uncertainty; 11:7-12:8 the poem of youth, age and death, closing with "vanity of vanities" (12:8), then the epilogue (12:9-14).
- 11:1-6 has four "you do not know" lines in the ESV (11:2; 11:5 twice; 11:6). Ignorance of outcomes is the reason to act, not to stall.
- 11:1 "Cast your bread upon the waters": charity (Gregory Thaumaturgus; Henry) or sea-trade venture (some modern readers). Henry blends them: "Send it a voyage, send it as a venture, as merchants that trade by sea."
- 11:2 "seven, or even to eight": numerical idiom, x and x+1, "as many as you can and then one more." Ambrose and others read it allegorically (sabbath and resurrection); not verified in a fetched primary text, so not quoted.
- 11:5 ESV footnote: some manuscripts read "the way of the spirit"; most read "the way of the wind, or how the bones grow in the womb." Hebrew ruach means both wind and spirit, tying 11:4 (wind) to 11:5.
- 11:8, 10 hevel (Strong's 1892), "vapor, breath" (ESV footnote: "a 'vapor' or 'mere breath'").
- 11:10 "pain" or "evil" (ESV footnote).

### John 4:43-54
- After two days in Samaria (4:40, 43), Jesus goes to Galilee. Cana to Cana: first sign at Cana (2:1-11), "second sign" at Cana (4:54). Chapters 2-4 framed by Cana.
- 4:44 puzzle: "a prophet has no honor in his own hometown," then "the Galileans welcomed him." Augustine (Tractate 16): homeland = Galilee; the Samaritans believed without signs while Galileans needed them. Chrysostom (Homily 35): homeland = Capernaum. Calvin rejects both ("excess of subtlety" in Augustine; not satisfied with Chrysostom) and follows Cyril: he left Nazareth for another part of Galilee. Sproul: read the welcome with irony; they welcomed a wonder-worker, not the Messiah.
- 4:46 "official," basilikos, a royal official; Calvin thinks a nobleman of Herod's court. Chrysostom: not the centurion of Matthew 8 (different faith; one says "I am not worthy," this one says "come down").
- Capernaum is on the lake, below Cana in the hills; "come down" (4:47, 49, 51). Distance roughly twenty miles (common estimate; spoken loosely as "most of a day's walk").
- 4:48 ESV note: "you" is plural, twice. Calvin: "a general reproof of a whole people."
- 4:52 "seventh hour" = 1 p.m. (ESV note). He reaches home the next day ("Yesterday at the seventh hour").
- 4:50 "believed the word," 4:53 "he himself believed, and all his household": stages of faith (Calvin, Spurgeon, Sproul).

### 1 Peter 1:10-16
- Peter writes to "elect exiles of the dispersion" in Asia Minor (1:1), from "Babylon" (5:13, traditionally Rome), early 60s A.D., before Nero's persecution became general (traditional dating; Henry heads the section "A.D. 66").
- Argument: 1:3-9 praise for salvation (living hope, kept inheritance, tested faith; covered in episode 1); 1:10-12 that salvation was the object of prophetic search and angelic interest; 1:13 "Therefore" introduces the letter's first imperative, "set your hope fully"; 1:14-16 holiness, quoting Leviticus (11:44-45; 19:2; 20:7).
- 1:11 "the Spirit of Christ in them": the preincarnate Christ's Spirit spoke through the prophets (Henry, Calvin). "Sufferings ... and the subsequent glories" (plural).
- 1:12 "long to look": parakupto (Strong's 3879), "stoop and look," used of the disciples peering into the tomb (John 20:5, 11; Luke 24:12) and in James 1:25.
- 1:13 Greek "girding up the loins of your mind" (ESV footnote); main verb elpisate, "hope" (Strong's 1679), with teleios, "fully"; nepho, "be sober" (Strong's 3525).
- 1:15-16 hagios (Strong's 40), "holy."
- 1:14 "obedient children," literally "children of obedience" (interlinear: teknon hupakoes); hupakoe (Strong's 5218) is from hupakouo, built on akouo, "to hear" (Bible Hub: "submission to what is heard"). Links to the shama theme in Jeremiah.

## 3. INTERPRETIVE QUESTIONS

### Jeremiah
1. Why does God commend the Rechabites' obedience to a merely human rule, and what does "stand before me" (35:19) promise?
   - Calvin: the counsel of Jonadab is not what is praised but the sons' obedience (Q02); the point is a "threefold comparison" (Q62): God vs a mortal man, God's constant speaking vs Jonadab's single command, God's easy law vs Jonadab's hard rules. "Stand before me" does not mean priests, as some rabbis held; it means Jonadab's line will always live, by God's special favor (Q04).
   - Gill: Targum "ministering before me"; worship in their families, not temple office; rabbinic tradition that Rechabite daughters married priests; Hegesippus's Rechabite priest at James's death. Gill: "these testimonies are not to be depended on," but the promise will not fail (Q07).
   - Poole: whether reward implies duty: God may reward reverence even in matters left free; parents cannot bind conscience in religion.
   - Jerome (Letter 58): the Rechabites are forerunners of the monastic life (Q05). Calvin explicitly rejects using the Rechabites to justify binding church rules on consciences (Q02 context). Tension worth naming.
   - Eusebius quoting Hegesippus (Q06): at the stoning of James the Just, "one of the priests of the sons of Rechab" cried out to stop it.
2. Jehoiakim "shall have none to sit on the throne of David" (36:30), yet Jehoiachin reigned. Contradiction?
   - Gill (Q14): three months "reckoned as nothing," never confirmed by Babylon, carried captive; Zedekiah was not Jehoiakim's successor in line.
   - Poole: "none that shall be king any considerable time"; and no record of how Jehoiakim died, but he had an ignominious burial (Q15; Jeremiah 22:19).
   - Henry: within weeks his son dethroned.
3. Did Jeremiah lie to the officials (38:24-27)?
   - Calvin (Q25): "It was not indeed a falsehood, but this evasion cannot be wholly excused"; he did go to pray for his life, but he was sent for; "he ought rather to have hearkened to God's word, in which simplicity is enjoined"; even God's servants "have sometimes spoken evasively, when oppressed with extreme fear."
   - Henry (Q26): "no lie or equivocation, but a part of the truth"; wise as serpents, harmless as doves.
   - Poole (Q27): not bound in all cases to speak the whole truth, "much less to those who have nothing to do to inquire of us."
   - Gill (Q28): "what he told them, no doubt, was truth; though he did not tell them all the truth."
   - Westminster Larger Catechism 144-145: duty to speak truth "in matters of judgment and justice"; sins include "concealing the truth, undue silence in a just cause" (Q60) but also speaking the truth unseasonably. Host's view: Jeremiah said nothing false, the officials had no right to the conversation, and the text reports without commending; Calvin's caution is still worth hearing.
4. Why is Ebed-melech a foreigner? Calvin (Q23): not one Judean counselor resisted; "there was one found, an Ethiopian." Henry (Q24): "more humanity, and more divinity too." Begg (Q22): ropes and rags, firmness and compassion. Calvin and Begg both reach for the Good Samaritan; the script will not, to keep the segment in Jeremiah.

### Ecclesiastes
1. Is 11:1 about charity or trade? Gregory Thaumaturgus (Q32) and Henry: charity; Henry's "venture" language (Q34) shows the commercial picture serves the charitable point.
2. Is 11:9 ironic or sincere?
   - Irony: Henry (Q36), Poole (Q38, "ironical concession," citing Numbers 15:39 for "walk in the ways of your heart ... eyes" as always bad); Gregory Thaumaturgus puts 11:9a in the mouth of a hedonist and answers him (Q33).
   - Sincere: Gill (Q37), "serious," mirth allowable "under due limitations," commended throughout the book.
   - Host's view: sincere. Ecclesiastes repeatedly commends joy as God's gift (2:24-25; 3:12-13; 5:18-20; 8:15; 9:7-9). "But know" does not cancel the joy; it frames it. Gregory's warning lands on how youth uses the joy, not on joy itself.
3. What is the logic of 11:3-6? Fixed things (rain, fallen trees) you cannot change; unknown things (wind, womb, God's work) you cannot know; so act. Spurgeon (Q39-Q41), Henry (Q35).

### John
1. What does 4:44 mean in context? Augustine, Chrysostom, Calvin (Q46), Sproul (irony). Host: Calvin's caution is fair; Sproul's point fits John's narrative, a welcome that is not honor.
2. Was the official's first request faith? Chrysostom: "the very coming and beseeching Him was a mark of faith," but imperfect; Augustine: he had not yet believed; Calvin: some taste of faith, then a "particular faith," then full discipleship. Spurgeon (Q49, Q61): spark, smoulder, flame, conflagration.
3. Why the rebuke (4:48)? Plural "you"; Calvin: general reproof; Chrysostom (Q44): he heals the father "sick in mind, no less than the son."

### 1 Peter
1. Did the prophets possess the salvation they searched for? Calvin (Q51): the passage was "strangely perverted by fanatics" to exclude Old Testament believers; they "partook of Christ as the real food of their souls," but did not see him manifested; they "spread the table" for us (Q50). Westminster Confession 7.5 (Q59): Old Testament ordinances "sufficient and efficacious ... to instruct and build up the elect in faith in the promised Messiah, by whom they had full remission of sins, and eternal salvation."
2. What is "the Spirit of Christ" in the prophets? Henry (Q54): proves Christ's preexistence; Calvin: "the ancient prophecies were dictated by Christ." Clement of Alexandria (Q56) oddly reads "spirits of Christ" as angels. Note the disagreement.
3. How does hope relate to holiness? Piper (Q57, Q58): the first command is hope in grace; holiness follows. Henry (Q55): imitate, never equal.
