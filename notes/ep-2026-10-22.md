## 1. SETTING AND BACKGROUND

### Jeremiah 51
- Second half of the Babylon oracle (50:1-51:58) plus the Seraiah epilogue (51:59-64). Final verse: "Thus far are the words of Jeremiah." Chapter 52 is a historical appendix that parallels 2 Kings 24:18-25:30 (Calvin: "the last chapter is not included in the prophetic book of Jeremiah, but ... contains history only"; Ellicott: "an editorial note by the compiler of Jeremiah's prophecies, Baruch or another").
- Date: 51:59 "in the fourth year of his reign" (Zedekiah), 594/593 B.C. Calvin: "seven years before the city was taken." Gill: "sixty years before the taking of Babylon." Same year as Jeremiah 27-28: envoys from Edom, Moab, Ammon, Tyre, Sidon (27:3); Hananiah in "the fourth year" (28:1) predicts the yoke broken "within two years" (28:3, 11). Jeremiah's letter to the exiles (29) says seek the welfare of the city (29:7) and seventy years (29:10).
- Did Zedekiah go to Babylon? ESV "went with Zedekiah". Calvin: Jewish story "wholly groundless"; particle can mean "for, or by, or in the place of Zedekiah." Gill and Poole: no record of such a journey; LXX "from Zedekiah", Targum "on an embassy of Zedekiah". Ellicott: probably Nebuchadnezzar summoned the king after the conspiracy of ch 27 to demand renewed homage. Host: either way, Seraiah is in the delegation.
- Seraiah son of Neriah son of Mahseiah = brother of Baruch son of Neriah son of Mahseiah (32:12). Ellicott: "The mention of both father and grandfather leaves no doubt that Seraiah was the brother of Jeremiah's friend and secretary, Baruch." Seal: Avigad identified a seal inscribed to Seriahu / Neriyahu as Seraiah son of Neriah; private collection (Biblical Archaeology Society image caption, https://library.biblicalarchaeology.org/images/bsba170403000ljpg/ via search; Wikipedia Seraiah ben Neriah). Unprovenanced; authenticity of related Baruch bullae disputed. Mention with caution.
- sar menuchah (51:59): KJV "a quiet prince" (from Luther); ESV "quartermaster". Keil and Delitzsch: "prince of the resting-place" (cf. Numbers 10:33), "quartermaster-general" who fixed the halting-places. Ellicott: "describes an office like that of our quartermaster-general." LXX "ruler of the gifts", Vulgate "prince of prophecy" (Ellicott; Calvin's editor). Calvin: "a prince of a quiet disposition", meekness, willing to obey a despised prophet at risk to his mission. Henry: "a prince of rest... of a calm temper." Poole: "the best interpreters see no reason to vary from our translation." Calvin's editor: 1 Chronicles 22:9 Solomon "a man of rest".
- Babylon's fall, October 539 B.C. (covered yesterday: Cyrus Cylinder, Nabonidus Chronicle). Today: the feast (51:39, 57) with Daniel 5; Herodotus 1.191 says the city was taken during a festival after Cyrus diverted the Euphrates (Ellicott on 51:39; Calvin cites Xenophon; Gill cites "Herodotus and Xenophon"). 51:36 "I will dry up her sea and make her fountain dry"; 51:32 "the fords have been seized". Calvin on 51:44: walls not pulled down in 539 but under Darius son of Hystaspes after revolt (Herodotus).
- Bel = Marduk's title (yesterday). 51:44 swallowed: Gill: the temple vessels deposited there (2 Chronicles 36:7) and "restored by Cyrus, Ezra 1:7". Keil and Delitzsch: the sacred vessels (Daniel 1) and the nations themselves.
- 51:1 Leb-kamai: "heart of my adversaries" (ESV footnote/Ellicott: "Literally, in the heart of my adversaries"), most commentators: atbash cipher for Kasdim (Chaldea), like Sheshach (25:26; 51:41).
- 51:25 "destroying mountain": Ellicott: "the phrase is the same as that which is applied in 2Kings 23:13 to the Mount of Olives ... 'the Mount of Corruption'", Josiah's reform, Ashtoreth, Chemosh, Milcom. Babylon was brick, not stone (Ellicott on 51:26), and is pictured as a volcano that burns out.
- 51:15-19 repeats 10:12-16 nearly word for word (Henry: "repeated to show that God will convince those by his judgments who would not be convinced by his word").
- 51:27 Ararat (Urartu), Minni (Mannaeans), Ashkenaz (Scythians): northern peoples under the Medes (Henry: Armenia, Ascania).
- Ezra 1:7-11 vessels returned (5,400); Ezra 2:64 the whole assembly 42,360, a small portion (Calvin on 51:45).

### Song of Songs 4:8-16
- Continues the wedding scene (3:6-11 procession; 4:1-7 wasf). 4:8 first use of kallah, "bride" (six times in 4:8-5:1). "My sister, my bride" only in 4:9-5:1 (Cambridge); Egyptian love songs call lovers "my sister" and "my brother" (Cambridge citing Budde).
- 4:8 Lebanon, Amana (Anti-Lebanon), Senir and Hermon: far north, wild, lions and leopards. Cambridge: emphatic "With me from Lebanon". Henry concise: "Christ's call to his church to come off from the world."
- 4:9 one Hebrew word libbavtini, from lebab "heart": "you have heartened me" or "you have taken my heart" (Cambridge prefers the latter; Keil and Delitzsch: love disarms). Gill: Vulgate "thou hast wounded my heart".
- 4:12 garden locked, spring locked, fountain sealed: Cambridge: Proverbs 5:15-18; Delitzsch: seal used of maiden-like behaviour. Gill: allusion to the king's garden near Jerusalem.
- 4:13 "orchard" is pardes, a Persian-derived word, the source of "paradise" (also Ecclesiastes 2:5; Nehemiah 2:8).
- 4:16 speaker: checked the fetched ESV text: the heading "Together in the Garden of Love / She" stands before "Let my beloved come", so the ESV gives 4:16a ("Awake, O north wind ... Blow upon my garden") to HIM and 4:16b to her. Sibbes agrees: words of Christ "because he calls it 'my garden'". Spurgeon reads the whole verse as the spouse's prayer. On the ESV/Sibbes reading: he says "my garden", she answers "his garden" -> 5:1 "I came to my garden".

### John 6:25-40
- Next day after the feeding (6:22), Capernaum; the discourse is in the synagogue (6:59). Passover near (6:4): manna, Exodus 16, Psalm 78:24. 6:35 the first of the seven "I am" sayings with a predicate, following yesterday's absolute ego eimi on the water (6:20). Discourse structure: motive (26-27), work (28-29), sign and manna (30-33), bread of life (34-40); 6:39-40 "raise up on the last day" (repeated 6:44, 54).

### 1 Peter 3:8-12
- "Finally" (to telos) closes the household code begun at 2:13 ("Be subject ... to every human institution"), servants (2:18), wives and husbands (3:1-7); now "all of you". Calvin's editor: "Honour all" (2:17) is exemplified through to 3:12; 3:13 resumes suffering. Five adjectives; tapeinophrones textual variant (KJV "courteous", philophrones) (Calvin's note 40).
- 3:10-12 quotes Psalm 34:12-16 (Greek version, Calvin). Peter used Psalm 34:8 already in 2:3. Psalm 34 title: David "changed his behavior before Abimelech" (Achish, 1 Samuel 21:10-15). Ellicott: Peter's adaptation, "Who cares to have a life worth having".

## 3. INTERPRETIVE QUESTIONS

Jeremiah 51
1. 51:35 vengeance prayer. "The violence done to me ... be upon Babylon ... My blood be upon the inhabitants of Chaldea." Calvin: Jeremiah "exhorts the faithful to deposit their complaints in the bosom of God, and to apply, or appeal to him, as their defender"; "By this passage we are taught to present our complaints to God, if we wish him to undertake our cause." Henry: "every drop of Jerusalem's blood shall be accounted for with interest" (concise paraphrase line). Tension with 29:7 (pray for its welfare). Resolution: two duties, one faith: seek Babylon's peace in daily life; hand Babylon's injustice to the Judge.
2. Seraiah's office and Zedekiah's journey (above).
3. 51:20-23 hammer: past (Babylon, Calvin) or future (Cyrus; "many render this passage in the future tense... and consider Cyrus to be intended", Calvin's editor note 87).
4. Fulfillment (handled yesterday; only note the feast and the vessels today).

Song 4:8-16
1. Who speaks 4:16a? Sibbes and the ESV headings: he does; Spurgeon: the spouse. Host follows the ESV: he calls the winds on "my garden", she answers "let my beloved come to his garden"; she agrees with his claim and gives herself.
2. Locked garden: chastity before marriage and exclusivity within (Proverbs 5). Cyprian's ecclesial use: the church enclosed; applied to argue baptism outside the church invalid. Augustine and the later church rejected rebaptism; host keeps the exclusivity point (one bride) but not Cyprian's conclusion.

John 6:25-40
1. Is faith our work? Calvin: faith called a work improperly, "a passive work, to which no reward can be paid"; and he denies this verse teaches faith is God's gift (gift taught elsewhere, 6:44, 65). Augustine: "Nor did He say, This is your work; but, This is the work of God, that you believe in Him whom He has sent; so that he who glories, may glory in the Lord."
2. 6:37 sovereignty and invitation: Spurgeon, "I never reconcile friends."
3. 6:36 "you have seen me and yet do not believe": seeing signs does not produce faith.

1 Peter 3:8-12
1. What blessing do we inherit, and are "good days" in this life? Augustine: "Good days you seek, together let us seek them, seek not here." Calvin: "It is indeed a desirable thing, since God has placed us in this world, to pass our time in peace." Ellicott: "a life worth having". Host: both; Peter applies a temporal Psalm to exiles whose inheritance is "kept in heaven" (1:4).
2. Does not retaliating make one weak? Calvin: "highest magnanimity."
