## 1. SETTING AND BACKGROUND

### Ezekiel 48 (deepest)
- Date: the temple vision is dated in 40:1 to the 25th year of exile, the 14th year after the city fell, about April 573 B.C. (taught ep 29). Ezekiel was taken with Jehoiachin in 597 B.C. (Ligonier "The Holy City": "As part of the group of Judah's leading citizens that was carried off into exile along with King Jehoiachin in 597/598 BC"). Call vision 1:1-3, fifth year of Jehoiachin's exile (593 B.C.). Latest dated oracle 29:17, 27th year (571 B.C.). Nebuchadnezzar rules Babylon; Jerusalem in ruins.
- Book structure: 1-24 judgment on Jerusalem; 25-32 nations; 33-48 hope; 40-48 the vision of temple, river, land. Ch 48 completes the land distribution begun in 47:13.
- 47:13-23 (reread, the end of the previous chapter): borders (north Hethlon, Lebo-hamath; east the Jordan and the Dead Sea to Tamar; south Meribah-kadesh and the Brook of Egypt; west the Great Sea). Fairbairn: substantially the boundaries of Numbers 34, Canaan proper within Jordan, so no Transjordan tribes. 47:14 "divide equally." 47:22-23 sojourners who have children among you "shall be to you as native-born children of Israel" and receive inheritance in whatever tribe they reside.
- Ch 48 structure (Henry, Pulpit, Ellicott): 48:1-7 seven tribes north (Dan, Asher, Naphtali, Manasseh, Ephraim, Reuben, Judah); 48:8-22 the terumah (holy contribution): priests' portion with the sanctuary in the midst (8-12), Levites (13-14), city strip 5,000 wide with the city 4,500 square plus 250 open land, farmland for city workers from all tribes (15-20), the prince's land east and west (21-22); 48:23-29 five tribes south (Benjamin, Simeon, Issachar, Zebulun, Gad); 48:30-35 twelve gates, circumference 18,000, the name.
- Differences from Joshua (Pulpit list; Keil): all twelve west of Jordan; parallel east-west bands of equal size; begins in the north; Dan moved to the far north (Laish/Dan, Judges 18); Gad brought over from Gilead to the far south; Reuben brought over and placed between Ephraim and Judah; Issachar and Zebulun moved south. Ellicott: the holy oblation is "fully one-fifth of the whole"; tribal portions rather less than two-thirds of Joshua's average.
- Ordering observations: Barnes and JFB and Fairbairn: Judah and Benjamin flank the center because of loyalty to temple and David's house; JFB/Fairbairn: Dan at the extreme north for its idolatry (Judges 18), and Revelation 7 omits Dan. Keil: "In the order of the several tribe-territories it is impossible to discover any universal principle of arrangement," though he explains several. Gates (Keil via Pulpit): north gates Reuben, Judah, Levi (Leah's sons: firstborn in age, firstborn by blessing, chosen for service); east Joseph, Benjamin, Dan; south Simeon, Issachar, Zebulun; west Gad, Asher, Naphtali. Levi gets a gate; Ephraim and Manasseh become one in Joseph.
- My observation from the ESV list: the sons of the handmaids (Dan, Naphtali by Bilhah; Gad, Asher by Zilpah) all lie at the far ends of the land (Dan, Asher, Naphtali the three northernmost; Gad southernmost). Keil and Fairbairn don't state this as a principle; present it as something to notice, not a claim of the commentators.
- Size: 18,000 cubits around, about six miles (Cambridge: "some-what under six miles"); Josephus reckoned Jerusalem in his day about four miles (Cambridge, Ellicott). If reeds, about thirty-plus miles (Ellicott, Poole considers it incredible).
- Name: Yahweh shammah. Never called Jerusalem in the vision (Henry). Keil: shammah means "there" here (Ezekiel uses it so in 23:3; 32:29-30); Fairbairn: "thither" (the he cannot be sunk), God's eyes from the temple toward the city and the land. Gill: Jewish exegesis reading shemah "its name" (Bava Bathra 75b).
- Glory arc: departed via the east (10:18-19; 11:22-23); returned by the east gate (43:1-5); city named 48:35 (Cambridge: "The prophet beheld the Lord forsake his temple (11), and he beheld him again enter it (43); now he abides in it among his people for ever.").
- Hebrew terumah (Ellicott: "oblation," same word as "offering" in 48:8; Keil translates "heave," "which ye shall lift (heave) off"). From rum, to lift: a contribution lifted off from the whole for the Lord.

### Job 17
- Continuation of Job's reply to Eliphaz (16-17), round two of the dialogues. Follows 16:19-21 witness in heaven and the plea for an arbiter.
- Structure (Cambridge): 17:1-2 broken spirit and mockers; 17:3-9 new appeal for a pledge with grounds, ending in 17:9; 17:10-16 challenge to friends and the grave as house.
- 17:3 'arav: Barnes: from 'arab "to mix, mingle; to exchange, to barter and then to become surety for anyone"; Genesis 43:9; 44:32 (Judah becomes surety for Benjamin). "Strike hands": the gesture of becoming surety (Proverbs 6:1; 17:18).
- 17:6 "one before whom men spit" (Keil derives topheth from tuph, to spit).
- 17:13-16 Sheol as house, pit as father, worm as mother; "where then is my hope?"

### John 12:12-19
- The next day after the Bethany dinner (12:1, six days before Passover); the Sunday before Passover. Recorded in all four Gospels (Sproul).
- Crowd: Passover pilgrims plus those who saw Lazarus raised (12:17-18).
- Palms: Sproul: palms associated with Tabernacles; after Simon Maccabeus drove the Syrians out, he was celebrated "with the waving of palm branches"; palm became a symbol of victory, stamped on coins of the revolt. Calvin: "The palm was the emblem of victory and peace among the ancients."
- Hosanna: Psalm 118:25; Calvin: "composed of two Hebrew words, and means, Save, I beseech you." The Hallel (Psalms 113-118) sung at feasts (Sproul).
- Zechariah 9:9; John has "Fear not, daughter of Zion" where Zechariah has "Rejoice greatly" (Calvin notes the words differ).
- 12:19 "the world has gone after him"; 12:20 next: Greeks come.

### 1 John 5:1-12
- Final chapter; argument returns to faith, love, obedience tests and climaxes in the testimony and life in the Son. Cerinthian background (Expositor's Greek Testament, Pulpit on bh 1 John 5:6; Irenaeus Against Heresies 1.26.1).
- 5:7-8 KJV addition ("three that bear record in heaven"): not in ESV; Calvin inclined to accept it; his Calvin Translation Society editor: "not found in any of the Greek MSS prior to the 16th century."

## 3. INTERPRETIVE QUESTIONS
Ezekiel 48:
1. Literal or symbolic land? Fairbairn: same reason to hold temple worship literal as land literal; arbitrariness to split them; 47:22 sojourners inexplicable on literal reading. Henry: uncertainty of measures "an intimation that these things are to be understood spiritually." Poole: if reeds, "cannot be conceived credible." Gill: the church. Ligonier: metaphors for the full number of God's people. Spurgeon (No. 2182) reads it of a literal millennial restoration, but says he does not understand much of Ezekiel and seeks practical edification. Host: symbolic picture, consistent with ep 29.
2. Why this order? Henry (Dan first, last first; Reuben's reproach forgotten; Zadok faithfulness), Fairbairn/JFB (Dan lowest place; Judah and Benjamin honored), Keil (no universal principle).
3. "There" or "thither"? Keil vs Fairbairn.
Job 17:
1. Whom does Job ask for a pledge? God (Ellicott, Barnes, Cambridge, Keil, Pulpit) vs Poole's two options; Gill and Henry's annotations: Christ the surety. Gregory's Vulgate reads differently ("Set me free, and put me beside thee") and takes it as the voice of Christ.
2. Is 17:9 Job speaking of himself? Barnes: yes, proverbial; Pulpit: righteous men generally; Cambridge: his own resolution, "perhaps the most surprising and lofty in the Book."
John 12:12-19:
1. Two animals (Matthew) or one? Calvin: synecdoche, one ridden; rejects allegory of ass = Jews, colt = Gentiles, which Augustine adopts (colt = Gentiles, ass = Israel). Chrysostom: both happened.
2. Why "Fear not" instead of "Rejoice"? Calvin.
1 John 5:1-12:
1. "Loves whoever has been born of him" = Christ (Augustine) or believers (Calvin, explicitly against Augustine).
2. Water and blood: baptism and death (Pulpit, JFB, Expositor's vs Cerinthus) vs pierced side John 19:34 (Calvin, Benson), vs sacraments (some in Henry).
3. Not burdensome vs experience: Calvin.
