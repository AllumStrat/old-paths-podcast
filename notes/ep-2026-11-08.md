## 1. SETTING AND BACKGROUND

### Ezekiel 27-28 (deepest)
- Date: 26:1 dates the Tyre oracles to "the eleventh year, on the first day of the month" (month missing in the Hebrew), i.e., 587/586 B.C., the year Jerusalem fell. Chapters 27 and 28 carry no new date and continue the Tyre cycle (26:1-28:19), followed by Sidon (28:20-23) and a promise to Israel (28:24-26). The oracles against the nations run 25-32, placed between the siege's start (24:1-2) and the fugitive's arrival (33:21-22), during Ezekiel's muteness toward Israel.
- King of Tyre: Henry (Ezekiel 28): "His name was Ethbaal, or Ithobalus, as Diodorus Siculus calls him that was king of Tyre when Nebuchadnezzar destroyed it." Gill on 28:2: "Whose name was Ethbaal, or Ithobalus, as he is called in Josephus" and rejects the rabbinic fable that this was Hiram. Carson: "Historically, the king in question was Ithobal II." (Modern numbering often Ithobaal III; avoid the number on air.) Josephus via Keil: "when Ithobal was on the throne, Nebuchadnezzar besieged Tyre for thirteen years."
- Tyre: island city about half a mile offshore (Gill on 27:3); twin harbors, the Sidonian (north) and Egyptian (south) (Keil/JFB on 27:3 "entrances to the sea" plural). Tyrian purple dye (Henry).
- Structure: 27:1-11 the ship built and crewed; 27:12-25 the trade catalogue (nations list, compare Genesis 10, Henry); 27:26-36 the wreck and the mourning sailors. 28:1-10 oracle against the prince (nagid): self-deification; 28:11-19 lament (qinah) over the king (melek); 28:20-23 Sidon; 28:24-26 Israel gathered.
- Ch 27 is a qinah, funeral song, sung over a city still standing (Henry: "It was yet in the height of its prosperity, and there appeared not the least symptom of its decay; yet the prophet must lament it").
- Place names: Senir = Hermon (Deuteronomy 3:9); Bashan oaks; Cyprus (Kittim); Elishah (perhaps Cyprus/Carthage); Arvad island city north; Gebal = Byblos (caulkers); Persia, Lud (Lydia), Put (Libya) mercenaries; Tarshish (Spain, Tartessos) silver iron tin lead; Javan (Ionian Greeks), Tubal, Meshech (Anatolia) traded "human beings" (slaves: Barnes on 27:13 "They were famous for dealing in slaves"; Joel 3:4-6 accuses Tyre and Sidon of selling people of Judah to the Greeks; Amos 1:9); Beth-togarmah horses; Dedan; Syria (Edom in some MSS); Judah and Israel wheat of Minnith (Ammonite town, Judges 11:33), honey, oil, balm (Gilead balm, Jeremiah 8:22); Damascus wine of Helbon; Arabia, Kedar; Sheba, Raamah spices and gold; Haran, Canneh, Eden, Asshur, Chilmad (Mesopotamia).
- Archaeology: Mauro (2014) reports that two small Phoenician merchant ships were located in 1997 by the US Navy nuclear research submarine NR-1 off the coast of Israel, sitting upright on the sea floor at more than 400 m; in 1999 Robert D. Ballard and L. E. Stager (Harvard) surveyed them with a remotely operated vehicle; the cargo dated them to the eighth century B.C.; the holds held hundreds of amphorae (385 Tanit, 396 Elissa) still in position; residue analysis (McGovern, Penn) indicated wine. Source: https://www.ancientportsantiques.com/wp-content/uploads/Documents/AUTHORS/Mauro/Mauro2014-PhoenicianShipwrecks.pdf (citing Ballard et al., "Iron Age Shipwrecks in Deep Water off Ashkelon, Israel," AJA 106/2, 2002). BAR attributions of Tyre as port of departure could not be fetched (host blocked), so do not say they sailed from Tyre; say Phoenician.
- Sennacherib relief of a Phoenician ship (Nineveh, ca. 700 BCE, British Museum) - Livius page; not used.
- Key Hebrew: kelilat yofi "perfect in beauty" (27:3, 4, 11; 28:12 "perfect in beauty" kelil yofi); compare Lamentations 2:15 "the perfection of beauty" of Jerusalem and Psalm 50:2 "Out of Zion, the perfection of beauty" (miklal yofi) - Henry notes Psalm 50:2. "In the heart of the seas" (belev yammim) 27:4, 25, 26, 27; 28:2, 8. Chotam toknit (28:12): Keil "Thou seal of a well-measured building"; toknit elsewhere only 43:10 of the temple plan (Keil, Ellicott, Cambridge). Keruv mimshach hassokek (28:14): "anointed guardian cherub"; Keil: sokek participle pointing back to Exodus 25:20 "the cherubim covered the capporeth with their wings"; ESV footnote 28:16 "Or banished you"; LXX/Origen read "with the cherub" (Origen: "created along with the cherubim"; "a cherub drove you forth"). ESV footnotes: 28:12, 13, 14 "meaning of the Hebrew phrase is uncertain."
- Nine stones of 28:13 are all among the twelve of the high priest's breastplate (Exodus 28:17-20) (Barnes; Henry; Benson), LXX expands to twelve (Benson); Keil denies the breastplate allusion.

### Job 9:21-35
- Job's first reply to Bildad (9-10). 9:1-20 courtroom (tsadaq, riv) covered last time. 21-24: "I am blameless; I regard not myself" (Keil: "I know not myself"), "It is all one... He destroys both the blameless and the wicked" (Pulpit compares Ecclesiastes 9:2), "he mocks at the calamity of the innocent," "The earth is given into the hand of the wicked... if it is not he, who then is it?" 25-26: days swifter than a runner (courier), skiffs of reed (papyrus boats, Isaiah 18:2; Cambridge), eagle swooping. 27-31: if I say I'll forget my complaint... I become afraid; wash with snow, lye (bor, vegetable alkali, Keil), you will plunge me into a pit, my clothes abhor me. 32-35: "not a man as I am... no arbiter (mokiach) between us who might lay his hand on us both"; ESV footnote "Or Would that there were an arbiter"; LXX "O that there were a mediator" (mesites) (Barnes, Henry). Rod (shevet), dread.
- Mokiach from yakach (Barnes): to argue, prove, decide; Genesis 31:37 (Jacob and Laban, "let them decide between us two"); Isaiah 11:3.

### John 9:26-41
- Setting: autumn, after Feast of Booths (7:2), Jerusalem; same day sequence: healing (9:1-12), interrogation (13-25), second interrogation and expulsion (26-34), Jesus finds him (35-38), judgment saying to Pharisees (39-41), continuing without break into 10:1 ("Truly, truly, I say to you" to the same Pharisees).
- 9:22 aposunagogos "put out of the synagogue" (also 12:42; 16:2).
- "Son of Man" ESV; footnote "Some manuscripts the Son of God" (Calvin's text). Son of Man ties to judgment (John 5:27) and 9:39.
- proskuneo "worshiped" (9:38) - in John used of worship of God (4:20-24).

### 1 John 2:1-11
- Author John, Ephesus, late first century (introduced in ep 21). Structure: 2:1-2 advocate and propitiation answering 1:8-10; 2:3-6 test of obedience ("whoever says"); 2:7-11 old/new commandment, light and darkness, love/hate of brother. "Whoever says" continues the "if we say" pattern of 1:6, 8, 10.
- teknia "little children"; parakletos "advocate" (one called alongside; also John 14:16, 26; 15:26; 16:7 of the Spirit, "another Helper"); dikaios "righteous"; hilasmos "propitiation" (also 4:10; related hilasterion Romans 3:25, Hebrews 9:5 mercy seat; Owen).
- 2:8 "the darkness is passing away and the true light is already shining" (John 1:9; 8:12; 9:5).

## 3. INTERPRETIVE QUESTIONS
Ezekiel:
1. Is 28:11-19 about Satan? (see outline; voices below).
2. Why lament a city God is judging? Henry: "We ought to mourn for the miseries of other nations... it is a part of the honour we owe to all men to bewail their calamities, even those which they have brought upon themselves by their own folly."
3. What was Tyre's sin? Not chiefly idolatry; commercial pride, violence in trade (28:16, 18), slave trading (27:13; Joel 3:6), gloating (26:2). Carson 28:5.
Job:
1. Is 9:22 blasphemy or truth? Pulpit: in a certain sense true (Ecclesiastes 9:2) but ignores forgiveness and future life; Henry: complaint of God not to be excused, though God considers his frame.
2. Is 9:33 a prophecy of Christ? Cambridge: no prophecy of the incarnation, a cry of the heart; Gregory and Spurgeon: Job calls for the Mediator. Host: not prediction but the right question.
John:
1. "God does not listen to sinners": Calvin (true, sinner = ungodly) vs Augustine (God hears even sinners: the publican). Resolution: the man means habitual God-defiers whom God would not empower for a sign; Augustine guards against using it to bar repentant sinners from prayer. Both right in their sense.
2. Did he worship Jesus as God? Calvin cautious; Chrysostom: declaring His divine power.
3. 9:39 "those who see may become blind": Augustine (those who think they see), Calvin (accidental result; light dazzles diseased eyes), Spurgeon 1798.
1 John:
1. "Whole world": Calvin, Augustine, Owen, Ligonier.
2. "So that you may not sin" vs grace as license: Calvin, Spurgeon 515.
3. Old and new commandment: Calvin (renewed daily), Augustine (darkness to old man, light to new man).
