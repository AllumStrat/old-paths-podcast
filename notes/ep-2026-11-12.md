## 1. Setting and background

### Ezekiel 35-37 (deepest)
- **Date.** 33:21 dates the fugitive's arrival to the 12th year, 10th month, 5th day of the exile, about January 585 B.C. (taught ep. 26). 33:23-39:29 are undated; they belong to the period after the news. Henry heads both 35 and 36 "(b. c. 587.)" in his section titles, reflecting the fall of the city.
- **Book structure.** 1-24 judgment on Jerusalem; 25-32 nations; 33-48 hope. 33 = hinge; 34 shepherds; 35:1-36:15 one paired oracle (Mount Seir vs mountains of Israel; 35:2 "set your face against Mount Seir"; 36:1 "prophesy to the mountains of Israel"); 36:16-38 new heart for God's name's sake; 37 dry bones + two sticks; 38-39 Gog; 40-48 temple and the glory's return (43:1-5).
- **Edom.** Already condemned at 25:12-14 and listed in the pit at 32:29. Henry: Mount Seir "here it is convicted and condemned by itself, and has woes of its own" and is "fitly put here, as in the prophecy of Obadiah, for all the enemies of the church." Ps 137:7 (ESV: "Lay it bare, lay it bare, down to its foundations!"); Obadiah 10-14 (stood at the crossroads to cut off fugitives); Lam 4:21-22.
- **Archaeology.** Arad ostracon 24: letter to the fortress commander Eliashib ordering men from Arad and Kinah to Ramat-negeb; commonly read as warning "lest Edom should come there." Dated variously c. 598/7 to the last years before 587; text damaged and reconstruction disputed (Faigenbaum-Golovin et al., Semitica 2020). Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arad_ostraca ; https://m.tau.ac.il/~alecsan1/Faigenbaum_Golovin_Arad24A_Semitica.pdf ; Bartlett, "Edom and the Fall of Jerusalem" https://www.andrews.edu/weblmsc/moodle/public/courses/relb274/lesson10/Bartlett%20Edom%20and%20the%20Fall%20of%20Jerusalem.pdf . Used on air only in general terms ("as most translators read it").
- **35:10** "These two nations ... shall be mine ... although the Lord was there." Henry: the temple was burnt, "but his promise to give that land to the seed of Jacob for an inheritance was not made void ... by that promise he did in effect still keep possession for Israel."
- **36:16-21** Israel profaned the name among the nations ("These are the people of the Lord, and yet they had to go out of his land," 36:20).
- **36:25-27** sprinkling (purification water, Num 19), new heart (Hebrew *lev*), heart of stone (*lev ha'even*) vs heart of flesh (*lev basar*), "my Spirit" (*ruchi*). Cf. 11:19-20 earlier promise. Westminster Confession 10.1 (effectual calling) uses the language "taking away their heart of stone, and giving unto them an heart of flesh."
- **37:1** Keil: the article on "the valley" points back to 3:22 (the plain where Ezekiel received the first revelation about Jerusalem's fate).
- **37:1-14** *ruach* appears 10 times in ESV-English as Spirit (v1), breath (v5, 6, 8, 9 x3, 10), winds (v9), Spirit (v14). Keil: two stages based on Genesis 2:7; cites Theodoret.
- **37:15-28** two sticks (Judah; Joseph/Ephraim), reunion of kingdoms divided after Solomon (c. 930 B.C., so 340+ years), one king, "my servant David" (37:24-25; cf. 34:23), covenant of peace, everlasting covenant (37:26), sanctuary in their midst forevermore (37:26-28), anticipating 40-48 and the glory's return. 37:27 is cited at 2 Corinthians 6:16 (not used on air; one NT move only).

### Job 13
- Middle of Job's longest reply (12-14), closing round one. Two halves: 13:1-19 to the friends; 13:20-28 to God. 13:4 "worthless physicians"; 13:7-8 speaking falsely for God; 13:12 "proverbs of ashes"; 13:15 textual question; 13:25 leaf and chaff; 13:26 iniquities of my youth.
- 13:15: Ketiv *lo'* (lamed-aleph, "not") vs Qere *lo* (lamed-vav, "to/in him"); Keil: one of eighteen such Ketiv/Qere cases; Targum, Syriac, Jerome favor "in him."

### John 11:1-16
- Jesus beyond the Jordan where John first baptized (10:40). Bethany about fifteen stadia, "not much short of two English miles" (Ellicott, 11:18; https://biblehub.com/commentaries/john/11-18.htm). Mary identified by the anointing told in ch. 12. 11:5-6 "loved ... So ... he stayed two days longer." Sleep as death (11:11-14). Thomas Didymus = twin (Henry: Hebrew Thomim).

### 1 John 3:1-10
- Follows directly from 2:29 ("born of him"). 3:1-3 adoption and hope; 3:4-10 practice of sin vs righteousness, children of God vs devil. 3:9 present tense ("makes a practice"); 1:8 rules out perfectionism.

## 3. Interpretive questions

**Ezekiel**
1. *Is 37:1-14 about bodily resurrection or national restoration?* Keil: slain nation (37:9 *harugim*, "slain"); the vision is not "intended to symbolize the resurrection of all the dead"; yet concedes the fathers rightly found a locus classicus for the resurrection. Tertullian: the figure presupposes the reality; also argues God tested the prophet's faith on a real point. Spurgeon: "not a mere figure," a parable built on resurrection. Henry: fulfilled in the return (Cyrus) and a pattern of the resurrection. Host: nation first (37:11), resurrection presupposed; Daniel 12:2.
2. *"Not for your sake" (36:22, 32): does God not love Israel?* Ligonier devotional: salvation is the Lord's self-imposed obligation by oath; Henry: "God's reasons of mercy are all fetched from within himself." Host: his name is the guarantee, not the rival, of his love.
3. *Why repeat Edom?* Henry: Edom stands for all the church's enemies (Obadiah); structural pairing with 36:1-15.

**Job**
1. *13:15 "Though he slay me, I will hope in him" or "I have no hope"?* Ellicott: "I have no hope" (better reading). Pulpit: cannot be, given 13:16. Keil: "I wait for him"; old rendering "thoroughly Job-like." Gill: marginal reading best. Host: traditional reading, because of 13:16.
2. *Speaking falsely for God (13:7)*: Henry: "God's truth needs not our lie."

**John**
1. *Why delay if he loved them (11:5-6)?* Calvin: do not judge God's love by present conditions; Chrysostom: so no one could say it was a fainting fit; Augustine: "He delayed to heal, that He might be able to raise to life."
2. *Why "sleep"?* Augustine: ease of waking; Calvin rejects that inference (common usage).
3. *Thomas: courage or despair?* Henry: courage with weak faith; Calvin: "language of despair," "inconsiderate zeal," though he does not refuse to die with Christ; Chrysostom: cowardice.

**1 John**
1. *3:9 "cannot sin": perfectionism?* Calvin: no; remnants of the old nature remain; Spirit rules; appeals to God's promise of a new heart. Augustine (Homily 4): "as" not equality (3:3, 3:7).
2. *3:2 seeing him as he is* — Augustine: we see him in the form of God; holy desire stretches the soul.
