## 1. Setting and background

### Daniel 9 (deepest)
- Date: "first year of Darius the son of Ahasuerus, by descent a Mede" (9:1), the year after Babylon fell to Cyrus's army in October 539 B.C. (ep. 35). So 539/538 B.C. Daniel was taken in 605 B.C. as a youth, so he is in his eighties.
- Henry: "In this year the seventy years of the Jews' captivity ended, but the decree for their release was not yet issued out; so that this address of Daniel's to God seems to have been ready in that year, and, probably, before he was cast into the lions' den." Henry reckons the seventy years from the first captivity (Jehoiakim's third year, Daniel 1:1). (bh_mhcw_daniel_9)
- Jerome: this Darius is the one who with Cyrus conquered the Chaldeans, not Darius Hystaspes (against Porphyry's late date). (jerome_dan)
- Structure of the book: court stories (1-6), visions (7-12). Chapter 7 (first year of Belshazzar) four beasts, Ancient of Days, one like a son of man; chapter 8 (third year of Belshazzar; language returns to Hebrew at 8:1) ram and goat named Media-Persia and Greece; Gabriel first appears at 8:16; 8:27 Daniel "appalled by the vision and did not understand it." Chapter 9 is the only vision that begins with Daniel reading Scripture and praying. Chapters 10-12 next.
- Jeremiah's seventy years: Jeremiah 25:11-12 and 29:10. Henry: Jeremiah 25:11 uses chorbath, "desolation," "the same word that Daniel here uses for the desolations of Jerusalem." 2 Chronicles 36:21 ties the seventy years to the land's Sabbaths (Cambridge, Keil).
- Prayer's sources: Piper lists allusions to Leviticus 26:40, Deuteronomy 28:64, Exodus 34:6, Psalm 44:14, Jeremiah 25:11. Leviticus 26:40-42 promises that if they confess their iniquity, God will remember his covenant. Tabletalk "Seventy More Weeks" sets the chapter against Deuteronomy 30:1-10 and Leviticus 26.
- Time of evening sacrifice (9:21): about the ninth hour; no temple since 586 B.C., but Daniel still keeps the hour (Jerome: prayer persisted from the morning sacrifice to the evening).
- Hebrew:
  - shabuim, "sevens" (9:24; ESV note "Or sevens"). Keil: not necessarily year-weeks, "an intentionally indefinite designation of a period of time measured by the number seven." Cambridge: "weeks of years, a sense not occurring elsewhere in Biblical Hebrew, but found in the Mishna."
  - nechtak, "decreed," literally "cut" (9:24); Cambridge: occurs only here in Biblical Hebrew; Gill: "the word, as it signifies 'to cut', aptly expresses the division, or section of these weeks into distinct periods, as seven, sixty two, and one."
  - chamudot, "greatly loved" (9:23), Bible Hub interlinear "precious"; HCSB "treasured by God"; Gill and Jerome "man of desires"; Jerome compares Solomon's name Jedidiah.
  - Spurgeon's literal rendering of 9:24: "To shut up the transgression, to seal up sin, and to cover up iniquity." YLT agrees: "to shut up the transgression, and to seal up sins, and to cover iniquity."
  - mashiach nagid, "an anointed one, a prince" (9:25); 9:26 "shall be cut off and shall have nothing."
- Background on the critical view: Cambridge (Driver) gives the Maccabean scheme (7 weeks end 538, 62 weeks end 171 B.C. with the murder of Onias III, last week 171-164 Antiochus) and then admits: "The number of years from 538 to 171 is not 434 (= 62 'weeks'), but 367; the number assigned in the prophecy is thus too large by 67." URL https://biblehub.com/commentaries/cambridge/daniel/9.htm

### Job 21:22-34
- Job's reply to Zophar's last speech (ch. 20). 21:1-21 (read 11-23, no episode): "Why do the wicked live, reach old age, and grow mighty in power?" (21:7) and "How often is it that the lamp of the wicked is put out?" (21:17), answering Bildad 18:5 and Zophar 20:5. Today's half finishes the speech and the second cycle of speeches.
- 21:22: Cambridge: "The principles of providence insisted on by the friends were not those according to which God's actual providence was administered. They were substituting their principles for His." Keil: "those that are high" = the heavenly beings.
- 21:23 "in his full vigor": Hebrew be'etsem tummo, "in the bone of his perfection" (Bible Hub interlinear; YLT "in his perfect strength"). Gill: "the words may be rendered 'in the strength of his integrity', or 'of his perfection'." tom is the root of Job's own "blameless" (tam, 1:1) and "integrity" (tummah, 2:3, 9).
- 21:24 "pails": Ellicott: "an uncertain word, occurring only here."
- 21:30: KJV "the wicked is reserved to the day of destruction"; ESV "the evil man is spared in the day of calamity." Barnes: Rosenmuller, Noyes and Schultens take "spared"; Barnes prefers "reserved" (chasak with le). Keil translates "During life removed at the time of dire calamity" (spared). Gill takes both: spared now, reserved to eternal destruction.
- 21:32: "watch is kept over his tomb": Cambridge, the word is "heap," i.e., a sepulchral monument; cites the Sidonian sarcophagus of Eshmunazar with its curses on grave robbers.
- 21:33: Ellicott: "Death is robbed of its repulsiveness and horror, seeing that all will be glad to join in his funeral procession."
- 21:34: "empty nothings" = hebel (Bible Hub interlinear), the word of Ecclesiastes. "falsehood": Ellicott, "all that is left of them is transgression" (ma'al).
- Henry on 21:29 cites "the prophecy of Enoch, the seventh from Adam, Jude 14": a real cross-reading link.

### John 13:18-30
- Upper room, Thursday night of Passion Week. 13:1-11 foot washing (ep. 35). 13:12-17 (no episode): "you also ought to wash one another's feet... If you know these things, blessed are you if you do them."
- 13:18 quotes Psalm 41:9. Sproul: the whole psalm is not messianic, since David confesses sin in it; the application is at the friend who ate bread. Henry and Jewish expositors: Ahithophel.
- 13:19 "that you may believe that I am he" (ego eimi; Sproul: the Septuagint's rendering of the divine name).
- Reclining at table (Sproul, Calvin): on left elbow, so the beloved disciple at Jesus' right could lean back on his chest. Sproul: seat of honor at the left; "The consensus is that the man who was sitting in the seat of honor that night... was Judas." Keller (2016) says the same. Ligonier devotional: hosts passed a morsel to the guest of honor.
- Satan entered him (13:27) after "the devil had already put it into the heart" (13:2). Luke 22:3 earlier. Augustine Tractate 62: first entrance to tempt, now to take possession.
- "And it was night" (13:30).

### Jude 8-16 (book introduced today)
- Author: "Jude, a servant of Jesus Christ and brother of James" (v1). Greek Ioudas, the same name as Iscariot (Henry: "He had a wicked name-sake"; Spurgeon: "That is to say Judas, not Iscariot"). Brother of James of Jerusalem, so a half-brother of Jesus (Matthew 13:55 lists James and Judas); Clement of Alexandria: he did not call himself the Lord's brother but servant. Date: probably the 60s A.D. (traditional), audience likely Jewish Christians who knew Enoch traditions (Ellicott: some had heard apostles, v17).
- Structure (Ellicott: Jude loves triplets): greeting (1-2), purpose: "contend for the faith that was once for all delivered to the saints" (3-4), three examples Israel, angels, Sodom (5-7), "these people" described and doomed (8-16), the apostles' warning and the believers' duty (17-23), doxology (24-25).
- 8-16: the pattern is example then "these" (houtoi, vv. 8, 10, 12, 14, 16).
- v9: Deuteronomy 34:6 "no one knows the place of his burial." Cambridge and Ellicott: Origen (De Principiis 3.2) says the dispute was recorded in the Assumption of Moses; Targum of Jonathan made Michael guardian of the grave. Clement of Alexandria: "Here he confirms the assumption of Moses." Zechariah 3:2 "The Lord rebuke you, O Satan."
- v11 Cain (Genesis 4), Balaam for gain (Numbers 22; 31:16), Korah's rebellion (Numbers 16).
- v12 spilades: Ellicott, "'rocks' is the safer translation," sunken rocks; Cambridge: "reefs" or "rocks below the sea"; agapai love feasts. "shepherds feeding themselves" (Ezekiel 34:2).
- v14-15 quote 1 Enoch 1:9; "ungodly" four times in v15. Ellicott: Ethiopic Enoch brought from Abyssinia by James Bruce in 1773. Ligonier Tabletalk: 1 Enoch "a favorite of the Dead Sea sect."

## 3. Interpretive questions

### Daniel 9
1. Why pray for what God has already promised? Henry: promises are "intended, not to supersede, but to excite and encourage, our prayers" (paraphrase). Jerome: lest "a feeling of security might produce carelessness" (paraphrase). Calvin: "prayer is the chief exercise of faith." Answer: God's promises appoint the means.
2. What and when are the seventy weeks?
   - Messianic, continuous: Africanus (via Jerome) from Artaxerxes' twentieth year (Nehemiah) to Christ; Tertullian (via Jerome) to Vespasian; Calvin (62 weeks from Darius's sixth year to Christ's baptism; last week = Christ confirming the covenant; "The angel now returns to Christ"); Henry ("the clearest, brightest, prophecy of the Messiah, in all the Old Testament"); Spurgeon 1882 (cut off in the middle of the week after three and a half years of ministry; would not fix dates); Ligonier Tabletalk "Atoning for Iniquity" (69 weeks correspond roughly to return under Cyrus to the death of Jesus; 490 "at least partly symbolic").
   - Last week detached to the end: Hippolytus (c. 200): after the 62 weeks and the gospel preached everywhere, "there will remain only one week, the last," with Elijah and Enoch and Antichrist. Lloyd-Jones (mljtrust summary): the gap/futurist view as a school from about 1830 (Irving, Darby, Scofield); urged humility.
   - Maccabean/critical: Cambridge; 67-year shortfall.
   - Symbolic: Keil (indefinite period measured by sevens, last week Antichrist); Tabletalk (sevenfold punishment, Leviticus 26).
   - Jerome refused to choose: "unsafe to pass judgment upon the opinions of the great teachers of the Church".
   Host view: the anointed one "cut off and shall have nothing" before the city and sanctuary fall (9:26) fits Christ and A.D. 70 better than any alternative; Jesus himself cites the abomination of desolation (Matthew 24:15) as still future in his day. Hold the arithmetic humbly.
3. Who is "he" in 9:27? Calvin: Christ confirms the covenant. Hippolytus/Keil/futurists: Antichrist. Host: leans to Calvin, mention briefly.

### Job 21:22-34
1. Spared or reserved in 21:30? Both translations defended (Barnes vs Keil). Job's argument in context (the friends say the wicked fall now) favors "spared"; the KJV's "reserved" became the church's comfort (Henry, Gill, Gregory). Job is honest about the facts; he does not yet say what follows.
2. Is Job denying justice? No: 21:22 says God judges even the high ones; he denies the friends' timetable (Cambridge, Henry).

### John 13:18-30
1. Did the morsel cause Satan's entry? Calvin: "It was, indeed, the occasion, but not the cause." Augustine: what matters is the receiver; "the bread was given to the traitor... as an evidence of the grace he had treated with ingratitude." Keller: "one last... blast of love."
2. "What you are going to do, do quickly": command? Augustine: "He did not command the crime, but foretold evil to Judas, and good to us." Chrysostom: not commanding nor advising but reproaching.
3. Why did no one understand? Each suspected himself (Sproul: "Lord, is it I?").

### Jude 8-16
1. Does Jude's use of Enoch and the Moses tradition make them Scripture? Tertullian: Enoch genuine Scripture. Jerome: Jude "rejected by many" for it, but gained authority. Augustine: Enoch "left some divine writings" (because Jude says so), but the books under his name are not canonical. Calvin: perhaps unwritten tradition, not an apocryphal book. Ligonier Tabletalk: Jude sifted wheat from chaff under the Spirit. Host: quoting a true sentence does not canonize its book (Paul and Aratus, Acts 17:28; Titus 1:12).
2. What was the dispute over Moses' body? Calvin: Satan wanted the grave known to make an idol of it. Henry: several guesses, including the Jewish church as Moses' body; he does not decide.
