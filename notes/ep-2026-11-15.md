The glory that left Jerusalem in Ezekiel 11 comes home through the east gate, and the gate is shut behind it. We look at why the whole mountain becomes most holy, why only the sons of Zadok come near, how Eliphaz turns a true description of sin into a false diagnosis of Job, how Caiaphas prophesied without meaning to, and how John tells believers to test the spirits.

## 1. SETTING AND BACKGROUND

### Ezekiel 42-44
- Same vision as 40:1: 25th year of exile, 14th after the fall, 10th day of first month, ~573 B.C. (ep. 29). Nebuchadnezzar still reigns in Babylon (605-562); Jehoiachin still a prisoner (released 561, 2 Kings 25:27). Jerusalem in ruins 14 years.
- Structure of 40-48: 40-42 the house measured; 43:1-12 the glory enters (climax, answering 8-11's departure); 43:13-46:24 altar and ordinances of worship (who may come near and how); 47-48 river and land.
- Glory's departure: 10:4, 18-19 (threshold → east gate), 11:22-23 (to the mountain east of the city), in the 6th year (8:1, 592 B.C.). Return now in the 25th year: about 19 years later in Ezekiel's timeline. Keil: "The coming of the theophany from the east points back to Ezekiel 10:19; Ezekiel 11:1 and Ezekiel 11:23... It was from the east, therefore, that it returned." (bh 43-2)
- Pulpit (43:2): earlier east gate always open in old temple; the shut gate is a new feature (bh 44-2).
- 42:15-20: outer wall 500 by 500 cubits (ESV "cubits"; KJV/Henry "reeds" — text dispute; Lightfoot's 'larger than Jerusalem' already used ep. 29) "to make a separation between the holy and the common" (42:20).
- 42:13-14: holy chambers for priests to eat most holy offerings; change garments before going to the people (cf. 44:19 "lest they transmit holiness").
- 43:7 "carcases/dead bodies of their kings": Ellicott — no historical proof of burials in temple area; language from Leviticus 26:30, means idols; Manasseh's idols in the courts (2 Kings 21:4-7). Henry: "only a wall between me and them" — palace adjoining temple; "The nearer the church the further from God."
- Zadok: priest faithful in Adonijah's revolt (1 Kings 1:7-8) when Abiathar went astray; Solomon installed Zadok in Abiathar's place (1 Kings 2:27, 35), fulfilling word against Eli's house (1 Sam 2:35). Ellicott 44:15; Fairbairn 479-480. Ezekiel himself is a priest (1:3), likely Zadokite.
- Levites demoted 44:10-14; cf. Josiah's reform: priests of high places did not come up to the altar (2 Kings 23:9; Poole cites 2 Kings 23:8-9).
- 44:28 "I am their inheritance" — Numbers 18:20; Deuteronomy 10:9 (Ellicott).
- Golden Gate (Jerusalem's eastern city gate): walled up 1541 under Suleiman (Madain Project, https://www.madainproject.com/golden_gate_jerusalem: "Ottoman Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent rebuilt it together with the city walls, but walled it up in 1541 CE, and it stayed that way.") Popular claims of fulfilment; but Ezekiel's is "the outer gate of the sanctuary" in a vision.

### Job 15:17-35
- Round two (15-21), Eliphaz's second speech, second half. Appeals to his own experience and to the tradition of the fathers "to whom alone the land was given, and no stranger passed among them" (pure, uncorrupted tradition). Job will answer in 16-17 (next) and directly rebut the claim in 21 (wicked prosper). Cf. Psalm 73:3-12 and 73:17-19.
- Structure: 17-19 intro; 20-24 inner terror; 25-27 cause (defiance of God; bossed shield; fat); 28-30 ruin; 31-35 summary: trust in emptiness (shav'), unripe grape, olive blossom, barren company, fire on tents of bribery, conceive trouble, give birth to evil.
- Ellicott 15:25: the bossed shield is the wicked man's, not God's (AV ambiguity).

### John 11:45-57
- Bethany, a few weeks before the final Passover (spring A.D. 30 or 33). Sanhedrin (Ligonier: mainly Sadducees with influential Pharisee minority). Caiaphas high priest about A.D. 18-36 (appointed by Valerius Gratus, deposed by Vitellius); Calvin and Chrysostom: "that year" not annual office but office for sale/Romans changing priests; best: that year of years.
- "Our place" — temple (Ligonier: "probably refers chiefly to the temple"); Calvin: temple or country.
- Ephraim near the wilderness (often identified with et-Taiyibeh NE of Jerusalem; not used). Passover purification (Numbers 9:6-13; 2 Chronicles 30:17-18).
- Fulfilment irony: Rome destroyed temple in A.D. 70 (Chrysostom: "when they had done that by doing which they thought to escape, they yet did not escape").

### 1 John 4:1-6
- Follows 3:24, first explicit mention of the Spirit; now not every spirit is that Spirit. Context: secessionists (2:19), Cerinthian/docetic denial (Ellicott, Meyer). Ellicott outline: difference among spirits (1), measure (2-3), encouragement (4), condemnation (5), conclusion (6).
- Greek: dokimazete — test as metals/coins are tested.

## 3. INTERPRETIVE QUESTIONS
- Ezekiel 44:2 Why is the gate shut? (a) Mary's perpetual virginity: Ambrose (attributed, De institutione virginis 8.52; sacred-texts note doubts wording), Jerome reports "some quite emphatically understand" it of Mary; Gill: "approved of, not only by Papists, but by many Protestant writers". (b) Scripture sealed until Christ opens it: Jerome's own commentary. (c) Heaven, entered by Christ alone by his own right: Henry concise. (d) Church kept pure: Gill. (e) Text: the honour of the place where the Lord entered: Ellicott, Pulpit, Poole, Keil. Host: (e) — the verse gives its own reason; the closed door preserves the memory and reverence (Henry complete: "God will have a way by himself").
- Ezekiel 43:18-27 Sacrifices in a future temple? Same answer as ep. 29 (symbolic picture); Fairbairn: prince offers sin offerings, so not literal messianic; Henry concise: no atoning sacrifices now (Heb 10:14).
- Ezekiel 44:10-16 Is the demotion harsh? Henry: mixture of mercy, still porters and servants; the faithful honoured. Poole: these are the sons of apostate priests.
- Job 15: Is Eliphaz right? Inner torment of wicked: Gregory yes (conscience); outward ruin always and soon: no (Job 21; Psalm 73). Henry concise on the logical fallacy.
- John 11:51 How can a wicked man prophesy? Chrysostom (grace used mouth), Calvin (two tongues; God honoured the office; not a precedent for Rome), Augustine (Spirit of prophecy used wicked men; office as sacramental), Ligonier (God's intention through human intention, Gen 50:20).
- 1 John 4:2 Is the confession only words? Augustine: deeds — schismatics deny it by rending unity; Calvin/Ellicott/Ligonier: doctrinal test specific to John's opponents; Ligonier: extends to all essentials.
- 1 John 4:1 Who tests? Calvin: whole church and each believer; private and public trial; Ellicott: all alike, not merely ordained class.
