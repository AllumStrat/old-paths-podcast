## 1. SETTING AND BACKGROUND

### Ezekiel 29-30
- Place in book: chapters 25-32 are oracles against the nations, placed between the start of the siege (24:1-2, January 588 B.C.) and the arrival of the fugitive (33:21). Seven nations (Ammon, Moab, Edom, Philistia, Tyre, Sidon, Egypt). Egypt gets the most: four chapters, seven oracles. Keil's structure (biblehub kad 29): "The announcement of the judgment upon Egypt is proclaimed in seven 'words of God.'" 29:1-16; 29:17-21; 30:1-19; 30:20-26; 31; 32:1-16; 32:17-32.
- Dates: 29:1 tenth year, tenth month, twelfth day (January 587 B.C.); 29:17 twenty-seventh year, first month, first day (April 571 B.C.), "the latest date among all Ezekiel's prophecies" (Ellicott); 30:20 eleventh year, first month, seventh day (April 587 B.C.). Henry lists five dates across 29-32. Henry: Egypt oracle placed after Tyre because "Nebuchadnezzar's gaining Egypt was the reward of his service against Tyre." Henry: first oracle "was just at the time when the king of Egypt was coming to relieve Jerusalem and raise the siege (Jer 37 5)".
- Pharaoh: Hophra (Jeremiah 44:30), Greek Apries, Wahibre, reigned 589-570 B.C., Twenty-sixth (Saite) Dynasty, capital Sais in the Delta. Wikipedia Apries: "In 588 BCE, Apries dispatched a force to Jerusalem to protect it from Babylonian forces sent by Nebuchadnezzar II (Book of Jeremiah 37:5; 34:21). His forces quickly withdrew". Later defeated at Irasa by Cyrene (c. 570), army revolted, Amasis became pharaoh 570; Apries returned in 567 with Babylonian help and was likely killed. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apries
- Herodotus 2.169 (Macaulay translation, Gutenberg): "But Apries is said to have supposed that not even a god would be able to cause him to cease from his rule, so firmly did he think that it was established." https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2707/pg2707.txt (Ellicott and Henry both cite this.)
- Lachish Letter 3 (ostracon, c. 588 B.C.): "The commander of the army Coniah son of Elnatan, has gone down to go to Egypt" https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lachish_letters . Shows Judah's leaders looking to Egypt during the crisis.
- BM 33041: Babylonian fragment, Nebuchadnezzar's 37th year (568/567 B.C.), march against Egypt, Wiseman reads "[Ama]sis". Search summary; the tablet's scope is debated. Mention as "a broken Babylonian tablet in the British Museum records a march against Egypt in Nebuchadnezzar's thirty-seventh year, 568 B.C." Only general claim.
- Geography: "from Migdol to Syene" = northeast frontier to Aswan, "the whole length of the land" (Ellicott). Pathros = Upper Egypt, "land of their origin" (Keil: Menes from Thinis). Noph = Memphis; Zoan = Tanis; No = Thebes (Luxor, Karnak); Sin = Pelusium "the key of Egypt" (Ellicott); On = Heliopolis; Pi-beseth = Bubastis; Tehaphnehes = Tahpanhes/Daphnae, border fortress, where the Jews fled with Jeremiah (Jeremiah 43:7-9) (Keil).
- Hebrew: tannim/tannin (29:3) "the lengthened animal, the snake; here, the water-snake, the crocodile, the standing symbol of Egypt in the prophets (cf. Isaiah 51:9; Isaiah 27:1; Psalm 74:13)" (Keil). Ye'or = Nile; ye'orim = its arms and canals. Mishenet qaneh, "staff of reed" (29:6; Isaiah 36:6; 2 Kings 18:21). Pe'ullah "wages" (29:20, Keil: "the recompense for labour, as in Leviticus 19:13"). Qeren "horn" (29:21; Psalm 132:17; 1 Samuel 2:1). Pitchon peh "opening of the mouth" (29:21; 16:63; 24:27).
- Refrain: "they shall know that I am the Lord" (29:6, 9, 16, 21; 30:8, 19, 25, 26).

## 3. INTERPRETIVE QUESTIONS
Ezekiel:
1. Did Ezekiel's Tyre prophecy (26:7-14) fail, as 29:18 seems to admit? Keil: "This does not imply that Nebuchadnezzar had been unable to accomplish the work which he had undertaken ... but simply that he had not received the recompense which he expected". Ellicott: "all that is meant is that he failed to obtain any considerable booty thereby". Henry: Tyrians "sent away by ship their best effects". Poole: "most conclude that it was delivered on composition". Gill: Jerome. Host: the prophet who invented a failure would not publish it; God is candid about wages, and chapter 26 itself already shifted from "he" to "they" (yesterday's episode noted Ellicott on this).
2. The forty years (29:11-13): no record of total depopulation. Ellicott: wilderness language, "the literal fulfilment of which was neither intended nor expected". Henry/Gill: the forty years end near Cyrus. Keil: Egypt "never recovered its former power after the Chaldean period". Host: take Ellicott's point about Ezekiel's symbolic numbers (4:6) and Keil's about the substance fulfilled.
3. Who is the dragon? Origen: the devil ("what real bodily dragon..."). Keil, Poole, Gill, Ellicott: crocodile, Pharaoh. Host: Pharaoh first; the sea-monster language (Isaiah 51:9) does carry the overtone of chaos opposing God.
4. Which broken arm (30:21)? Keil: the repulse of Hophra's relief army (Jeremiah 37:5-7). Ellicott/Henry: Carchemish 605. Gill: Hophra defeated by the Cyreneans. Host: Keil fits the date best.
5. How can Nebuchadnezzar "work for me" (29:20)? Gill "not intentionally, but eventually"; Poole: "God had further, higher, and more excellent ends"; Carson: no conscious obedience (Isaiah 10:5); WCF 5.4.
Job:
1. 10:13: what did God hide in his heart? Keil/Cambridge/Ellicott: Job suspects a hostile purpose behind the kindness. Barnes/Henry: piety, all is God's counsel. Host: Job is right that there is a hidden purpose, wrong about its character (Job 1-2; James 5:11 "the purpose of the Lord, how the Lord is compassionate and merciful").
2. Is 10:2 "Do not condemn me" a good prayer? Henry: yes (cross, not curse). Gregory: God judges in two ways.
John:
1. Who is the door in 10:1? Chrysostom: the Scriptures; then in 10:7 Christ. Augustine and Calvin: Christ. Strain: don't press metaphors; both shepherd and door.
2. Who are the thieves? Sproul: the clergy who cast out the blind man. Calvin: all false teachers, not Moses and the prophets. Augustine: all apart from him; prophets came "with Him".
3. "Calls his own by name": Calvin doubts intimate knowledge reading; Augustine: names written in the book of life.
1 John:
1. Who are children, fathers, young men? Calvin: teknia all; Henry: stages.
2. Is creation evil? Augustine: no (ring analogy). Calvin: world = life apart from the kingdom.
