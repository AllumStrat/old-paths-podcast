## 1. SETTING AND BACKGROUND

### Ezekiel 33-34 (deepest)
- Book structure: 1-24 judgment on Jerusalem (ending with the siege, 24:1-2, Jan 588 B.C.); 25-32 oracles against the nations; 33-48 hope and restoration. Henry: "The prophet has now come off his circuit, which he went as judge... he now returns to the children of his people."
- 33:1-20 restates the watchman commission of 3:16-21 and the individual-responsibility teaching of chapter 18 (Henry: "These verses are the substance of what we had before (ch. xviii. 20, &c.)"). The Ligonier devotional (Tabletalk, Oct 1, 2013) frames it: exiles moved from fatalism to accusing God of injustice.
- 33:21 date: twelfth year of exile (Jehoiachin's, from 597), tenth month, fifth day. With the series' chronology (fall of the city summer 587, ch. 32 March 585, Ellicott "nearly two months before... Ezekiel 32"), the fugitive arrives about January 585 B.C. Keil: the year and a half is not a reason to emend to "eleventh" (Syriac, Doederlein, Hitzig); distance to northern Mesopotamia and post-catastrophe confusion explain it. Ellicott: Ezra 7:9's four-month journey; a captive caravan with spoil would be slower. Cambridge: Syriac and some MSS read eleventh year, "very generally accepted," eleven and twelve easily confused. Gill: different regnal reckonings shorten the gap. Henry: news must have reached Babylon earlier; this is the eyewitness.
- 33:22 closes the muteness thread: 3:26-27 (bound and mute except when God speaks), 24:25-27 (promise: on that day your mouth will be opened), 29:21, now fulfilled. Keil: the hand of the Lord came the evening before; "silent no more" means the restriction is lifted. Henry: the oracles to the nations (25-32) were probably delivered in writing.
- 33:23-29 those in the "waste places" of Judah after the fall (Henry links to the period around Gedaliah's murder and Johanan, Jer 40-43) boasting from Abraham.
- 33:30-33 the exiles in Tel-abib gossiping by walls and doors, coming to hear the prophet as entertainment ("lustful songs", ESV footnote: songs of love).
- Chapter 34 expands Jeremiah 23:1-8 (Keil, Ellicott). Shepherds = rulers (Keil, Ellicott; Ellicott: "a common Scriptural expression for rulers", the name in use since David was taken from the flocks, 2 Sam 5:2, Ps 78:70-71). Kings of the ANE called themselves shepherds (Ligonier devotional: "common ancient Near Eastern imagery that saw kings, priests, and other leaders as shepherds").
- Hebrew: ra'ah, "to shepherd, to tend, to pasture, to feed" (Strong's 7462, BDB); participle ro'eh = shepherd; BDB notes reflexive "pastured (fed, enriched) themselves Ezekiel 34:2,8,10." URL: https://biblehub.com/hebrew/7462.htm
- 34:23-24: "one shepherd, my servant David" (Keil: "one" against the many bad shepherds and against the divided kingdoms, Ezek 37:24; not David raised from the dead; the sprout of David called David in Hosea 3:5 and Jeremiah 30:9; points back to 2 Samuel 7:8). Gill: not David nor Zerubbabel; Kimchi owns it as the Messiah. Henry: "He is the one Shepherd, under whom Jews and Gentiles should be one fold."
- John 10 rests on Ezekiel 34 (Sproul's Good Shepherd Discourse sermon reads Ezek 34:1-5 alongside John 10 and John 21 "feed my sheep").

### Job 12
- Begins Job's third and longest reply (chs. 12-14), closing the first cycle of speeches; reasons with friends then turns to God (Henry). Henry's three parts: 1-5 rebuke of their conceit; 6-11 the wicked often prosper; 12-25 God's sovereign wisdom and power.
- Keil: 12:7-10 is the counterpart of Bildad's 8:8-10 and Zophar's 11:7-9; "The working of God... is the sermon which is continuously preached by all created things."
- 12:9 "the hand of the Lord": Henry: the only time the name Jehovah appears in the discourses between Job and his friends. (The phrase also matches Isaiah 41:20.)

### John 10:22-42
- Feast of Dedication (Hanukkah), instituted 164 B.C. by Judas Maccabaeus after Antiochus Epiphanes' profanation (1 Macc 4:52-59), eight days from 25 Kislev, called Feast of Lights (Ellicott). Wieseler: Tabernacles Oct 19, Dedication Dec 20, about two months after 10:21 (Ellicott). Calvin: enkainia means renovations. Solomon's colonnade was a rebuilt porch on the east side (Calvin: not Solomon's original).
- Ellicott: "It is the Feast of Lights, and He had declared Himself the Light of the world... It is the feast which commemorates the cleansing of the Temple."
- Greek: parrēsia, "boldness, confidence, openness, plainness" (Strong's 3954), John 10:24 "plainly" and 1 John 2:28 "confidence". URL: https://biblehub.com/greek/3954.htm
- 10:34 quotes Psalm 82:6 (Calvin: magistrates called gods because God gave them authority; "Scripture cannot be broken means, that the doctrine of Scripture is inviolable").
- 10:40 across the Jordan, Bethany beyond Jordan (John 1:28).

### 1 John 2:24-29
- Continues the antichrist section (2:18-27); 2:28 begins the transition to the children-of-God section (ESV heading "Children of God" at 2:28). "Abide" (menō) six times in 2:24-28.
- 2:28 "appears" and "coming" (parousia); first mention in the letter of being "born of him" (2:29), which chapter 3 develops.

## 3. INTERPRETIVE QUESTIONS
- Ezekiel 33:11: If God has no pleasure in the death of the wicked, why do the wicked die, and how does this square with election? Calvin on 18:23 (same words): God sincerely calls all, the prophet speaks not of the secret counsel but recalls men from despair; God's will is simple though it looks various to us. Henry: God sincere and in earnest; sinners "die because they will die". Piper's "Are There Two Wills in God?" treats Ezek 18:23 and 18:32 (could not fetch the article: desiringgod.org 403; gospeltranslations wiki empty) so not used. Host: hold both, Deut 29:29; Westminster Confession 3.1 not needed on air beyond a sentence.
- Ezekiel 33:21: twelfth or eleventh year? Keil and Ellicott vs Cambridge (Syriac). Host: keep the text.
- Ezekiel 34:23: who is "David"? Keil, Gill, Henry, Ligonier: the Messiah of David's line; John 10:11, 16 confirm.
- Ezekiel 34: Who are the shepherds? Keil: rulers (civil authorities); Ligonier and Sproul: also spiritual leaders; Augustine: applies to bishops.
- Job 12:7-10: what do the beasts teach? Henry: either that robbers prosper or God's sovereign dominion; Keil: God as creator and preserver. Gregory allegorizes beasts as men of slow parts.
- John 10:30: unity of will/power or essence? Calvin: agreement, not homoousios; Augustine: the Jews understood what the Arians did not; Chrysostom: same power implies same essence. Host: with Chrysostom's inference.
- John 10:34-36: why cite Psalm 82? Calvin: argument from less to greater; Ellicott: technical rabbinic argument with a deeper meaning; Augustine: "If the word of God makes you gods, how can the Word of God be otherwise than God?"
- 1 John 2:27: "no need that anyone should teach you" vs teachers? Calvin and Augustine.
