## 1. SETTING AND BACKGROUND

### Ezekiel 13-15
- Date: no new date formula between 8:1 (sixth year of Jehoiachin's exile, sixth month, fifth day = September 592 B.C.) and 20:1 (seventh year, fifth month, tenth day = August 591 B.C.). Chapters 12-19 are undated oracles inside that year. Zedekiah is king in Jerusalem (597-586); his rebellion against Babylon is brewing (Ezekiel 17).
- False prophecy in the period: Hananiah's two-year promise (Jeremiah 28:1-4, 593 B.C.; Poole cites Jeremiah 28:11); Jeremiah's letter to the exiles warns against "your prophets and your diviners who are among you" (29:8-9) and names Ahab son of Kolaiah and Zedekiah son of Maaseiah (29:21-23), roasted by Nebuchadnezzar, and Shemaiah (29:24-32). Henry notes the false prophets were both in Jerusalem (Jeremiah 23:14) and among the captives (Jeremiah 29:8).
- Place in book: chapters 12-24 answer the objections that Jerusalem will not fall. 12:21-28 proverbs of delay, 13 false prophets, 14 elders and righteous men, 15 vine, 16 the foundling bride, 17 eagles and vine, 18 sour grapes, 19 lament.
- Culture: mud-brick and cob walls in Mesopotamia and Judah melt in heavy rain; plaster covers cracks. Spurgeon 1868: "one of the cob walls in the East, daubed with bad mortar which had not been well tempered, that is to say, not well mixed with the straw." Calvin: sand without lime. The prophetesses' kesatot and mispachot (13:18): Keil says the meaning "cushion or pillow" (LXX, Vulgate pulvilli) is "merely an inference" and rejects it; mispachot likely close-fitting head coverings; Ephrem Syrus took kesatot as amulets (Keil rejects). ESV "magic bands" and "veils." Calvin compares the Roman augur's veiled head (Livy).
- Daniel (14:14, 20; 28:3): taken to Babylon in 605 B.C. (Daniel 1); by 592 he had interpreted Nebuchadnezzar's dream and been promoted (Daniel 2:48). Critical view: an ancient Canaanite hero "Danel" known from Ugaritic texts (the Aqhat tale). Keil: "The fact that Daniel is named before Job does not warrant the conjecture that some other older Daniel is meant."
- Hebrew: tafel (Strong's 8602): BDB lists I. adjective "tasteless, unseasoned" (Job 6:6; Lamentations 2:14 of unsatisfying prophecies) and II. noun "whitewash" (Ezekiel 13:10, 11, 14, 15; 22:28). https://biblehub.com/hebrew/8602.htm. Chayits (2434), "a party wall," occurs once. https://biblehub.com/hebrew/2434.htm. 14:3 "taken their idols into their hearts," literally caused them to go up upon their heart (Henry: "They have made their idols to ascend upon their hearts (so the word is)"). Gillulim, Ezekiel's characteristic word for idols. 14:9 pittah, persuade or entice (Keil; 1 Kings 22:20 same verb). 13:4 shu'alim, foxes or jackals.
- Lev 26 background to the four judgments (Keil: famine 26:26, beasts 26:22, sword and pestilence 26:25).

### Job 4
- Eliphaz the Temanite: Teman in Edom, known for wisdom (Jeremiah 49:7). Probably the eldest (speaks first). Begins the first cycle (4-14).
- Structure: 4:2-6 Job's past counsel turned against him; 4:7-11 retribution observed ("as I have seen"), with five Hebrew words for lion (Henry: "The Hebrew tongue has five several names for lions, and they are all here used"); 4:12-21 the night vision.
- 4:5 "it touches you": Henry notes "The very word that Satan himself had used, ch. 1:11, 2:5."
- 4:17 ESV footnote "Or more than": KJV "Shall mortal man be more just than God?"
- 4:6 Hebrew lacks "of God" (ESV footnote).
- Forward links: Job 9:2 asks the same question as 4:17; 42:7 God says the friends have not spoken of him what is right.

### John 8:12-20
- The Feast of Booths has just ended (7:37 "the last day"). Setting 8:20 "in the treasury" (Gill: "The place where the thirteen chests stood, into which the people put their voluntary contributions"; Sproul: "thirteen receptacles shaped in the form of the shofar"). The treasury chests stood in the Court of the Women, which is where, according to the Mishnah (Sukkah 5:2-3, fetched in episode 14 research, paraphrase only), four great golden lamp stands were lit at Booths, and there was no courtyard in Jerusalem that was not lit by them.
- John 7:53-8:11 bracketed; Calvin notes those who omit it join 8:12 to the last-day sermon of 7:37.
- Law of two witnesses: Deuteronomy 17:6; 19:15. Contrast John 5:31.

### 2 Peter 2:10-16
- Peter writing near death (1:14), mid 60s, from Rome (1 Peter 5:13 "Babylon"); false teachers in the churches of Asia Minor; parallel Jude 8-13.
- 2:10 "especially" carries on from 2:9; authadeis "willful" or self-pleasing (Calvin's editor: "self-pleasing ... whose god was self"); doxas "glorious ones."
- 2:13 ESV footnote: "Some manuscripts love feasts" (agapais for apatais; Jude 12 has agapais).
- 2:14 ESV footnote "Or eyes full of an adulteress."
- 2:15 Bosor for Beor (Numbers 22:5). Gill: Beor and Bosor "are the same names," explained by Chaldean pronunciation since Peter was "now being at Babylon."
- Balaam: Numbers 22-24; 31:16; Revelation 2:14; Jude 11.

## 3. INTERPRETIVE QUESTIONS

Ezekiel:
1. 14:9 How can God "deceive" a prophet? Calvin: not mere permission; 1 Kings 22; God uses Satan "as organs of his vengeance"; the prophet deceived through the people's fault. Keil: perfect tense, divine ordination; fathers and Lutherans wrong to read permission; but "this does not destroy human freedom" and it falls only on "persons who admit evil into themselves." Henry: "Both are sins in him who does them, and so they are not from God; both are punishments to him to whom they are done, and so they are from God." Westminster Confession 5.4: "not by a bare permission"; "the sinfulness thereof proceedeth only from the creature."
2. 14:14 Why these three, and why Daniel? Henry (Daniel as a modern instance between two ancients), Keil and Jerome (climax of deliverances), Spurgeon 1651 (Daniel about thirty, "sandwiched"), Criswell (Daniel had the largest place in the people's hopes), Augustine (three classes: leaders, continent, married). Ugaritic Danel view noted in one sentence.
3. 14:22-23 Comfort how? Henry: you will see their repentance; Calvin and Keil: you will see their wickedness and acknowledge the judgment just.
4. 13:5 The breach: by prayer or teaching? Calvin restricts it to teaching ("I restrict it to their teaching") though he admits Psalm 106:23; Poole: "partly by prayer, and partly by doctrine, and partly by personal reformation"; Henry: "they were not praying prophets." Gregory: free speech against the powers.

Job 4:
1. Was the vision from God? Trapp's survey; Henry (good angel, misapplied); Gregory reads the "secret word" both of Christ and of heretics' claims. Host: true content, false use.
2. 4:7 "who that was innocent ever perished?" Gregory and Henry (Abel) and Trapp (John the Baptist): true eternally, false temporally.

John 8:
1. 8:14 vs 5:31. Calvin on 5:31: "he speaks by way of concession."
2. What is the "light of the world" against? Augustine: not the sun (against the Manichees); Chrysostom: "Not of Galilee"; Calvin: no spark outside Christ.

2 Peter 2:
1. Glorious ones: Calvin: magistrates ("he refers to the imperial and magisterial power"); Gill: government of parents, masters, magistrates, and Christ; Oecumenius and Bede on dignitaries and angels; the majority modern reading (cf. Jude 8-9, ESV "glorious ones") takes them as angelic beings, probably fallen ones. Host: lean angelic, Calvin's application still holds.
2. Why the donkey? Calvin: the angel showed himself to the donkey first, "as the greatest indignity." Henry: even a miracle did not turn him.
