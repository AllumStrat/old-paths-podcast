## 1. SETTING AND BACKGROUND

### Ezekiel 40-41
- Date (40:1): 25th year of "our exile" (Jehoiachin's, 597 B.C.) and the 14th year after the city was struck (586). That is about 573 B.C. Keil reckons 575, Cambridge 572, and Henry 574. Only 29:17 (571) is later (Cambridge).
- The last dated oracle before this was 32:17 and 33:21. The interval is about twelve years.
- "At the beginning of the year, on the tenth day of the month": Ellicott, Keil and Pulpit take it as Nisan, the tenth being the day the Passover lamb was chosen (Exodus 12:2-3). Barnes and Hitzig suggest a jubilee year and the Day of Atonement (Tishri 10); Keil and Ellicott reject this. Joshua 4:19: the people came up out of the Jordan on the tenth day of the first month (host observation from Scripture).
- "On that very day" (40:1): Gregory sees mercy shown on the same day as wrath (SermonIndex translation, paraphrase only).
- Henry: "See how seasonably the clearest and fullest prospects of their deliverance were given, when they were in the depth of their distress" (Henry is resting, so paraphrase).
- Structure: 40-48 is one vision (Henry). 40-42 the temple; 43 the glory returns; 44 the priests; 45-46 land and prince; 47 the river; 48 the tribes and the city "The Lord Is There." Chapter 40 has the outer wall, east gate, outer court, north and south gates, inner court gates, tables, priests' chambers and the porch. Chapter 41 has the nave, the Most Holy Place, side chambers, the western building, decoration and the table.
- Bookends: chapter 1 (Chebar), chapters 8-11 (Jerusalem's abominations, glory leaves 11:23), now 40 (high mountain). JFB: "there, the vision was calculated to inspire terror; here, hope and assurance."
- Measures (Reformation Study Bible, 40:5): a short cubit of about 17.4 inches and a long cubit of about 20.4 inches. "The rod was about ten feet long."
- Archaeology (Reformation Study Bible, 40:9): "These gateways resemble gates of the Solomonic period unearthed in excavations at Hazor, Megiddo, and Gezer (1 Kin. 9:15). The way through the center of the gate was flanked by three guardrooms on each side." URL: https://classic.biblegateway.com/resources/reformation-study-bible/Ezek.40.9 (BAS page on the Gezer gate is blocked, 403. The search summary noted that the Megiddo gate's dating is disputed, Fargo, BAR 1983, so say "resemble" only.)
- Steps: seven to the outer gates (40:22, 26), eight to the inner (40:31, 34, 37), and ten to the porch (40:49, following the LXX). Keil: "ten steps in front of the porch answering very well to the eight steps before the gateway of the inner court, and to the seven steps in front of the gateway of the outer court". Gill notes the same ascent.
- Most Holy Place, 20 by 20 cubits, the same as Solomon's (1 Kings 6:20; Poole, Gill, Ellicott). RSB 41:4: a cube, compare Revelation 21:16.
- 41:3-4: "he went into the inner room." JFB: "The angel went in and measured it, while Ezekiel stood in front, in the only part of the temple accessible to him." Fairbairn: the Most Holy Place stood "in a sense by itself, and into which the prophet did not enter with the angel."
- 41:18-19: cherubim with two faces, a man and a young lion, alternating with palm trees, as in Solomon's temple (1 Kings 6:29). Ellicott: four faces in chapter 1, two here for carving. Poole: palms are emblems of victory. Host: cherubim and palms recall Eden (Genesis 3:24) and the garden.
- 41:22: an altar of wood, "the table that is before the Lord." Ellicott, Poole and Pulpit: the altar of incense. The RSB and Cambridge: the table of the bread of the Presence called an altar. Pulpit/Plumptre: wood only, no gold, perhaps because of an impoverished people (paraphrase).
- Missing furniture: Fairbairn notes no ark, no lampstand mentioned. Jeremiah 3:16 (via Havernick in Fairbairn): the ark "shall not come to mind."

### Job 15:1-16
- Round two of the dialogues begins (15-21). Eliphaz speaks first again, as the "most respectable, most influential, and perhaps oldest of the friends" (Keil, paraphrase).
- Henry's three parts of chapter 15: reproves Job (2-13), persuades him to humble himself (14-16), and lectures on the fate of the wicked (17-35, tomorrow).
- 15:2 "windy knowledge," Hebrew da'at ruach, "knowledge of wind" (Benson, Barnes). East wind, qadim, is scorching and destructive (Barnes, JFB, Poole).
- 15:4 "doing away with the fear of God and hindering meditation" (Henry: religion summed up in fearing God and praying).
- 15:7-8 "Are you the first man who was born? Or were you brought forth before the hills?" Henry links it to Wisdom in Proverbs 8:23-25. "Have you listened in the council of God?"
- 15:10 the gray-haired. Henry: "We have the fathers on our side" (Henry's paraphrase of Eliphaz).
- 15:11 "the comforts of God... the word that deals gently with you." Cambridge: Eliphaz describes his own teaching and "the gentle and conciliatory manner of his own first speech." Keil: l'at means "gently," like the softly flowing Siloah (Isaiah 8:6).
- 15:14-16 restate Eliphaz's night oracle (4:17-19) and echo Job's own 14:1, 4 ("born of a woman"; "Who can bring a clean thing out of an unclean?").

### John 11:38-44
- Bethany. The tomb is a cave with a stone (11:38). This is the seventh and climactic sign before the Passion. 11:4: "it is for the glory of God." 11:40 picks it up.
- Four days (11:39): Chrysostom says Martha's objection is part of the evidence.
- Grave clothes: Calvin says Jewish practice was a shroud with the head wrapped separately. Contrast 20:6-7, where Jesus' cloths are left lying.

### 1 John 3:19-24

- 3:20: a textual and grammatical question about "for" (Ligonier). Two readings, more rigorous or more merciful.
- 3:23: the first use of "believe" in the letter. One "commandment" (singular) with two parts.

## 3. INTERPRETIVE QUESTIONS

Ezekiel:
1. What is this temple? Fairbairn's four classes are (1) historico-literal (Solomon's, Villalpandus, Grotius); (2) historico-ideal (what the returnees should have built, Eichhorn); (3) "Jewish-carnal," a literal future rebuilding with sacrifices at Messiah's coming (some Jewish writers and "a considerable party" in England); and (4) "Christian-spiritual, or typical," which "From the Fathers downwards, this has been the prevailing view in the Christian church... Luther, Calvin". Fairbairn's own view is (4), with the vision as an ideal picture in the historical mould. Evidence: the "visions of God" form (Fairbairn, Ellicott); dimensions (Lightfoot); the high mountain (Ellicott: "cannot apply literally to the hill of Moriah"); the boundaries; the river; and Hebrews and John 4. Gregory: "cannot be understood according to the letter." Ligonier: the returnees were never condemned for not building it; the dimensions look symbolic. Henry: it encouraged them to build another temple, and it also points to the gospel church (a combined view). The host takes view 4 with Henry's first element. NT: Revelation 21.
2. Who is the bronze man? RSB: "an angelic guide." Henry: "not a created angel, but Jesus Christ." Spurgeon 1578: "none other than our divine Lord." Gregory: Christ. Henry Concise: "not clear." The host leaves it open.
3. Why doesn't Ezekiel enter the Most Holy Place? Fairbairn: its peculiar sacredness. The host points to the question of access, to be followed up in 43-44 (the east gate shut, 44:2).

Job:
1. Is Eliphaz wrong? His theology in 15:14-16 is true (Henry: "his doctrine (for it is sound)... misapplied to Job"). Gregory: the friends say true things (Paul quotes Eliphaz, 1 Corinthians 3:19), but not rightly against Job.
2. Did Job "do away with the fear of God"? No (Henry: "This charge was utterly false"). But Eliphaz infers it from Job's words (Henry: charging consequences the opponent abhors).

John:
1. Why the stone and the unbinding by others? Chrysostom: the witnesses' senses. Calvin: the same. Augustine: allegory of the church loosing sins. Calvin rejects auricular confession from this text.
2. Why pray aloud? 11:42. Chrysostom 64: Christ did not need the prayer as an impulse, since he worked other miracles without prayer. Calvin: thanks for an already-granted prayer made in the groaning.

1 John:
1. Is "God is greater than our heart" a comfort or a warning? Calvin: "with reference to judgment... judges more severely." Spurgeon exposition: "what must be your position before the bar." Ligonier "God is Greater" (2005): comfort, "the ultimate basis for our assurance." Pulpit: "Neither the one nor the other... An awful thought for the impenitent, a blessed and encouraging thought for the penitent."
2. Does "because we keep his commandments" make answered prayer a reward? Calvin: an inseparable addition, not a cause (the sun and heat). Augustine: Paul's thorn, "heard for salvation, when he was not heard according to his wish."
