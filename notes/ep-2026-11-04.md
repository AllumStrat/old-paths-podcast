## 1. SETTING AND BACKGROUND

### Ezekiel 20-21
- Date: 20:1 "seventh year, fifth month, tenth day" of Jehoiachin's exile = Ab (July/August) 591 BC. Ellicott: "a little more than two years (two years, one month, and five days) after Ezekiel's call... or a little less than a year (exactly eleven months and five days...)" after 8:1. Gill: month Ab, "on this day afterwards Jerusalem was twice destroyed, first by the Chaldeans, and then by the Romans" (Gill, Bible Hub). Jeremiah 52:12 dates the burning of the temple to the tenth day of the fifth month.
- Place in book: chapters 1-24 oracles against Judah before the fall; 20:1 opens the third dated block (1:1; 8:1; 20:1; 24:1). Chapters 20-23 are a sequence of indictments (20 history, 21 sword, 22 bloody city, 23 two sisters). Elders sit before Ezekiel again as in 8:1 and 14:1.
- King: Zedekiah (597-586). Egypt intriguing; Babylon will march in 588. 21:18-23 imagines Nebuchadnezzar choosing between Rabbah of Ammon and Jerusalem, both rebels (Ammon in Jeremiah 27:3 conspiracy; Baalis in Jeremiah 40:14).
- Archaeology: Babylonian clay liver models used for hepatoscopy. Science Museum Group (copy of British Museum original, 2050-1750 BCE): "The liver of an animal was interpreted in a practice called hepatoscopy. Specific areas of the body were examined closely and small sticks were placed in the hole of a corresponding clay model, like this one. The pattern of the sticks was examined and interpreted by priests and diviners." URL: https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co85238/copy-of-clay-liver-used-for-divination-original-from-babylon-2050-1750-bce
- Keil on 21:21: hepatoscopy "a species of divination to which great importance was attached by both the Babylonians" https://biblehub.com/commentaries/kad/ezekiel/21.htm ; Ellicott: "familiar to every reader of classical literature."
- Numbering: ESV 20:45-49 = Hebrew 21:1-5.

### Job 7
- Job's first reply (6-7) to Eliphaz; chapter 7 turns from the friends (6) to God directly (7:7 "Remember", 7:12ff "you"). Poetry; four strophes. 7:17-18 a parody of Psalm 8:4-5 (Keil: "in some degree a parody on Psalm 8:5"). 7:20 "watcher of mankind," notser ha'adam (Keil: "the strict and constant observer of men"; KJV "preserver of men"). 7:12 sea or tannin, sea monster; Keil suggests the Nile and the crocodile. 7:6 shuttle; tiqvah is hope (and cord in Joshua 2:18; host's lexical note).
### John 8:48-59
- Feast of Booths, temple treasury (8:20), end of the long dispute 8:12-59. Climax of ego eimi (8:24, 28, 58). Stoning for blasphemy (Leviticus 24:16). "Jesus hid himself and went out of the temple" - compare glory departing temple in Ezekiel 10-11 (episode 16) - real echo? John 8:59 leaving the temple; mention lightly in close? Keep out unless short.
### 2 Peter 3:10-18
- End of the letter; Peter's last words (1:14). 3:10 "exposed" (Greek found; some manuscripts burned up, ESV note). 3:12 hastening. 3:15-16 Paul's letters set with "the other Scriptures." 3:18 doxology to Christ.

## 3. INTERPRETIVE QUESTIONS
- Ezekiel 20:25: what are the statutes not good? Fathers (Justin Dial. 21; Irenaeus AH 4.15.1): ceremonial law given for hardness. Calvin, Keil, Poole: idolatrous practices God gave them over to; v.26. Gill lists views.
- Ezekiel 20:37 rod and bond: judgment (Calvin) or grace (Spurgeon); Henry both.
- Ezekiel 21:3-4 righteous and wicked vs 9:4 and 18:20: temporal calamity vs eternal distinction (Henry, Gill).
- Ezekiel 21:27 who is he? Genesis 49:10 (Keil, Ligonier, Henry, Gill).
- Job 7: does Job sin here? Keil: "hurried forward to the use of sinful language"; Spurgeon 2206: weaknesses near strength.
- John 8:58 eternal being vs office (Augustine vs Calvin's broader reading which still includes essence).
- 2 Peter 3:10 annihilation or renewal (Augustine, Calvin: renewal; Strain: resurrection analogy). 3:12 how hasten (Strain).
