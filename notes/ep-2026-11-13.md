## 1. SETTING AND BACKGROUND

### Ezekiel 38-39
- Undated. Sits after the news of the fall (33:21, tenth month of the twelfth year, about January 585 B.C.) and before 40:1 (twenty-fifth year of exile, 573 B.C.). Henry dates the section "b.c. 585".
- Structure of the book: 1-24 judgment on Jerusalem; 25-32 nations; 33-48 hope. Within 33-48: watchman and shepherds (33-34), land and new heart (35-36), bones and two sticks (37), Gog (38-39), temple and land (40-48). Gog comes after Israel "dwell securely" (38:8, 11, 14; 39:26): the restored people face a last assault before the temple vision. Poole: God forewarns them lest they expect uninterrupted prosperity after return. Henry: "in the world they shall have tribulation".
- Seven oracles marked by "Thus says the Lord God" (38:3, 10, 14, 17; 39:1, 17, 25).
- Names: Magog son of Japheth (Genesis 10:2), between Gomer (Cimmerians) and Madai (Medes). Josephus (Antiquities 1.6.1) and Jerome: Scythians. Ellicott: Scythians took Sardis 629 B.C., spread in Media 624, bribed off from Egypt by Psammetichus, driven back 596. Gog: Pulpit and Barnes note Gyges of Lydia appears as Gu-gu in Assyrian records (Ashurbanipal); Ellicott notes George Smith's identification of Ga-a-gi, a Saka chief. Meshech and Tubal = Moschians and Tibarenes south of the Black Sea (Herodotus 3.94, 7.78; Pulpit); Assyrian Mushki and Tabal in Anatolia. Beth-togarmah, "uttermost parts of the north". Persia, Cush (Ethiopia), Put (Libya) at east and south edges. Keil: north, east, south, "on the borders of the then known world".
- Rosh: ESV text "chief prince"; footnote "prince of Rosh". LXX takes Rosh as a proper name; Jerome, Aquila, Targum take it as "chief". Ellicott: attempt to derive "Russian" "cannot be considered as sufficiently supported". Pulpit: Hengstenberg protested that it "deals hardly with the poor Russians". Ligonier devotional rejects the Russia reading.
- 38:12 "center of the earth" = tabbur ha'arets, navel of the earth (Ellicott: "Literally, in the navel of the earth"; also 5:5 Jerusalem "in the center of the nations").
- 39:9-16 seven years burning weapons; seven months burying; Numbers 19:11ff seven days' uncleanness after contact with dead (Cambridge-type note on bh 39:9). Valley of the Travelers "east of the sea" = Dead Sea (Ellicott, Keil); Ellicott takes it as probably imaginary and as signal for nonliteral reading. Hamon-gog = multitude of Gog; Hamonah = multitude.
- 39:17-20 sacrificial feast (Isaiah 34:6; Jeremiah 46:10; Ellicott: "to fill out and heighten the description of an immense slaughter"). Revelation 19:17-18 "the great supper of God".
- Revelation 20:8 Gog and Magog after the thousand years. Keil: Ezekiel names God as the leader; Revelation names Satan as deceiver, like 2 Samuel 24:1 / 1 Chronicles 21:1.

### Job 14
- Ends Job's reply to Zophar (12-14) and the first cycle of speeches. Three movements: 1-6 man's brief troubled life, plea "look away"; 7-17 tree's hope, wish to be hidden in Sheol, question of 14:14, God calling and longing; 18-22 erosion, "so you destroy the hope of man".
- Wordplay: 14:7 "sprout again" yachalif; 14:14 "my renewal" chalifati (footnote: or relief). Keil: chaliphah = "relief, change", tsava "warfare" (soldier's service, compare 7:1). Barnes: tsava "my warfare; my enlistment; my hard service". Keil on 14:7: Job thinks especially of the palm, which sends up suckers; Isaiah 6:13 stump.
- Interpretations of 14:14: Pulpit and Barnes and Poole: question expects "no" (not in this world). Ellicott: "Why ask the question if it were absolutely certain that he would not?" Gill: no on earth; yes at the general resurrection. Cyril of Jerusalem (using Greek text) reads 14:10 as a question and 14:14 as an affirmation.

### John 11:17-37
- Bethany about two miles (fifteen stadia, ESV note). Four days: Jewish belief (later recorded) that the soul hovered three days; four days put death beyond doubt (Chrysostom Homily 63 stresses witnesses, stench). Martha holds Pharisee doctrine of last-day resurrection (Ligonier, Jordan Stone). Fifth "I am" with predicate (Stone). 11:33, 38 embrimaomai, ESV margin "was indignant"; Warfield: "irrepressible anger". Tarasso "troubled". 11:35 edakrysen, quiet tears versus klaio wailing of Mary and the Jews.
- Voices: Calvin (Martha's faith mixed with disorderly passions; I am the resurrection = spiritual and bodily; Christ "clothed himself also with human feelings" against Augustine's view that he "brought groaning... upon himself of his own accord"); Chrysostom Homily 63 ("groaned in spirit means, restrained His trouble"; weeps "to prove His human nature"); Warfield (anger at death).

### 1 John 3:11-18
- Follows 3:10 (children of God vs devil; righteousness and love as twin marks). 3:11 "the message... from the beginning" (echo 1:5 and 2:7). Cain (Genesis 4) as the first child of the evil one; envy (Augustine). 3:13 world hates you. 3:14 assurance "we know" by love; 3:15 hater = murderer (Matthew 5:21-22). 3:16 "By this we know love" (Calvin's editor: love, not "of God"); 3:17 bios = "the world's goods" (livelihood); splanchna "heart/bowels". 3:18 word/talk vs deed/truth.
- Voices: Calvin (sign, not cause; follow at a distance), Augustine Homily 5 (start with your goods; envy of Cain), Ligonier devotional "The Message of Love".

## 3. INTERPRETIVE QUESTIONS
- Ezekiel: (a) Who is Gog? Options: Scythians (Ellicott background); Antiochus Epiphanes and the Seleucids (Poole, Henry possibility); Turks or Rome (Poole, Gill); Goths (Ambrose); symbolic final enemies of the church (Augustine on Revelation 20; Ellicott on 38:17; Pulpit "ideal character"; Ligonier). Host: an ideal, composite figure for the last assault, resumed in Revelation 20. (b) Is it literal? Seven years of firewood, valley east of the sea, birds' sacrifice: Ellicott says these force a nonliteral reading. (c) Why does God bring Gog? 38:16, 23; 39:7, 21-23: that the nations may know him and understand the exile.
- Job: (a) Does 14:14 deny or hope? (b) Is Sheol a hiding place (14:13)?
- John: (a) Why weep if he will raise him in minutes? (b) What does embrimaomai mean, grief or anger? (c) What does "shall never die" mean?
- 1 John: (a) Does love save or show? (Calvin: sign, not cause). (b) Does "lay down our lives" make us redeemers? (Calvin: no, "the virtue or benefit of our death cannot be the same").
