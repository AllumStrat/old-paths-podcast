## 1. SETTING AND BACKGROUND

### Ezekiel 17-19 (deepest)
- Date: no new date heading; still inside the block from 8:1 (sixth year, sixth month, fifth day of Jehoiachin's exile = late summer 592 B.C.) to 20:1 (seventh year, fifth month, tenth day = August 591 B.C.). Ezekiel is at Tel-abib on the Chebar canal, mute except when God opens his mouth (3:26 until 33:22).
- Kings: Josiah died 609 at Megiddo. Jehoahaz (Shallum) reigned three months in 609 and was deposed and taken to Egypt by Pharaoh Neco (2 Kings 23:31-34; Jeremiah 22:10-12). Jehoiakim (Eliakim) 609-598, Egypt's appointee then Babylon's vassal; rebelled; died 598 (2 Kings 24:1-6; 2 Chronicles 36:6 says Nebuchadnezzar bound him in bronze chains to take him to Babylon). Jehoiachin (Jeconiah) reigned three months, surrendered March 597, taken to Babylon with princes and craftsmen (2 Kings 24:8-16), Ezekiel among the exiles. Zedekiah (Mattaniah), Jehoiachin's uncle and Jehoahaz's full brother (same mother Hamutal, 2 Kings 23:31; 24:18), installed by Nebuchadnezzar 597; made to swear by God (2 Chronicles 36:13). Rebelled c. 589 trusting Egypt (Jeremiah 37:5-7); Jerusalem fell 586; Zedekiah captured near Jericho, sons killed before his eyes at Riblah, blinded, taken to Babylon, died there (2 Kings 25:4-7; Jeremiah 52:11). Ezekiel 12:13 "he shall not see it, though he shall die there" (episode 16) and 17:16 "in Babylon he shall die".
- Egypt: Pharaoh Psammetichus II (595-589) and then Hophra/Apries (589-570) courted Judah. The second eagle of 17:7 is Pharaoh generically; the embassy of 17:15 "sending his ambassadors to Egypt, that they might give him horses and a large army".
- Archaeology 1 (17:4, 17:12-13; 19:9): Jehoiachin's ration tablets, Babylonian archive near the Ishtar Gate (excavated 1899-1917 by Koldewey), c. 595-570 B.C., now in Berlin (VAT 16378); list oil rations for "Ya'u-kinu, king of the land of Yahudu" and his five sons, alongside craftsmen from Arvad, Byblos and Greek lands. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehoiachin%27s_Rations_Tablets . Use: the "top of the cedar" really did end up drawing oil rations in "a city of merchants", still called king by the Babylonians, which is part of why exiles thought he would come back (Jeremiah 28:4).
- Archaeology 2 (17:15): Lachish ostracon 3 (probably shortly before Lachish fell, 588-586): "The commander of the army Coniah son of Elnatan, has gone down to go to Egypt". Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lachish_letters . A few years after chapter 17, but shows the Egypt policy Ezekiel condemned in action. Use one sentence at most.
- Structure: chapter 16 (marriage allegory) -> 17 (political allegory, same story) -> 18 (the people's excuse refuted) -> 19 (lament for the princes). 20:1 brings a new date. Chapter 17 picks up 16:59 "you who have despised the oath in breaking the covenant" (same phrase in 17:18). Chapter 17's vine continues 15's useless vine; 19:10-14 returns to the vine.
- Words: chidah (riddle, 17:2; the word of Samson's riddle, Judges 14) and mashal (parable/proverb, 17:2; same root as the proverb of 18:2 "use this proverb", moshlim et-hamashal). Rak, tender (17:22). Qinah, lament (19:1, 14). Shuv, turn (18:21, 23, 30, 32). Chayah, live (18:9, 13, 17, 19, 21-23, 28, 32; cf. 16:6 chayi).
- Law background: Deuteronomy 24:16 (fathers not put to death for children); Exodus 20:5 (visiting iniquity "of those who hate me"); 2 Kings 14:6 Amaziah's application. Jeremiah 31:29-30 quotes the same proverb and promises its end in the new covenant. Lamentations 5:7 "Our fathers sinned, and are no more; and we bear their iniquities."
- 19:10 textual note (ESV footnote): most Hebrew manuscripts read "in your blood" (cf. 16:6).
- 19:9 "cage" or "wooden collar" (ESV footnote); Poole: "a great cage, as wild beasts are conveyed"; Keil "the cage with nose-rings"; JFB: hooks through captives' noses as in Assyrian sculptures.

### Job 6
- First reply of Job, answering Eliphaz (chapters 4-5). Poetry; the second speech in the first cycle (4-14). 
- Structure: 1-7 the weight of grief and why his words were rash; 8-13 the wish to be crushed and the one comfort; 14-23 the friends as a wadi; 24-30 a plea to be taught and looked at.
- 6:4 Shaddai, the Almighty; arrows (Psalm 38:2). 6:6 tafel, tasteless (the word for whitewash in Ezekiel 13:10-15, and Lamentations 2:14). "Juice of the mallow" (ESV, Hebrew uncertain); KJV "white of an egg"; LXX "taste in vain words" (Barnes). 6:10 "I have not denied the words of the Holy One" (KJV concealed). 6:14 chesed (kindness); lammas, "to the one melting/despairing" (Barnes: mas from masas, to melt); ESV footnote: Hebrew uncertain; two readings (the one who withholds kindness forsakes the fear of the Almighty; or a despairing man needs kindness lest he forsake the fear of the Almighty). 6:15 bagad (deal treacherously) like a nachal (wadi); Jeremiah 15:18 "deceitful brook". 6:19 Tema (Arabian oasis, Isaiah 21:14) and Sheba (southern Arabia). 6:21 "you see my calamity and are afraid". 6:25-26 forceful upright words; the words of the despairing are wind.

### John 8:31-47
- Still the Feast of Booths, autumn AD 29 or so, in the treasury of the temple (8:20), same day as 8:12-30. 8:30 "many believed in him"; 8:31 addressed "to the Jews who had believed him" (perfect participle). Within 15 verses they are seeking to kill him (8:37, 40) and by 8:59 picking up stones.
- Structure: 31-32 condition and promise (abide, know, free); 33-38 slavery and the son in the house (Genesis 21:10, Isaac and Ishmael); 39-41 Abraham's children do Abraham's works; 41-47 God's children love the Son; your father the devil, murderer and liar; 47 whoever is of God hears.
- Political irony: Judea under Rome; Jewish history of bondage to Egypt and Babylon (Chrysostom).

### 2 Peter 3:1-9
- Peter's final letter, written shortly before his death (1:14), c. AD 64-68, probably from Rome; "This is now the second letter" (3:1), most naturally referring to 1 Peter. Purpose: reminder (1:12-15; 3:1-2). Chapter 2 dealt with false teachers; chapter 3 with their eschatological scoffing.
- Structure: 1-2 reminder of prophets and apostles; 3-4 scoffers and their argument from uniformity; 5-7 creation by word, flood by word, fire reserved by the same word; 8 God's time (Psalm 90:4); 9 not slow but patient, that all should reach repentance.
- Words: empaiktai (scoffers) en empaigmone; bradunei (is slow); makrothumei (is patient); "toward you" (ESV; some manuscripts "on your account"). Cross-references in ESV: 3:4 to Ezekiel 12:22, 27 and 11:3; 3:9 to Ezekiel 18:23, 32; 33:11 and 1 Timothy 2:4.

## 3. INTERPRETIVE QUESTIONS

Ezekiel
1. Why is God so angry about a broken treaty with a pagan tyrant? Answer: it was sworn in God's name (2 Chronicles 36:13), so 17:19 "my oath ... my covenant". Calvin: God as "guardian of treaties"; Psalm 15:4; the treaty was lawful because God had put Judah under Babylon (Jeremiah 27). Calvin also notes the first cause of the fall was idolatry; the oath is what men could most readily see.
2. Who is the tender sprig of 17:22? Zerubbabel as a beginning (Calvin, Poole "in the type Zorobabel, in the antitype Messiah"), Messiah in full (Keil: the Messiah himself; Ellicott: nearly all interpreters, Jewish and Christian; Gill cites the Targum). Calvin warns Christians not to force every restoration text onto Christ alone, but says this one "without doubt refers to Christ" in its fullness. Host: Calvin's both-and.
3. Does Ezekiel 18 contradict Exodus 20:5? Poole and Gill: God visits iniquity on children who follow their fathers' sins ("of those who hate me"). Henry: temporal calamities may fall on children, eternal death never for another's sin. Calvin: we are not condemned in Adam as innocent in ourselves (original sin is our own corruption).
4. Does "he shall surely live" (18:9) teach salvation by works? Henry's Concise (paraphrase): the question here is not the meritorious ground of justification but God's dealings with the righteous and wicked; JFB: "righteous" not absolutely. Righteousness in 18:9 is covenant faithfulness "acting faithfully" (emunah). 18:22 "none of the transgressions ... remembered" shows forgiveness is the ground.
5. HARD QUESTION: "Make yourselves a new heart" (18:31) when God says "I will give you a new heart" (11:19; 36:26); and "I have no pleasure in the death of anyone" (18:32) when not all are saved. Augustine: he gives what he commands (On Grace and Free Will 31). Calvin: command shows duty not ability; God's will revealed in his word vs secret counsel; "God takes up the character of a mourner". Henry (quoting Augustine's Latin): God does not command impossibilities but admonishes to do what you can and pray for what you cannot. Spurgeon 1795: question, assertion, oath (18:23, 18:32, 33:11); judge who hates punishing. Sproul: no pleasure, yet decrees; judge sentencing his own son with tears.
6. Who is the second lion of 19:5-9? Keil: Jehoiachin (taken to Babylon in 597 fits verse 9 and the ration tablets). Gill and others: Jehoiakim (2 Chronicles 36:6). Calvin interprets the first as Jehoahaz. Host: Jehoiachin.
Job
1. Was Job wrong to speak rashly (6:3)? He admits it; Henry: his passionate expressions "though they could not be justified, might be excused" (paraphrase). The friends should not "reprove words" of a despairing man (6:26).
2. 6:14 textual question (above).
3. Gregory's Vulgate reading of 6:2 "my sins" and his mystical reading of the Mediator as the balance (quote available) versus the Hebrew "vexation". Use only if room.
John
1. Were "the Jews who had believed him" true believers? Chrysostom: they had believed but did not continue (paraphrase). Ryle notes: "this 'believing' was not faith of the heart" (paraphrase). Point: 8:31's condition is the test.
2. What is "the truth" that frees? Chrysostom: Christ himself. Ryle: "cannot ... mean the Personal Truth" but the whole doctrinal truth about Christ. Host: in John the two cannot be pulled apart (14:6; 17:17).
3. "We were not born of sexual immorality" (8:41): JFB, not an illegitimate race in religion (idolatry as harlotry, Hosea 2:4; Ezekiel 16); Meyer's note on Bible Hub records Euthymius Zigabenus and others hearing an allusion to Jesus' birth. Mention both briefly.
2 Peter
1. Does 3:8 give a timetable (world-week)? Irenaeus yes (Against Heresies 5.28.3); Spurgeon No. 447 no.
2. Who are "any" and "all" in 3:9? Gill: the elect, "us-ward"; Calvin: God's revealed will in the gospel toward mankind, not his hidden purpose. Host: Calvin, because Peter echoes Ezekiel 18:23, 32.
3. Why delay? Patience toward repentance (Calvin: God is not tardy).
