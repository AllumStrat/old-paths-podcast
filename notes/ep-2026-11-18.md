## 1. SETTING AND BACKGROUND

### Daniel 1-2
- Date: "In the third year of the reign of Jehoiakim" (1:1) = 605 B.C. Jeremiah 25:1 and 46:2 call 605 the fourth year of Jehoiakim and first of Nebuchadnezzar. JFB (biblehub daniel/1-1): Jeremiah counts the accession year, Daniel does not [Jahn]; also Fairbairn: deportation end of third year shortly before Carchemish. Cambridge: "king of Babylon" used proleptically. Keil argues it must be fourth year.
- Babylonian Chronicle ABC 5 (livius.org, https://www.livius.org/sources/content/mesopotamian-chronicles-content/abc-5-jerusalem-chronicle/): Nebuchadnezzar as crown prince defeated the Egyptians at Carchemish ("beat them to non-existence"), conquered Hamath; Nabopolassar died 8 Abu (15 Aug 605); "on 1 Ululu [7 September 605.] he sat on the royal throne in Babylon"; in the accession year went back to Hatti-land and took heavy tribute. Same chronicle records capture of Jerusalem 597 (Ezekiel's deportation).
- Pulpit (daniel/2-1): Berosus says Nebuchadnezzar hurried across the desert; Wieseler: reign counted from the following new year, so "second year" (2:1) can fall in the third year of training. Barnes: co-regency solution.
- Ligonier "Daniel is Taken to Babylon" (https://learn.ligonier.org/devotionals/daniel-taken-babylon): Daniel ministered 605-539/538; contemporary of Jeremiah and Ezekiel; critics date the book 400 years later because of antisupernaturalism; quotes Calvin: Nebuchadnezzar did not conquer by his own valor but because God wished to humble his people.
- Ezekiel names Daniel at 14:14, 20 (with Noah and Job) and 28:3 ("you are indeed wiser than Daniel") - JFB notes on 1:8.
- Languages: Hebrew 1:1-2:4a; Aramaic from 2:4b ("in Aramaic") to 7:28; Hebrew 8-12. Ellicott (daniel/2-4): "God is about to reveal facts connected with the Gentile world, and therefore a Gentile language is used as the vehicle of the revelation." JFB: Aramaic portion treats "the course, character, and crisis of the Gentile power". Pulpit: Aramaic was the everyday language of Mesopotamia (Winckler), Jeremiah 10:11 Aramaic verse.
- Names (Barnes daniel/1-7; Ligonier "Court of a Pagan King"): Daniel "God is my judge"; Hananiah "the Lord is gracious"; Mishael "who is what God is"; Azariah "whom Jehovah helps" (Barnes); Belteshazzar connected with Bel (Barnes: "Bel's prince"; Ligonier: "may a god protect his life"); Abednego "servant of Nego/Nebo" (Barnes). Daniel 4:8 Nebuchadnezzar: named after his god.
- Shinar (1:2): Jerome, the land of the tower of Babel; only part of the vessels taken.
- Ch. 2: Ligonier "A Dream of a Statue" (https://learn.ligonier.org/devotionals/dream-statue): Babylonians looked for omens in stars, dreams, animal livers; anyone could invent a meaning; giving the dream itself was a sign of inspiration.
- raz (Aramaic, "secret, mystery"): biblehub.com/hebrew/7328.htm, Persian loanword; Daniel 2:18, 19, 27, 28, 29, 30, 47 (twice), and 4:6.
- ga'al (1:8 "defile"): Barnes (daniel/1-8): "commonly used in connection with 'redemption,'... In later Hebrew, however, it means, to be defiled".

## 3. INTERPRETIVE QUESTIONS

Daniel:
1. Why did Daniel refuse the king's food? (a) Mosaic food laws/blood (Gill, Poole reason 1, Cambridge). (b) Food and wine consecrated to idols (Keil, Ellicott, Benson, JFB, MacLaren). (c) Not unclean at all; refused as a snare to assimilation, to remember his people (Calvin; Geneva notes; Poole reasons 3-4). (d) To display God's power (Ligonier "Court of a Pagan King", which rejects (a) because wine is clean and (b) because vegetables would also have been offered). Host: (c) with (b): eating the king's portion daily ties you to the king; Daniel took the education, kept the name in his heart, drew the line at the table. Note 10:3 Daniel later eats meat and wine (Gill) - consistent with Calvin.
2. The four kingdoms: Babylon, Medo-Persia, Greece, Rome (Calvin, Jerome, Henry, Ligonier). Critical view (Media separate, Greece fourth; Porphyry per Jerome) mentioned in one sentence at most.
3. The stone "cut by no human hand": Jerome: virgin birth. Calvin: restricting it to the birth is unnecessary; it means divinely sent, a kingdom not of earth. Henry: the stone the builders refused because not cut by their hands. When does it strike? Calvin: Messiah's kingdom at close of fourth monarchy; JFB: cannot be first advent (toes); host: kingdom set up "in the days of those kings" - in Christ's first coming, growing to fill earth, consummated at the last (Luke 20:18 allusion).

Job: Is Bildad wrong? His doctrine (the wicked perish) is true in the end (Gregory), but false as a reading of this life and false of Job.

John: 1. Why does Jesus answer the Greeks with the grain? 2. "All people" in 12:32: all without exception? Augustine: "all things" (Latin), or all foreordained; Calvin: Greek MSS read all men; children of God from Jews and Gentiles (with Chrysostom).

1 John: What is the sin that leads to death? Views (Derek Thomas): apostasy of a professing believer (Thomas, Marshall); unbelievers both (Stott); physical death of a believer (Warfield); believer vs unbeliever. Tertullian (Montanist): adultery and fornication irremissible. Calvin: apostasy, total defection; do not rashly conclude; love hopes well. Ligonier devotional: eternal death, final apostasy/blasphemy of the Spirit; be careful not to hastily assume.
