## 1. SETTING AND BACKGROUND

### Ezekiel 4-8 (deepest)
- Date: Ezekiel's call 1:1-2, fifth day, fourth month, fifth year of Jehoiachin's exile = summer 593 B.C. Chapter 8:1 = sixth year, sixth month, fifth day = late summer 592 B.C. (Calvin: "an interval of a year and two months"; Keil: 413 days on a lunar reckoning).
- King in Jerusalem: Zedekiah, Babylon's appointee, already drifting toward Egypt and rebellion (Jeremiah 27-28, 37). Exiles in Babylon hoping for a quick return; Jerusalem's remaining population boasting that they were the good figs (cf. Jeremiah 24; Calvin on 4:4: "the Jews were more obstinate because God had spared them" and the exiles "grieved at their obedience").
- 3:22-27: glory appears in the valley; Ezekiel to shut himself in his house, cords placed on him, tongue to cling to roof of mouth, mute except when God opens his mouth. Chapters 4-5 are therefore preached mostly in silence, by sign-acts (Henry: "as his silence had a voice").
- Structure: 4:1-3 the brick and siege; 4:4-8 lying on sides; 4:9-17 siege rations and defiled bread; 5:1-4 the razor and hair; 5:5-17 interpretation ("This is Jerusalem"); 6 oracle against the mountains of Israel (high places), with remnant (6:8-10); 7 "the end" oracle against the land; 8 the temple vision, which runs through chapter 11 (glory departs in 10-11).
- Background: Ligonier notes ancient Near Eastern peoples engraved city plans on bricks. Twenty shekels about eight ounces (Ligonier). Sixth of a hin a little over a pint.
- Shaphan: Josiah's secretary who read the rediscovered law (2 Kings 22:8-10); sons Ahikam (protected Jeremiah, Jeremiah 26:24), Elasah (carried Jeremiah's letter, 29:3), Gemariah (whose chamber Baruch read the scroll in, 36:10); grandson Gedaliah (40:5). Jaazaniah son of Shaphan in 8:11 (Keil: Shaphan "probably the person mentioned as a man of distinction in 2 Kings 22:3"; distinct from the Jaazaniah of 11:1).
- Tammuz: Mesopotamian Dumuzi; Jerome identified with Adonis (Keil quoting Jerome); Calvin preferred Osiris. Fourth month named Tammuz, the month of 1:1.
- Image of jealousy: likely the Asherah image Manasseh put in the temple (2 Kings 21:7), removed by Josiah, apparently restored (Henry, Keil).
- Mishnah Sukkah 5:4: at the Feast of Booths, the priests reached the east gate, turned west to face the sanctuary, and recited Ezekiel 8:16 then "and we, our eyes are to God."

### Job 2
- Second heavenly council, repeating 1:6-8 nearly word for word, then "he still holds fast his integrity" (tummah), the word his wife will throw back at him in 2:9.
- 2:3 "you incited me against him to destroy him without reason": hinnam, the same word as Satan's "for nothing?" in 1:9 (Gill: "in vain"; Poole: "to destroy him in vain, or without effect, or to no purpose").
- Sores "from the sole of his foot to the crown of his head" (cf. Deuteronomy 28:35). Potsherd and ashes (the town ash heap; Gregory "dunghill").
- Friends: Eliphaz the Temanite (Teman in Edom, famed for wisdom, Jeremiah 49:7), Bildad the Shuhite (Shuah, son of Abraham and Keturah, Genesis 25:2), Zophar the Naamathite.

### John 7:45-53
- Last day of Booths. Officers (hyperetai, temple police) sent in 7:32 return empty-handed. Sanhedrin members (chief priests and Pharisees). Nicodemus (3:1-2) appeals to the law's demand for a hearing (cf. Deuteronomy 1:16-17). "No prophet arises from Galilee": Jonah son of Amittai from Gath-hepher in Zebulun (2 Kings 14:25; Ellicott, Gill, Cambridge). 7:53-8:11 absent from the earliest manuscripts (ESV note); tomorrow.

### 2 Peter 1:12-21
- Peter near death (1:14), mid-60s, probably Rome. 1:12 "established" (esterigmenous) from sterizo (Luke 22:32 "strengthen your brothers"). 1:13-14 "body" is literally tent (skenoma); 1:15 "departure" exodos, the word of Luke 9:31 at the Transfiguration. 1:16-18 Transfiguration (Matthew 17, Mark 9, Luke 9). 1:19-21 the prophetic word; 1:21 "carried along" pheromenoi, same verb as the voice "borne" in 1:17-18; Acts 27:15, 17 of a ship driven by wind (Barnes, Pulpit).
- auchmeros "dark": properly dry, squalid (Ellicott, JFB, Pulpit on Bible Hub 2 Peter 1:19).

## 3. INTERPRETIVE QUESTIONS
Ezekiel:
1. Did Ezekiel literally lie 390 + 40 days? Calvin: by vision only ("this was not in reality completed"; Ezekiel 8:1 also "a prophetic vision"). Henry: really done, "every day ... at a certain time of the day, when he received visits." Gill sets out both (Kimchi and Maimonides for vision; "others are of opinion that all this was really done"; the chronological objection). Keil: 413 days available vs 437 needed; Hitzig's leap-year solution "purely conjectural"; Kliefoth says all internal. Host: public, real, set hours (4:12 "in their sight"; 3:25 cords; 4:3 "a sign for the house of Israel").
2. What do 390 and 40 mean? Calvin and Henry: Israel's sin from Jeroboam's revolt, Judah's last forty years (Henry: from Josiah's 13th year, Jeremiah's call). Ligonier: 390 from about 976 under Solomon; 40 = a generation. Keil: 430 recalls Egypt (Exodus 12:40), a new bondage. Poole: whatever the count, "God's wonderful patience."
3. "Bear their punishment" (nasa avon): type of Christ? Calvin: "an expiation is not here described"; Keil: "not to be viewed as vicarious and mediatorial ... purely epideictic and symbolical." Host: agree, he displays the burden; Isaiah 53:12 and 1 Peter 2:24 show the one who bears it away.
4. 6:9 nishbarti: God broken? Henry: grieved like a husband; Calvin: two senses, prefers worn out by obstinacy; also "may refer this to pardon."
5. 8:17 branch to nose: uncertain (Calvin lists explanations, rejects the scribal-emendation idea, prefers "put forth a branch to their wrath"). Leave open.
Job:
1. Can God be "incited"? Gregory: "because we ourselves never strike unless when moved, the stroke of God itself is called the moving Him." Gill/Poole: hinnam = in vain.
2. Job's wife: Gregory hard on her (ladder); Spurgeon "however she meant it"; Gill "intimates that she was a good woman ... but now spake not like herself." Henry: likely well affected to religion, couldn't bear the loss.
3. "Did not sin with his lips": Bible Hub note "There might be risings of corruption in his heart, but grace had the upper hand."
John:
1. Was Nicodemus a believer here? Calvin: "a neutral man ... is not a true disciple of Christ" yet the seed later "acquired new and sudden vigor from the death of Christ." Augustine: "not indeed as being himself unbelieving, but timid." Host: timid seed; John 19:39.
2. No prophet from Galilee: false historically (Jonah).
2 Peter:
1. "More sure" word: Calvin, more sure to Jews who revered the prophets; Reformation Study Bible, Scripture "a more solid proof than even the spectacular experience"; Piper, "more sure" because "confirmed on the mount of transfiguration." Host: ESV reading (confirmed), plus the RSB point for us.
2. "Until the day dawns": Calvin, the final day ("when we shall see face to face"); Augustine Tractate 35, lamps no longer needed in that day; Piper: "dawn without and daystar within."
3. 1:20 private interpretation: Calvin and RSB, about origin not reader.
