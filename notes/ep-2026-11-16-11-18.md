## 1. Setting and background

### Ezekiel 45-47 (deepest)
- Date: within the vision dated in 40:1, "in the twenty-fifth year of our exile... in the fourteenth year after the city was struck down" = about April 573 B.C. Nebuchadnezzar II reigns in Babylon; Jerusalem in ruins since 586. Ezekiel among the 597 exiles at the Chebar canal.
- Structure: 40-48 is the final vision: 40-42 temple measured; 43 glory returns (43:1-5), "place of my throne... forever" (43:7); 44 shut east gate, prince, Zadokite priests; 45-46 land and worship ordered around the house: holy district (45:1-6), prince's land (45:7-8), justice for princes (45:9-12), offerings and feasts provided by the prince (45:13-46:15), prince's inheritance law (46:16-18), sacrificial kitchens (46:19-24); 47:1-12 river from the temple; 47:13-23 borders, sojourners inherit; 48 tribal allotments and city "The Lord is there".
- Previous chapter end (44:28-31): "I am their inheritance... I am their possession" for priests.
- Background: 45:9 "evictions" = literally expulsion (Ellicott, with allusion to 1 Kings 21, Naboth). 46:18 JFB and Poole also name Ahab and Naboth. Prince's land fixed so that "my princes shall no more oppress my people" (45:8).
- 45:18-25: new-year cleansing of sanctuary, Passover with a bull sin offering for prince and people, offerings far richer than Mosaic (Ellicott; Barnes: deviation shows symbolic vision).
- 46:9: Ellicott and JFB: crowd order at the feasts. Pulpit reports Hengstenberg: reason "a theological one... to signify that each should go out of the sanctuary another man than he came in." Poole: none might turn their back on the temple.
- 47: Jerusalem has no river (MacLaren). Gihon/Siloam flow from rock below temple hill (Barnes; Isaiah 8:6). Tradition of the Etam aqueduct into the temple (Barnes, Poole, Gill, Henry: but this is the reverse, water flowing out). Route impossible over the Mount of Olives (Ligonier); Dead Sea so salty nothing lives (Ligonier). En-gedi, "the fountain of the goat," a copious spring midway on the western shore (Ellicott); En-eglaim uncertain (Jerome: near Jordan mouth; Ain el-Feshkhah; or east shore). Southern salt flats left encrusted with salt after floods (Ellicott, Pulpit/Robinson).
- Hebrew: nachalayim (47:9) is dual, "two rivers"; Hengstenberg, Keil, Plumptre: dual of intensification, "double river" (Pulpit; Ellicott). mey afsayim "water of ankles" (Keil). 47:2 rare verb pakah, "ripple" or trickle (Keil; Ligonier "trickling"). nasi = prince/leader (Fairbairn: the earthly head has "only the name of the prince", God occupies the throne).
- 47:22-23: sojourners who have children among you inherit with the tribes, "an entirely new feature... added to the Mosaic law" (Ellicott). Ligonier: in the restoration Gentile believers are no longer second-class citizens.
- Revelation 22:1-2 is "evidently founded upon this passage" (Ellicott); John 7:38 "as the Scripture has said".

### Job 16
- Round two of the dialogue (15-21). Eliphaz's second speech (15) just ended. Job answers (16-17). Setting: patriarchal era, land of Uz. Structure: 16:1-5 to friends ("miserable comforters", literally comforters of trouble; "windy words" answering Eliphaz's 15:2); 16:6-17 God's assault (worn out, shriveled, torn, gnashed teeth, struck on cheek, seized by neck, target for archers, breach upon breach, warrior; sackcloth; prayer pure); 16:18-22 cry: earth do not cover my blood (Abel, Gen 4:10; Keil), witness in heaven, advocate, "a few years... the way from which I shall not return".
- 16:19 "he who testifies for me" = sahadi, Aramaic equivalent of 'ed "witness" (Cambridge; Jegar-sahadutha Gen 31:47); "advocate or sponsor" (Cambridge).
- Anticipates Job 19:25-27 (Ellicott, Pulpit).

### John 12:1-11
- Six days before Passover (Saturday before Passion week, traditionally). Bethany under two miles from Jerusalem; after the plot of 11:53 and the question "will he come to the feast?" (11:56; Calvin links). Parallel Matthew 26:6-13, Mark 14:3-9 (house of Simon the leper; Calvin holds same event; head and feet). Pound of pure nard, 300 denarii, about a year's wages (Ligonier devotional). Luke 7 is a different anointing (Ligonier).
- Structure: 1-3 dinner and anointing; 4-6 Judas' objection and John's explanation (thief, moneybag); 7-8 Jesus' defense; 9-11 crowd comes to see Lazarus; priests plan to kill Lazarus.
- Place in John: closes the Lazarus sign (11) and opens Passion week; "the hour" approaches (12:23).

### 1 John 4:7-21
- Letter written late first century (traditionally from Ephesus) to churches unsettled by secessionists who denied Christ came in the flesh (4:1-6). Third cycle of John's tests (truth, love, obedience). 4:7-21 is the letter's fullest treatment of love: source (7-10), obligation (11-12), assurance through Spirit and confession (13-16), confidence at judgment (17-18), proof in brotherly love (19-21).
- Key words: agape; hilasmos "propitiation" (4:10, also 2:2); teleioo "perfected" (4:12, 17, 18).

## 3. Interpretive questions

Ezekiel
1. Who is the prince? Gill (45:22): "Christ himself is this Prince". Henry (45:16): "Some read it to the prince, and understand it of Christ" but applies the prince to Christian magistrates. JFB (46:16-18): "The mention of the prince's sons is another argument against Messiah being meant by 'the prince.'" Fairbairn: not collective (Havernick), not simply Messiah: "utterly irreconcileable... with those requiring the presentation of sacrifices and sin-offerings for the prince"; to be explained "as part of an ideal description of coming realities under the form and aspect of the old relations". Poole on 45:22: "Prepare for himself, to expiate his own sins." Host: a real, ideal Davidic ruler drawn in old-covenant shapes; the sin offering shows he still needs atonement; the vision is honest about the limits of every earthly prince.
2. Literal or symbolic river? Ellicott: "The ideal character of this vision of the waters is so plain upon its face"; Henry: "must so necessarily have a mystical and spiritual meaning that thence we conclude the other parts of his vision have a mystical and spiritual meaning also"; Ligonier "Life-Giving Water": the river "convinces us" the vision is symbolic; impossible route.
3. What are the unhealed marshes? Ellicott: man is still free to hear or forbear, the world will contain unhealed places. Henry: the mire of their own sinfulness, or the moisture of their own righteousness. Pulpit: limitation of the river's life-giving influence (rejects Driver/Smend "salt pans" as a blessing).

Job 16
1. Who is the witness in heaven, when God is the one attacking? Keil: faith brings together "two opposite principles". Barnes: returning confidence in God. Gregory Moralia 13: allegorically the Father witnessing to the Son; morally, he who seeks human praise seeks a witness on earth, the one who aims to please God has a witness in heaven. JFB citing Umbreit: "God must support me against God".
2. 16:21 "that he would argue the case of a man with God": Poole and Benson offer a reading of Christ the Son of man pleading; Barnes: a wish to contend directly with God. Host: Job asks for what the gospel supplies, without yet seeing it.

John 12:1-11
1. Was Mary's extravagance a model? Calvin: extraordinary act, not a rule; those who imitate with costly display are "apes, and not imitators". Sproul: "always time for an extravagant manifestation of our adoration of Christ". Ligonier devotional: disciples' concern for poor not wrong but priorities are.
2. Why make a thief the treasurer? Calvin: "the judgments of God are a deep gulf"; Chrysostom: to cut off all excuse, longsuffering to recall him; Augustine: to teach the church patience in tolerating the wicked.
3. Why kill Lazarus? Chrysostom: "Is the having received a benefit a crime?"

1 John 4:7-21
1. What does "God is love" mean? Sproul: not a tautology, not reversible ("love is God"); a Hebrew way of saying God is so loving that love is identified with his character; doesn't swallow justice. Calvin: "his nature is to love men"; "as God is the fountain of love, this effect flows from him".
2. Whose love casts out fear (4:18)? Augustine Homily 9: our charity; fear prepares the place like a bristle drawing thread. Calvin: God's love known by faith gives peace; rejects servile/filial fear reading; his editor notes most commentators (Beza, Doddridge, Scott) take it as love in us.
3. How can God love us first and need propitiation? Calvin on 4:10: love and propitiation both true; God interposed his Son "because he loved us".
