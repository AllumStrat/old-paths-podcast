## 1. SETTING AND BACKGROUND

### Lamentations 4-5 (deepest)
- Date: after the fall of Jerusalem in the fifth month (Av) of 586 B.C. Temple burned (Jer 52:12-13). Chapter 4 looks back on the siege (January 588 to July 586) and the capture of Zedekiah; chapter 5 looks at life afterward under occupation ("we must pay for the water we drink").
- Structure: chapter 4 is the fourth poem, single acrostic, 22 stanzas of two lines each (shorter than 1-2's three lines; Calvin's editor: "they have only two or four"). Begins with eikah "How" like chapters 1 and 2 (interlinear 4:1 'ê·ḵāh). Pe-before-ayin again: 4:16 opens with pene "the face of" (interlinear; ESV note "Hebrew The face of the Lord"), 4:17 opens with ayin ('odenah) and contains 'enenu "our eyes".
- Chapter 5: 22 verses but not acrostic (Henry; Ellicott suggests "half-finished"). Henry: "Some ancient versions call this chapter, 'The Prayer of Jeremiah.'" Communal "we" throughout.
- History: Egypt as the ally that failed (4:17; Jer 37:5-7, Pharaoh Hophra's army withdrew; Ellicott: "probably Egypt (Jeremiah 37:7)"). Zedekiah fled by night toward the Arabah and was taken in the plains of Jericho (Jer 39:4-5; 52:7-9; Henry). 4:13 the blood of the righteous: Uriah son of Shemaiah killed by Jehoiakim (Jer 26:20-23), Jeremiah nearly killed by priests and prophets (Jer 26:8, 11). 4:12 "the kings of the earth did not believe": Sennacherib's failure (Henry), the temple slogan of Jer 7:4.
- 4:15 "Unclean!" was the leper's cry (Lev 13:45); here shouted at the priests by others (Ellicott).
- 4:21 Edom in "the land of Uz": the cup of wrath (Jer 25:15-21; 49:7-22); Edom's gloating (Ps 137:7; Obadiah 10-12; Henry, Gill). Gill: Uz here a region in or near Idumea; Job's land was "near the land of Edom". Tomorrow: Job 1:1 "a man in the land of Uz".
- 4:22 "accomplished" / "he will keep you in exile no longer" (ESV note: "he will not exile you again"); Henry ties to Isaiah 40:2.
- 5:7 compare the proverb of Ezek 18:2 / Jer 31:29; 5:16 confession "woe to us, for we have sinned".
- 5:18 jackals/foxes on Zion: Micah 3:12 quoted in Jer 26:18 "Zion shall be plowed as a field".
- 5:19 compare Psalm 102:12, 25-27 (Calvin and Henry).
- 5:21 Hebrew: hashivenu YHWH elekha venashuvah, chaddesh yamenu kekedem (interlinear hă·šî·ḇê·nū... wə·nā·šū·ḇāh, ḥad·dêš ... kə·qe·ḏem). Root shuv "turn, return". Compare Jer 31:18 "bring me back that I may be restored" (Ephraim).
- 5:22 ki im ma'os me'astanu: "unless you have utterly rejected us" (ESV); KJV "But thou hast utterly rejected us". Synagogue practice: repeat 5:21 after 5:22 (also at end of Isaiah, Ecclesiastes, Malachi) (Calvin, Ellicott, Barnes, Keil and Delitzsch).
- Last lecture: Calvin's Lamentations lectures end with the announcement that he will begin Ezekiel the next day.
- Archaeology: (used in prior episodes: Mount Zion ash layer, ration tablets). Today: none needed beyond Egypt/Hophra; keep it textual.

### Song of Songs 8:8-14
- Final section. Speakers per ESV headings: "Others" (8:8-9), She (8:10-12), He (8:13), She (8:14).
- The brothers of 1:6 ("my mother's sons were angry with me; they made me keeper of the vineyards; but my own vineyard I have not kept") return; 8:12 "My vineyard, my very own, is before me" answers 1:6.
- 8:10 "as one who finds peace" (shalom; ESV note "Or brings out"), wordplay with Shelomoh (Solomon) and Shulammite (6:13). Cambridge: siege imagery ("he dealt with her as a king deals with a city which he cannot capture, he made peace").
- 8:11 Baal-hamon = "lord of a multitude" (Ellicott); a thousand pieces of silver per keeper (Isaiah 7:23 a thousand vines worth a thousand shekels, Henry).
- 8:14 "Make haste" is barach, "flee" (Cambridge: "This should be flee"; Gill: "may be rendered, 'flee away'"), repeating 2:17 "be like a gazelle... on cleft mountains" (Ellicott).
- No church father commentary located: Gregory of Nyssa's homilies end at 6:9, Bernard's sermons end at 3:1 (noted in episode 11 threads); no public-domain father on 8:8-14 found.

### John 7:25-36
- Feast of Booths, autumn A.D. 29 (likely), mid-feast teaching in the temple (7:14). "Some of the people of Jerusalem" (locals who know the leaders' plot, Chrysostom).
- Hidden-Messiah belief: Trypho in Justin's Dialogue 8 (Messiah unknown "until Elias come to anoint Him"); Cambridge Greek Testament: "This text is the strongest, if not the only evidence" of belief that the Messiah's parents would be unknown. Micah 5:2 Bethlehem known (Matt 2:5; John 7:42).
- 7:30 "his hour had not yet come" (2:4; 7:6, 8; 8:20; fulfilled 12:23; 13:1; 17:1).
- 7:32 officers (temple police) sent; they return in 7:45-46 ("No one ever spoke like this man").
- 7:35 diaspora ton Hellenon: "Dispersion among the Greeks". Ellicott: diaspora occurs elsewhere in the NT only in James 1:1 and 1 Peter 1:1; "Greeks" means Gentiles, not Hellenists.

### 1 Peter 5:8-14
- Written from "Babylon" (5:13) probably Rome in the early 60s, before Nero's persecution of 64 or at its start; through Silvanus (Silas of Acts 15-17; 2 Cor 1:19; 1-2 Thess 1:1) as carrier or secretary.
- 5:8 Greek: nepsate (be sober), gregoresate (be watchful), antidikos (opponent in a lawsuit; JFB), diabolos (slanderer), hos leon oruomenos (roaring lion), peripatei (walks about; Ellicott: compare Job 1:7; 2:2), katapiein (swallow down, devour) (Bible Hub interlinear).
- Peter's own history: told to watch and pray in Gethsemane and slept (Mark 14:37-38); Luke 22:31-32 Satan "demanded to have you, that he might sift you like wheat... when you have turned again, strengthen (sterizo) your brothers". 5:10 uses sterixei: "confirm".
- 5:10 katartisei: Vincent's Word Studies notes the verb is used of mending nets (Matt 4:21) and restoring an erring brother (Gal 6:1). Then sterixei, sthenosei, themeliosei (establish on a foundation).
- 5:13 "she who is at Babylon": Rome by figure (Eusebius HE 2.15, citing Clement's Hypotyposes and Papias); literal Babylon (Calvin); Mark "my son" (Acts 12:12; Papias on Mark as Peter's interpreter).

## 3. INTERPRETIVE QUESTIONS

Lamentations:
1. Who is "the breath of our nostrils, the Lord's anointed" (4:20)? Josiah (Targum, Rashi via Gill; Jerome's elegy view; rejected by Calvin and Gill: Josiah was slain, not taken, and by Egyptians), Zedekiah (Calvin, Henry, Gill, Carson), Christ directly (Justin, Irenaeus; Theodoret per Gill). Host: Zedekiah historically; the words outrun him and are properly fulfilled in Christ (Calvin's "properly belong to Christ only").
2. Is 5:7 a complaint like Ezekiel 18:2? Calvin and Henry: no, it is confession plus plea; 5:16 confirms.
3. 5:21: whose turning? Calvin: God's turning to favor, "he does not speak here of repentance"; Henry and Piper: converting grace. Host: the verse covers both: "to yourself" means relationship, and only God can effect the turning.
4. 5:22: despair or "unless"? Calvin's "except it may be" (reasoning from the impossible), Ellicott, Cambridge, Pulpit; versus KJV despair and the synagogue's discomfort. Keil and Delitzsch: hope "merely glimmers from afar, like the morning star" (Gerlach). Host: the book ends honest and open, and the open ending is itself a prayer.
Song: 1. Who speaks 8:8-9 and who is the little sister? Brothers (literal), Jewish church about Gentiles (Henry, Poole). 2. 8:10 "peace": with whom? 3. Why end on "flee/make haste"?
John: 1. How can they both know and not know where he comes from? (Augustine, Chrysostom, Calvin). 2. What does "his hour" mean: fate or will? (Augustine: will; Calvin and Ryle: God's decree). 3. "Where I am you cannot come": present tense "I am" (Augustine); judgment (Ryle).
1 Peter: 1. Babylon = Rome, Mesopotamian Babylon, or Egyptian Babylon? 2. Is 5:10 a prayer or promise? (Calvin: textual variant, future vs optative; ESV future). 3. Why does Peter mention worldwide brotherhood?
