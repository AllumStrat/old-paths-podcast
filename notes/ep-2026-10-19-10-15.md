## 1. SETTING AND BACKGROUND

### Jeremiah 39-43 (deepest)
- Dates: Siege begins ninth year of Zedekiah, tenth month (Tebeth), i.e., January 588 B.C. (39:1; 52:4; 2 Kings 25:1). Breach eleventh year, fourth month, ninth day, i.e., July 586 B.C. (39:2). Temple burned fifth month (Calvin notes "The Temple was burnt in the fifth month"; 52:12). Gedaliah murdered in the seventh month (41:1), per Jewish chronicle cited by Gill the third of Tishri, 52 days after the temple's destruction; the fast of the seventh month (Zechariah 7:5; 8:19). (Gill on 41:1, https://biblehub.com/commentaries/gill/jeremiah/41.htm). Some date Gedaliah's murder later (582); keep to "seventh month" and possibly the same year.
- Book structure: Chapter 39 is the hinge: forty years of warnings fulfilled. Chapters 40-44 narrate the aftermath in Judah and Egypt, ending in Jeremiah's last sermons in Egypt (44). Then 45 Baruch, 46-51 the nations, 52 historical appendix. Henry and Poole: the heading "The word that came to Jeremiah" in 40:1 is not resumed until 42:7; chapters 40-41 are narrative introducing that prophecy (Henry on 40:1-6; Poole on 40:1).
- Zedekiah's flight: by the king's garden, gate between the two walls, toward the Arabah (Jordan valley), overtaken in plains of Jericho; Riblah in Hamath (Nebuchadnezzar's field headquarters on the Orontes in Syria). Henry: Zedekiah "but thirty-two years of age". Compare 32:4-5, 34:3 (he would see the king of Babylon eye to eye) and Ezekiel 12:13 (he shall not see Babylon, though he die there) - the two prophecies reconciled by the blinding. (Ezekiel 12:13 cross reference: ESV text, known; can be used without quotation as a reference.)
- Archaeology:
  - Nebo-Sarsekim tablet (BM 114789): Babylonian cuneiform tablet from Sippar, dated 595 B.C., records gold given by Nabu-sharrussu-ukin, the "chief eunuch" (rab sha-reshi), matching "Nebu-sar-sekim the Rab-saris" of 39:3. Identified by Michael Jursa in 2007. Sources: Biola Magazine 2007, https://biola.edu/blogs/biola-magazine/2007/rare-find-supports-book-of-jeremiah-solves-puzzle- ("The tablet, dated 595 B.C., contains an inscription recording a gift of gold given by a Nebo-Sarsekim — a 'chief official' — to a Babylonian temple." and "it stayed undeciphered until a visiting Viennese researcher recognized the name."). Note: ESV now reads Nebu-sar-sekim correctly.
  - Lachish Letter 4 (Tabletalk, Joshua Van Ee, https://tabletalkmagazine.com/article/2023/09/lachish-letters/): "We are watching the [fire] signals of Lachish in accordance with all the signs which my lord gave, for we cannot see Azekah." Debated whether Azekah had fallen (Jer 34:7). Letters found in destruction layer at Lachish, 588-586 BC.
  - Gedaliah bulla from Lachish, found 1935 in ash layer: "Belonging to Gedaliah, who is over the House" (https://cojs.org/Seal_of_Gedaliah-_586_BCE/). Posen Library: "This Gedalyahu is thought by many to be Gedaliah ben Ahikam... It is assumed that he previously held the position of steward in charge of the royal palace" (https://www.posenlibrary.com/node/682). Present as probable, not proven.
  - Tahpanhes = Tell Defenneh on the eastern frontier of the Delta, Greek Daphnai. Petrie 1886 excavated; a large brick platform before the fort called "Kasr Bint el-Yehudi" ("castle of the Jew's daughter"); Petrie judged it "highly probable" the pavement of 43:9. Nebuchadnezzar's annals fragment: invasion of Egypt in his 37th year, 568-567 B.C. (ISBE on Bible Hub atlas, https://biblehub.com/atlas/tahpanhes.htm). ESV "pavement"; KJV "brickkiln" (Poole discusses).
- Family of Shaphan: Gedaliah son of Ahikam son of Shaphan (39:14; 40:5). Ahikam protected Jeremiah in 26:24 (Henry on 40:7). Shaphan read the law to Josiah (yesterday).
- Ishmael son of Nethaniah son of Elishama "of the royal family" (41:1); sent by Baalis king of the Ammonites (40:14). Henry: "Ishmael was of the seed royal, and would therefore be easily tempted to envy and hate one that set up for a governor in Judah, who was not, as he was, of David's line".
- Asa's cistern at Mizpah (41:9; 1 Kings 15:22). Gibeon's great pool (41:12; 2 Samuel 2:13, per Henry). Geruth Chimham near Bethlehem: Chimham son of Barzillai, 2 Samuel 19:37-40 (Henry; Poole).
- Ligonier devotional (https://learn.ligonier.org/devotionals/warning-those-left-behind): "Despite having seen the Word of the Lord come true with their own eyes in Jerusalem's fall, the people still did not trust Him."

### Ecclesiastes 12
- Close of the book. 11:7-12:8 one unit: youth, age, death. 12:8 repeats 1:2, framing the book (hevel; ESV note: "vapor" or "mere breath"). 12:9-14 epilogue about the Preacher, his method (weighing, studying, arranging proverbs), goads and nails, one Shepherd, warning about many books, conclusion.
- Authorship: traditional Solomonic (Henry "It is probable that Solomon wrote this when he was himself old").
- 12:1 "Creator" is plural in Hebrew (boreka), Henry: "Remember thy Creators; the word is plural"; Gill same, Trinity reading.
- 12:5 almond: Gill "which most interpret of the hoary head... whence it has its name of haste in the Hebrew language; see Jeremiah 1:11". Hebrew shaqed (almond) from shaqad (to watch, be wakeful). Jeremiah 1:11-12 wordplay: shaqed / shoqed "I am watching over my word to perform it."
- 12:6 silver cord, golden bowl, pitcher at fountain, wheel at cistern (lamp and well images).
- 12:7 echoes Genesis 2:7 and 3:19.
- 12:13 Hebrew literally "this is the whole of man" (ESV note: "Or the duty of all mankind"). Augustine reads "this is every man".

### John 5:1-15
- Third sign in John (after Cana water and official's son). Unnamed feast (Chrysostom "Methinks that of Pentecost"; Calvin "the probable conjecture is that he means Pentecost"). Sheep Gate (Nehemiah 3:1). Bethesda: twin reservoirs north of Temple Mount near St Anne's; "two large water reservoirs separated by a broad rock dike... with four colonnaded porticos around the sides and one across the central dike" and "Some claimed that John had invented the detail of the five porticoes to represent the five books of Moses" (https://www.seetheholyland.net/?p=327).
- Verse 3b-4 absent from earliest manuscripts (ESV note; Piper).
- Sabbath burden law: Calvin cites Jeremiah 17:21 ("the Law expressly forbids to carry any burden whatever on the Sabbath-day, (Jeremiah 17:21.)").
- Ioudaioi note in ESV.
- Leads into the discourse 5:16-30 (tomorrow).

### 1 Peter 1:17-25
- Written by Peter from "Babylon" (Rome) about A.D. 62-64 to elect exiles in Asia Minor. 1:13-2:10 the call to holiness. 1:17 "Father who judges impartially" - fear during the time of exile (paroikia). 1:18-19 ransomed (lutroo) not with silver/gold (Isaiah 52:3 "you shall be redeemed without money"), Passover lamb. 1:20 foreknown/manifested. 1:21 faith and hope in God. 1:22 obedience to the truth -> brotherly love. 1:23-25 born again of imperishable seed through the living and abiding word; quotes Isaiah 40:6-8 (the comfort-my-people chapter addressed to exiles).
- Polycarp (c. 110) quotes 1:21 in his Letter to the Philippians, chapter 2.

## 3. INTERPRETIVE QUESTIONS

### Jeremiah
1. Were the remnant sincere in 42:1-6? Calvin: "they either came dissemblingly... or they were extremely stupid, and hypocrisy had wholly deprived them of their understanding." Henry: "They compliment him thus in hopes to persuade him to say as they would have him say." Poole: "These men (though wretched hypocrites)." Gill on 42:20 "deceived your souls". 42:20 ESV "you have gone astray at the cost of your lives".
2. Why ten days (42:7)? Henry: "perhaps, to punish them for their hypocrisy or to show that Jeremiah did not speak of himself, nor what he would, for he could not speak when he would". Calvin: "He might have done so immediately, but he deferred, that the prophecy might have more weight." Gill: "deferred, that it might have the greater weight with it when it came; and that it might appear that it was not of the prophet himself". Gill notes Abarbinel's view that it was the Day of Atonement and rejects it.
3. Was Gedaliah wrong to disbelieve Johanan (40:16)? Henry: "Herein he discovered more good humour than discretion, more of the innocency of the dove than the wisdom of the serpent." Calvin: "there is a medium between simplicity on the one hand, and cruelty on the other." Calvin also says Gedaliah, as a magistrate, "ought to have carefully inquired". Henry: Johanan's offer to kill Ishmael showed "more courage and zeal than sense of justice; for, if it be lawful to kill for prevention, who then can be safe". Poole: Gedaliah "a man of a good humour, not too credulous".
4. Nebuchadnezzar "my servant" (43:10): Calvin "he is called God's servant, because he executed what God himself had decreed"; Poole "because he was to obey him in what he should do, though he intended not so". Henry: "The world's princes are God's servants".
5. Why did 80 men bring offerings to a burned temple (41:5)? Calvin rejects that they had not heard; they came "that they might testify their piety in that place". Poole: possibly hadn't heard. On cut bodies, Calvin thinks no violation intended; Poole calls it "a barbarous fashion used by the heathens, and forbidden the Jews".

### Ecclesiastes
1. Is 12:2-6 an allegory of the body (Targum, Henry, Gill: keepers=hands, strong men=legs, grinders=teeth) or a picture of a household/town shutting down under storm and death? Answer: the images clearly do evoke aging; but read as a poem of a great house going dark, not a code to crack every detail. Henry admits "some difficulty in them to us now".
2. Is the epilogue (12:9-14) a later editor correcting a skeptic? Confessional view: the epilogue states the book's own conclusion (Henry: "the great enquiry which Solomon prosecutes in this book").
3. 12:7 does the spirit return to God imply all are saved? Gill: returns "to God the Judge of all, who passes sentence".

### John
1. Why ask "Do you want to be healed?" Chrysostom: to show the man's perseverance. Calvin: to kindle desire and alert witnesses. Spurgeon: to rouse a paralyzed will.
2. Is the man a hero or a weakling in 5:15? Chrysostom: bold confession ("with loud voice confessed and proclaimed the benefit" is about 5:11; on 5:15 he doesn't condemn). Calvin: "he had no solid doctrine or clear light on which he could rely." Piper: healed "who had not even believed on him".
3. Is sickness linked to sin (5:14)? Calvin and Ryle: sin is the root of sickness generally; John 9:3 warns against a simple one-to-one rule. Piper: the "worse thing" is final judgment (5:28-29).

### 1 Peter
1. To whom was the ransom paid? Gregory Nazianzen Oration 45.22: not to the evil one; not demanded by the Father; the Father accepts it.
2. "Futile ways inherited from your forefathers": Jewish or Gentile readers? Calvin applies to Jews (dispersion), and to all.
3. "Living and abiding word of God": living word, or word of the living God? Calvin prefers "word of the living God", the editor prefers "living word".
